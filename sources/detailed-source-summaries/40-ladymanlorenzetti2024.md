# ladymanlorenzetti2024

## Full Citation
James Ladyman and Luca Lorenzetti, "Effective ontic structural realism," *The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science* (2024). https://doi.org/10.1086/729061.

## Detailed Summary
Ladyman and Lorenzetti argue that several recent discussions of effective realism should be unified within an ontic structural realist framework. Their basic complaint is that effective realism is attractive because it allows realism about non-fundamental, domain-limited theories, but recent versions often leave it unclear in what sense they are genuinely realist rather than merely pragmatically permissive. The paper's solution is to supply a common metaphysical package drawn from ontic structural realism: realism about modal structure, scale relativity of ontology, and the theory of real patterns.

The paper surveys three areas where effective realism has recently been invoked. The first concerns non-relativistic quantum mechanics, where Fraser and Vickers propose that one should be realist only about the commitments shared across major interpretations. The second concerns quantum field theory, where effective realism is used to explain how one might be realist about theories whose own formalism already treats them as scale-limited and effective. The third concerns superseded theories, where Robertson and Wilson argue that some theoretical relics can be retained as effective ontology when the old theory remains explanatory within a restricted domain.

In the quantum-mechanics case, Ladyman and Lorenzetti argue that ontic structural realism provides the missing metaphysical underpinning. If the shared commitments across interpretations concern objective possibilities and branching structures, then a realist should treat these as commitments to objective modal structure in the world. This, they argue, is a more genuinely realist basis than functionalism alone. It also avoids the problems they see in Egg's version of effective ontology, which they regard as too permissive and vulnerable to Saatsi's inconsistency objection.

The authors then bring in the second and third parts of the OSR package. Real patterns explain how one gets effective entities out of modal structure, and scale relativity explains why domain-limited ontologies do not automatically conflict. Their central example is the familiar tension between Newtonian gravity and general relativity. On the OSR picture, there is no inconsistency in being realist about both, because they describe different scale-relative structures. The point is not that incompatible ontologies are all true at once in the same respect, but that existence claims must be indexed to the relevant domain or scale.

The paper also responds to Egg's earlier critique of structural realist quantum mechanics. Egg had argued that a structural realist treatment tied to measurement threatens to become anthropocentric and therefore insufficiently realist. Ladyman and Lorenzetti reply that OSR need not rely on an observer-centered notion of measurement. A decoherence-based account of effective collapse can ground an objective effective ontology without anthropocentrism, while remaining neutral about deeper interpretive disputes.

In discussing quantum field theory, the authors argue that effective realism needs more than selective commitment to robust quantities under renormalization-group flow. Without a realist metaphysics, that position threatens to collapse into structural empiricism or mere pragmatic acceptance. OSR, they argue, supplies the needed realist content: the robust, RG-invariant structures identified by the theory can be understood as objective modal structures, and the ontology associated with them is scale-relative from the outset.

The final substantive application concerns superseded theories and theoretical relics. Ladyman and Lorenzetti argue that OSR naturally accommodates Robertson and Wilson's proposal that some old theoretical entities remain in ontology when the old theory is domain-restricted and still explanatory. They treat this as continuous with special-science ontology. The same real-patterns framework that vindicates entities such as viruses can, in principle, vindicate some higher-level or historically superseded ontological posits. They also claim that OSR improves Robertson and Wilson's formulation by replacing talk of levels of abstraction with the more precise notion of scale relativity.

The conclusion is that effective realism is best understood not as a stand-alone doctrine, but as a structural realist view about scale-relative modal structures and the real patterns they sustain. On this picture, OSR unifies effective realism across quantum mechanics, quantum field theory, and the ontology of superseded theories.

## Core Argument Map
- **Starting diagnosis:** Recent effective-realist proposals are promising but often insufficiently explicit about what makes them realist.
- **Unifying proposal:** Ontic structural realism supplies the needed metaphysics through realism about modal structure, real patterns, and scale relativity of ontology.
- **Quantum mechanics application:** Shared commitments across interpretations can be understood as commitments to objective modal structure.
- **Anti-permissiveness move:** Scale relativity and real patterns avoid the inconsistency problems raised against looser forms of effective ontology.
- **Quantum field theory application:** ER about QFT becomes genuinely realist when RG-robust structure is taken to represent modal structure in the world.
- **Old theories application:** Theoretical relics can be retained as scale-relative real patterns when restricted theories remain explanatory in their proper domains.
- **Conclusion:** Effective realism is most coherent and defensible when embedded within OSR rather than treated as an isolated stance.

## Relation to Real Patterns Need Closure
This paper is highly relevant to RPNC because it is an explicit recent attempt to frame real patterns and effective ontology within a broader metaphysical package. It is especially useful where it insists that effective realism needs stronger realist credentials than mere pragmatic usefulness and where it links real patterns to modal structure and scale relativity.

For RPNC, the paper is helpful in two main ways. First, it shows that current defenders of effective realism themselves feel pressure to explain what prevents effective ontology from collapsing into permissiveness or quasi-empiricism. Second, it treats real patterns as the mechanism by which effective entities "earn their keep" across domains, including special sciences and superseded theories. That gives RPNC a clear contemporary interlocutor.

The limits are equally important. Ladyman and Lorenzetti defend a structural-realist and scale-relative form of effective realism, but they do not provide a closure criterion for when a pattern is admissible. Their answer to inconsistency worries is primarily domain- or scale-indexing. That is important, but it does not by itself settle the more fine-grained anti-gerrymandering question that motivates RPNC. So the paper is best used as a strong background and partially overlapping view, not as if it already states the RPNC thesis.

## Reviewer-Relevant Cautions
- The paper's main task is **unificatory and metaphysical**. It is not principally a new formal theory of real-pattern individuation.
- Its notion of **effective ontology** is tied to **scale relativity**. Claims from the paper should usually preserve that indexing.
- The response to **Saatsi-style inconsistency worries** relies on domain restriction and scale indexing, not on a closure test.
- The treatment of **quantum mechanics** remains intentionally neutral on deeper interpretive disputes. The claim is about overlap and shared modal commitments.
- The paper extends OSR to **old theories** and special sciences, but it does not thereby show that every explanatorily useful posit qualifies as a real pattern.
