# andersen2025

## Full Citation
Holly K. Andersen, "The density of structure," *Synthese* 206, no. 5 (2025): article 237. https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-025-05305-y.

## Detailed Summary
Andersen argues that much realist metaphysics about structure is guided by an often unacknowledged assumption she calls Sparsity: the idea that genuine structure must be comparatively rare, bounded, or limited enough that metaphysics can in principle sort the real from the merely apparent by imposing restrictive criteria from the outset. Her central claim is that this assumption should be rejected. A pragmatist pattern metaphysics, drawing on Dennettian real patterns, should separate three questions that are often run together: what structure is, how much structure there is, and which specific structures matter for inquiry. Once those are disentangled, it becomes possible that the world is dense in genuine structure, perhaps even inexhaustibly so.

The opening sections diagnose how Sparsity operates across very different positions. Some views tie realism to a uniquely correct carving of the world. Others allow pluralism but still build in an upper bound so there is not "too much" structure. Andersen argues that these views conflate metaphysical characterization with empirical and methodological questions. The result is that a substantive empirical expectation about how much structure the world contains gets smuggled in as if it were a metaphysical truth.

To loosen that restriction, she adopts a broad realist starting point and uses pattern metaphysics as a minimal account of what structure is. Patterns, on this approach, are specified by identification conditions, including a noise tolerance. Slight changes in those identification conditions yield slightly different patterns. If one pattern has instances in the world, nearby variants may well do so too. This means that once one abandons Sparsity, there may be many overlapping, closely related, all-genuine structures rather than a single uniquely privileged one.

The paper's central contrast is with two poles in the literature. On one side are positions that assume uniqueness or at least a strong upper bound, including post-Lewisian "joint-carving" pictures and, in Andersen's discussion, rainforest realism as developed by Ladyman, Ross, and Collier. On the other side is McAllister's far more expansive view, according to which all possible patterns exhibited in data count as evidence of structures in the world. Andersen rejects both. The first builds Sparsity into metaphysics too early. The second builds plenitude into metaphysics too strongly. Her own proposal is more modest metaphysically: pattern metaphysics should say what would count as a pattern worth checking for, while leaving it an empirical matter whether there are instances and how much genuine structure there turns out to be.

This sets up the paper's most distinctive thesis, namely that structure may be dense in a mathematically strong sense. When patterns involve continuously variable parameters, there can be indefinitely many nearby variants. Since finite investigators cannot check all of them, the task of metaphysics cannot be to identify in advance the complete set of genuine structures. Nor can realism require the possibility of a final science that exhausts all structure. The appropriate lesson is not anti-realism. It is that metaphysics should be minimal, while the serious winnowing of candidate structures should be done by methodological and empirical considerations.

The later sections develop this as a pragmatist realism. Our interests, goals, and limits inevitably shape which patterns we investigate and refine. That does not make structure mind-dependent. The direction of fit still runs from world to representation: our choices succeed only if they latch onto patterns that actually occur and are useful for explanation, modeling, prediction, or other inquiry-guiding purposes. The important point is that interests help narrow inquiry because there may be too much genuine structure to pursue exhaustively, not because interests create reality.

Andersen therefore offers a deliberately thin realism. Calling some structure genuine says relatively little by itself. The heavier work of selecting among overlapping real structures belongs to methodology, coherence, empirical fit, and modeling aims, not to a priori metaphysical restriction. The paper is thus best read as a defense of realist pluralism without uniqueness and without an antecedent sparsity constraint.

## Core Argument Map
- **Target:** Many realist metaphysics presuppose **Sparsity**, the view that genuine structure must be limited enough to sort cleanly in advance.
- **Diagnostic claim:** Those views conflate three questions: what structure is, how much structure there is, and which structures are worth using.
- **Pattern-metaphysics move:** Use Dennettian patterns to give a minimal account of structure in terms of identification conditions and noise tolerance.
- **Anti-uniqueness point:** Slightly different but overlapping pattern specifications can each pick out genuine structure.
- **Middle path:** Reject both restrictive sparse-joints metaphysics and McAllister-style unrestricted plenitude.
- **Density thesis:** The world may contain more genuine structure than finite inquiry could ever exhaust.
- **Methodological consequence:** Metaphysics should not pre-decide which exact structures are real; empirical and methodological constraints should do the winnowing.
- **Pragmatist consequence:** Interests and goals guide inquiry because of abundance of structure, not because they create that structure.
- **Realist conclusion:** One can reject uniqueness and Sparsity while remaining fully realist about structure.

## Relation to Real Patterns Need Closure
This paper is highly relevant to RPNC because it presents a live challenge to any attempt to make real-pattern realism more selective. Andersen argues that metaphysics should not do too much restrictive work in advance, and that the world may be far denser in genuine structure than sparse realist views allow. That makes her an important interlocutor for any closure-based proposal.

For RPNC, the right lesson is not simply to oppose Andersen. Her paper is useful because it clarifies what any stricter criterion would need to show. A closure condition cannot just reassert that there are fewer real patterns than she allows. It would need to explain why some structures have a stronger claim to ontological seriousness than nearby variants, and why that restriction is not merely a disguised reintroduction of Sparsity.

RPNC can therefore use Andersen as both ally and foil. She supports a realist, pattern-based, anti-uniqueness framework. But she also presses exactly the objection RPNC must answer, namely: what justifies moving from thin pattern realism to a more selective ontology without begging the question? Any use should keep that dialectical balance clear.

## Reviewer-Relevant Cautions
- The text source used here is a **preprint**, while the cited version is the **2025 Synthese publication**. Final wording and pagination may differ.
- Andersen's target is the **Sparsity presupposition**, not realism as such. The paper is strongly realist in a thin, pragmatist way.
- Her use of density does **not** imply that every describable pattern has instances in the world; that is part of her disagreement with McAllister.
- The paper intentionally shifts load-bearing work from **metaphysics** to **methodology and empirical inquiry**.
- Any use in RPNC should avoid caricaturing Andersen as a simple "anything goes" pluralist. She allows abundant structure while still requiring worldly constraint.
