# ladyman2026

## Full Citation
James Ladyman, "Patterns all the way up: Prolegomena to a future naturalised metaphysics," in *Dennett's Real Patterns in Science and Nature*, ed. Tyler Millhouse, Stephen Petersen, and Don Ross (Cambridge, MA: MIT Press, 2026).

## Detailed Summary
Ladyman's chapter is a programmatic defense of using Dennett's idea of real patterns as the basis for a future naturalized metaphysics. Its central claim is not merely that higher-level entities can sometimes be real, but that the ontology of science more generally is best understood in terms of projectible, explanatory, and computationally useful patterns distributed across domains and scales. He argues that the Game of Life picture, which has been philosophically productive, should be treated as a heuristic rather than as a literal model for scientific ontology, because actual science does not present a single privileged bitmap-like base level from which all other ontological commitments neatly emerge.

The paper begins by revisiting Dennett's original proposal. A real pattern is something that figures indispensably in projectible generalizations that explain and predict the world, and its reality is tied to compression and reduction of computational cost. Ladyman treats this as an alternative to Quinean ontological commitment. Scientific and ordinary ontologies alike contain objects, properties, processes, and relations that enable such projectible generalizations. The point of the real-patterns framework, on his telling, is not that ontology is arbitrary or merely perspective-bound, but that the world contains stable modal organization that different sciences track at different levels.

He then stresses both the usefulness and the limits of the familiar Game of Life analogy. It helps show how structurally persistent entities can be real without being tied to one fixed set of micro-constituents. But it also misleads if taken too literally, because the Game of Life has a hidden implementation-level mechanism, a finite and cleanly specified state space, and a privileged lower-level description. Scientific practice, by contrast, involves multiple scales, multiple domains, scale-relative descriptions, and no single hierarchy that cleanly orders all the sciences. The result is that naturalized metaphysics must take seriously the actual architecture of scientific representation rather than importing too much from a toy computational example.

Much of the chapter is then devoted to a set of theses about science. Ladyman emphasizes that scientific progress has expanded ontology across domains and scales while also integrating those domains; that ontological commitment in science depends on triangulation across different evidential and representational contexts; that the ontology of science is accompanied by a plurality of kinds of causation, law, mechanism, symmetry, probability, and equilibrium; and that scientific modal structure is always local, effective, and limited in domain. Laws and regularities are never simply globally exceptionless descriptions of the world at all scales. Rather, they hold under conditions, across specific scales, and against a background of environmental stability.

This leads to a strong emphasis on scale-relativity and effective ontology. Composition in science is not mere bare mereology. Scientific part-whole explanations are typically diachronic and dynamical, involving interactions among parts, separation of timescales, and context-sensitive stability conditions. Ladyman therefore rejects the idea that there is one simple hierarchy from microphysics upward through chemistry, biology, and psychology. Scientific models instead cut across scales in different ways, and ontological commitment commonly requires support from multiple domains and multiple scales at once.

At the same time, Ladyman does not abandon materialist integration. He argues that scientific practice strongly supports physicalist research programs over rival programs that posit sui generis non-physical ontologies to explain chemistry, life, or mind. The modal structures of the special sciences stand in systematic relations to the modal structures of physics, either directly or indirectly. But this does not collapse higher-level ontology into eliminativism, because special sciences often track universal forms of behavior that are independent of detailed microphysical states. The task of those sciences is precisely to identify the descriptive categories that track such universality.

The final payoff is an account of special-science ontology as abstraction, aggregation, approximation, and coarse-graining. Scientific real patterns are typically lossy. They compress information and track collective behavior with fewer degrees of freedom than a full lower-level specification would require. This is enough for scientific realism, on Ladyman's view, provided the resulting ontology supports projectible modal structure. The chapter is therefore best read as a broad metaphysical orientation: a defense of effective, scale-sensitive, pattern-based realism grounded in actual scientific practice.

## Core Argument Map
- **Starting point:** Dennett's real patterns can serve as an alternative basis for ontological commitment in science.
- **Positive core:** What science commits us to are patterns that support projectible explanation and prediction while reducing computational burden.
- **Heuristic warning:** The Game of Life illustrates emergence, but it should not be mistaken for a literal model of scientific ontology because science lacks a single privileged bitmap level.
- **Historical thesis:** Scientific progress expands ontology across domains and scales while also integrating those domains.
- **Evidential thesis:** Ontological commitment normally requires triangulation across multiple forms of evidence and representation.
- **Modal pluralism:** Science deploys many kinds of laws, causes, mechanisms, symmetries, and explanatory structures rather than one unified modal template.
- **Scale thesis:** Scientific ontology is effective and scale-relative; laws and models hold only over limited domains and conditions.
- **Composition thesis:** Scientific part-whole relations are dynamic and interaction-laden, not exhausted by formal mereology.
- **Materialist integration:** Higher-level ontology is real, but its legitimacy is connected systematically to successful lower-level physical research programs.
- **Conclusion:** Naturalized metaphysics should treat the ontology of science as patterns all the way up, not as one fundamental base plus derivative appearances.

## Relation to Real Patterns Need Closure
This chapter is directly relevant background for RPNC because it advances the kind of scale-sensitive, anti-mereological, pattern-based realism that RPNC also wants to preserve. It is especially useful for resisting the thought that there must be one uniquely privileged ontological level, or that higher-level ontology is suspect unless it reduces to micro-descriptions without remainder.

Its relevance is still mainly programmatic. Ladyman gives a wide-ranging naturalized-metaphysical orientation, not a restrictive criterion that distinguishes admissible from merely convenient pattern attributions. For RPNC, that makes him both ally and pressure point: he helps justify why real-pattern realism should be generalized across science, while leaving open the further question of what disciplined condition rules out gerrymandered, unstable, or merely transient coarse-grainings.

RPNC should therefore use Ladyman as support for effective ontology, scale-relativity, and the legitimacy of lossy higher-level description, but not imply that this chapter already delivers a closure condition. It does not. Its contribution is to widen and naturalize the real-patterns framework.

## Reviewer-Relevant Cautions
- The source text used here is a **2024 preprint** of a chapter cited in its **2026 MIT Press** publication form. Pagination and some wording may differ from the final published version.
- The chapter is **programmatic and synthetic**. It proposes a metaphysical orientation rather than a formal criterion for real patternhood.
- Ladyman's emphasis on **effective ontology** and **scale-relativity** should not be read as anti-realist or as reducing ontology to mere convenience.
- The paper defends **materialist integration** without endorsing simple bottom-up reductionism or a single cross-science hierarchy.
