## TARGET 1 — §1 thesis paragraph, closing move

**Original final sentence:**

> If a macro-candidate is to be more than a physically allowed summary, it must be
> non-redundant in a dynamical sense, not merely useful or compressive.

### Twelve variants

1. A macro-candidate must be non-redundant in a dynamical sense, not merely useful or compressive, if it is to be more than a physically allowed summary.
2. Being useful or compressive is not enough; the candidate must be dynamically non-redundant.
3. What separates a real macro-candidate from a physically allowed summary is dynamical non-redundancy, not usefulness.
4. Usefulness and compressibility do not settle the question. Dynamical non-redundancy does.
5. The demand is dynamical non-redundancy, not mere utility.
6. A candidate earns more than summary status only by being dynamically non-redundant.
7. Compression and utility leave the question open. Only dynamical non-redundancy closes it.
8. To count as more than a summary, a candidate must do dynamical work its parts do not.
9. The question is whether the candidate is dynamically non-redundant, not whether it is useful.
10. Summaries are useful too. The candidate must be dynamically non-redundant.
11. Dynamical non-redundancy is what is being asked for here, and utility does not supply it.
12. A candidate that is merely useful remains a summary; dynamical non-redundancy is the further thing.

### What the variants share (the attractor)

Eleven of twelve end on a **corrective contrast**: assert the real thing by denying a
nearby impostor. The impostor is always the same pair (useful / compressive). Nine put
the denial in the final clause, so the paragraph's last beat is a negation.

Named:

> **Attractor A — the corrective closer.** End the paragraph by naming what the claim is
> *not*. The rhythm is "X, not Y," and it lands on Y.

Two further patterns visible across the set:

> **Attractor B — the summarizing closer.** The last sentence restates the paragraph's
> thesis in compressed form. It adds no content; it exists to feel like an ending.
> (Variants 3, 5, 6, 9 are pure restatement.)

> **Attractor C — the two-beat aphorism.** Short declarative, then shorter declarative.
> "X does not settle it. Y does." (Variants 4, 7, 10.) This is the motto shape the
> smell list warns about.

### Why this is worth fixing here

The paragraph already says, twice, that closure is the modal reading and that
observational success is insufficient. The closer says it a third time in slogan form.
The reader does not need it. Cutting it ends the paragraph on the actual criterion
instead of on a denial.

### Conservative direction

Not one of the twelve. Cut the closer and let the paragraph end on the working
sentence, which is the one that says what closure does. Trust the reader to carry the
contrast that the preceding four sentences already established.

## TARGET 2 — paragraph closers across the manuscript

Measured: 39 of 206 body-paragraph closers are 14 words or fewer. A sample:

- "This staged filtering is exactly where closure earns its metaphysical role."
- "Closure supplies that prior sorting question."
- "The criterion is restrictive about robust commitment, not about every possible sense of reality."
- "The restriction concerns which of Pearl's outputs carry ontological weight, not the framework itself."
- "The realism-relevant discriminator is causal autonomy under admissible perturbation, not microphysical smallness by itself."
- "It is not."

The same three attractors recur: corrective contrast (A), compressed restatement (B),
two-beat aphorism (C). "Exactly where closure earns its metaphysical role" is also
motto-making in the smell list's sense.

### Conservative direction

Do not hunt all 39. Most short closers are fine: a short sentence after a long one is
just rhythm. Fix the ones where the closer *adds nothing the paragraph has not already
established* and exists to feel conclusive. Three or four edits, not thirty.

## TARGET 3 — paragraph openers

Openers are less patterned than closers (top repeated shape occurs 3 times in 209
paragraphs). But a distinct habit shows in the §3 sequence: several paragraphs open by
announcing their own job.

- "The diagnosis is exact."
- "The verdicts are those of the previous case, and for the same reasons."
- "That is a real constraint on the pressure case, and it excludes the simplest candidates."

> **Attractor D — the orienting opener.** Tell the reader what kind of paragraph this is
> before the paragraph does it.

### Conservative direction

Delete the orienting sentence and start with the content. The paragraph's job is
visible from what it says.
