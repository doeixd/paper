# Summary: Redundancy Pass, and an Honest Assessment of the Length Problem

## What was cut

Two cuts, both removing text the argument had outgrown rather than defenses.

1. **§5.1 "Screening Off Already Suffices" deleted** (141 words). After the reframe,
   the paper answered this objection twice: weakly here, and strongly in §2.2 ("Which
   Reading of Screening Off Closure Secures"), which concedes the rejoinder outright
   and presses it into the thesis. Answering the same objection twice, the second time
   defensively, is worse than answering it once well. Its label was unreferenced, and
   nothing in it was unique: the credit to Franklin and Robertson it carried is made
   more fully in §1 and §2.2.

2. **§3.3's four-element preamble compressed** (~250 words). The section opened by
   announcing four elements ((i) intervention indexing, (ii) conditions for realist
   commitment, (iii) graded verdict protocol, (iv) downgrade rules) and then restated
   each in turn. Those four items are §1.1's point 2 verbatim in substance, which
   already says the criterion supplies "explicit intervention indexing, graded
   verdicts, and downgrade conditions." The "Second" and "Fourth" paragraphs
   duplicated §1.1 and the §3 opener; the graded-category definitions in "Third"
   duplicated the conclusion (§6).

   **Preserved:** the claim that verdicts "are not merely epistemic confidence labels"
   but report where a candidate sits in the space of closure relations. This appears
   nowhere else in the manuscript and is load-bearing against the instrumentalism
   charge, so it was kept and re-anchored rather than dropped with the surrounding
   list. This is exactly the failure mode `CLAUDE.md` warns about, where a
   tightening pass silently removes a defense.

Result: 21,107 words to 20,713. Clean build, 50 pages, zero undefined references.

## The honest assessment

A 2% cut against a recommendation to halve the paper is not a result. It is a
finding, and the finding is that **the remaining length is not padding.**

I went looking for restatement with keyword analysis. The diffuse repetition the review
complained about is real and measurable: "downgrade" and its cognates appear 61 times
across 25 sections; "parasitic"/"borrowed" 40 times across 15. But almost every
instance is a clause carrying an actual qualification, not a restatement that can be
deleted. The paper is already disciplined about the obvious remedy: the objections
section opens by factoring out three standing commitments that "will not be restated
each time."

The 10,000 words that would have to go are not redundant prose. They are, in round
numbers:

- **Objections and replies, ~3,900 words** across nine remaining subsections. The
  review's reception note criticizes "exhaustive bullet-pointed objection handling" as
  part of what makes the paper read as padded.
- **Illustrations, ~2,600 words**: the distributed case, the near-tie prediction
  contrast, regime dependence, sparse-intervention qualification, stable-versus-merely-
  entailed. The review's fifth objection lands squarely here: these are "stipulated,
  not demonstrated," and every verdict is "the intuitively pre-ordained one."
- **Admissibility and regime apparatus, ~2,400 words**: hierarchical admissibility,
  regime individuation, admissibility disputes, procedural safeguards, verdict
  reporting.

Each is a defense against a real objection. Cutting any of them trades resilience for
brevity, and that is an author's call about what the paper is, not a copy-editing
decision an agent should take unilaterally. `CLAUDE.md` is explicit that the most
common failure mode in revision is silently dropping qualifications, and a 10,000-word
cut made on my own judgement would do exactly that at scale.

## Recommendation, for a decision

If the target is a specialist journal at 10,000 to 12,000 words, the cut has to come
from the three blocks above, and I would take them in this order.

1. **Illustrations first, cutting roughly 2,000 of the 2,600.** This is the only block
   where cutting *answers* a review objection rather than trading against one. The
   review's charge is that the criterion "always ratifies prior intuitions in its toy
   cases," so we learn nothing about hard cases. Four stipulated illustrations do not
   defend better than one, and the review's own remedy was "replace one toy example
   with one real worked case." Keeping one illustration plus the depression case, which
   is the only one that ends in a genuinely contested verdict, is stronger than keeping
   all of them.
2. **Objections second, consolidating nine into four or five.** Several were written to
   defend framings the paper no longer holds after the reframe, which is how §5.1
   became redundant. The same audit likely applies to the horizon-relativity and
   formal-closure-collapse replies.
3. **Apparatus last, and least.** This is where the paper's answers to the circularity
   charge live. It is the block I would most resist cutting, because the review already
   identifies the anti-circularity defenses as "procedural, not decisive." Thinning
   them makes the weakest part weaker.

The one thing I would not do is trim evenly across the paper. That preserves the
structure the review actually objects to, which is that the same five points recur,
while making each statement of them terser and harder to follow.

## Outstanding

1. **Length remains the largest open item**, and now needs a decision rather than a
   pass. See the ordering above.
2. **The blinded submission file is still stale**, now lacking five passes of work. It
   must be resynced from the main body before submission, and doing that before the
   length decision would be wasted effort.
3. **Title.** "Operationalizing Screening Off for Rainforest Admission" is now earned
   by the countermodel rather than overpromising, since operationalizing screening off
   is precisely and only what the paper claims to do. Recommend keeping it.
