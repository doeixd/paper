# Summary of Edits: Panpsychism Methodological Warrant Revision

Revised `pan/overdrawing-on-experience-mainbody.tex` to integrate the latest critique while preserving the paper's measured academic voice and limited argumentative scope.

The introduction now states the methodological distinction more explicitly: first-person experience establishes that consciousness exists, gives cautious access to features of the experiences with which we are acquainted, and does not by itself establish the metaphysical status of consciousness. This makes the paper's central target clearer. Loose rhetoric about introspection is only part of the issue. The deeper issue is the movement from knowing experience from within to treating experience as a guide to fundamental ontology.

The scope section now grants the sophisticated abductive version of panpsychism more directly. It explains that the abduction still carries a warrant problem when it treats ordinary first-person experience as the right kind of candidate to fill the categorical vacancy left by structural physics. That step is now framed as a substantive metaphysical interpretation, not a deliverance of acquaintance.

The discussion of phenomenal character now handles indeterminate experience more carefully. The revision clarifies that the argument does not require sharply articulated phenomenal character. Vague moods, peripheral vision, and foggy anticipation still count as ways experience is given. This prevents minimal or indeterminate experience from becoming an easy route to characterless subjectivity.

The Russellian section now targets the "positive grip" move more directly. It grants why phenomenal filling is tempting while stressing that first-person acquaintance supplies ordinary experience, not a settled account of that experience as intrinsic, categorical, or fundamental nature.

The explanatory-burden section now states the main consequence more sharply: once first-person warrant is cordoned off, the hidden phenomenal base must earn its keep through explanatory work. The revision also adds a fairness caveat that physicalism faces its own burden unless it denies or deflates the explanandum. This keeps the objection comparative rather than one-sided.

The conclusion now presents the result as a constraint rather than a disproof. It says that panpsychism must compete on flat abductive ground with rivals and adds that the argument does not vindicate illusionism or deflationary physicalism. The final framing treats panpsychism as the central case of a broader temptation to move too quickly from phenomenology to fundamental metaphysics.
