# Source Review: Franklin-Robertson and Effective OSR

## Franklin and Robertson

### Source thesis relevant to this paper
Franklin and Robertson want a rainforest admittance criterion that is compatible with theoretical reducibility. Their central move is that emergence requires screening off plus novelty, which lets them exclude gerrymanders without collapsing back into an irreducibility test.

### Citation and use suggestions
1. Use Franklin and Robertson in the introduction as the source of the paper's immediate dialectical target: not "what is fundamental?" but "what counts for admittance to the rainforest?" This fits the current introduction around `\section{Introduction: Closure and the Admittance Problem}` and the existing transition from lines 15-45.
2. Use them in `\subsection{Closure and the Primacy of Physics Constraint}` around lines 413-444 to motivate the second PPC pressure. Their own admittance framing helps show that the issue is not only physical compatibility, but non-redundancy relative to lower-level description.
3. Use them in `\subsection{Closure, Underdetermination, and Objecthood Discipline}` around lines 880-930 and again in `\subsection{Beyond Screening Off}` around lines 1754-1788 to make the common-cause proxy case do philosophical work. The point is not that the proxy is obviously real, but that screening off can be satisfied by borrowed stability.
4. Use them in the introduction's novelty/positioning passage around lines 99-140 to say that screening off and novelty are genuinely necessary filters, while closure supplies the missing transition-autonomy test.
5. Use them in the conclusion around lines 2190-2239 to keep the final claim cumulative rather than competitive: closure completes rainforest admission, it does not replace screening off or novelty.

- "admittance criteria for the rainforest" (`=== PAGE 3 ===`, lines 77-78).
- "theoretical reducibility is divorced" (`=== PAGE 4 ===`, line 139).
- "must be emergent" (`=== PAGE 2 ===`, around lines 88-90).

### What not to claim
- Do not say Franklin and Robertson already supply closure, intervention indexing, or a lattice ontology.
- Do not say they settle the proxy/common-cause case in the strong sense our paper needs.
- Do not make them sound like they are defending a general objecthood theory for all macro-candidates independent of their own emergence criterion.

### Where the current manuscript underuses or misframes them
- The paper already uses them correctly as a screening-off-plus-novelty interlocutor, but it still underuses their explicit rainforest-admittance framing.
- The manuscript should say more clearly that their view is reducibility-compatible, because that is what makes them a useful bridge to closure rather than a rival irreducibility view.
- The draft should keep treating the proxy case as a real pressure case for screening off, not as a cheap refutation of their proposal.

## Ladyman and Lorenzetti

### Source thesis relevant to this paper
Ladyman and Lorenzetti unify effective realism with OSR by tying effective ontology to modal structure, real patterns, and scale-relative ontology. Their key point is that ontology can be real and mind-independent while still being domain- and scale-relative.

### Citation and use suggestions
1. Use them in `\subsection{Novelty and Positioning}` around lines 108-139 to clarify the paper's relation to effective realism. The point should be that closure adds admission and downgrade discipline to an already realist scale-relative picture, not that OSR is being replaced.
2. Use them in `\subsection{Closure and the Primacy of Physics Constraint}` around lines 413-444 to support the claim that the PPC is a non-redundancy constraint, not a desert principle. Their scale-relativity thesis helps show why microphysical completeness does not automatically privilege the micro-level.
3. Use them in `\subsection{Why Closure Carries Ontological Weight}` around lines 724-780 to back the move from representational convenience to objective modal structure. Their real-pattern framing is the cleanest source for the claim that the paper is tracking ontology, not merely model fit.
4. Use them in `\subsection{Closure, Underdetermination, and Objecthood Discipline}` around lines 880-930 and in the objection replies around lines 1831-1849 to support the claim that `robust`, `qualified`, and `indeterminate` are ontology-reporting verdicts, not just confidence scores.
5. Use them in the conclusion around lines 2190-2239 to say that the rainforest is best understood as a scale-relative structured space, but keep the closure criterion as this paper's contribution rather than theirs.

### Short quotes
- "To be is to be a real pattern" (`=== PAGE 9 ===`, line 350).
- "Scale relativity of ontology" (`=== PAGE 9 ===`, line 362).
- "always effective ontology" (`=== PAGE 9 ===`, line 373).
- "realist position" (`=== PAGE 3 ===`, line 82).

### What not to claim
- Do not say they give an intervention-indexed objecthood criterion. They give the metaphysical frame for effective ontology, not our specific diagnostic.
- Do not say they already solve the admittance problem for rainforest realism in the exact way this paper does.
- Do not collapse their real-patterns account into closure alone. Their view is broader than closure and should stay broader in the memo and later drafting.

### Where the current manuscript underuses or misframes them
- The paper currently cites them mainly as `effective OSR` support. That is correct, but too thin. Their explicit scale-relativity thesis should be used more directly when the paper explains why a macro-level can be real without being fundamental.
- The draft should use their real-pattern language more explicitly in the transition from formal closure results to ontological verdicts.
- The manuscript should not imply that they themselves endorse the paper's closure criterion. They supply the metaphysical setting, not the paper's final admission rule.
