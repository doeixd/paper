# Reviewer-Response Revision to `real-patterns-need-closure-SYNTHESE-mainbody.tex`

## Context

Two AI reviews of the closure paper (one structural/stylistic, one
philosophically sharper). The second review identified an internal
tension between the intro's "necessary but not sufficient" framing
and §5.1's implicit concession that Franklin and Robertson's criterion
already has the conceptual resources for the modal reading of
conditional independence. Most of this revision addresses that tension
and related consequences. Conservative trim approach throughout: only
cut verbatim duplicates or genuinely weakening material, never
qualifications.

## Changes by tier

### Tier 1 — Structural reframing

1. **Title change.** Updated across `sn.tex`, `sn-submission.tex`, and
   `title-page.tex`. Previous: *Beyond Screening Off: Transition Closure
   as the Dynamical Criterion for Rainforest Admission.* New: *Real
   Patterns Need Closure: Operationalizing Screening Off for Rainforest
   Admission.* The repo-consistent main title is kept, and the
   "operationalizing" subtitle replaces the overclaiming "beyond"
   framing — aligns the visible thesis with what the paper actually
   defends (closure as the admissibility-indexed selector, not as a
   replacement for screening off).

2. **Realigned novelty framing.** Thesis paragraph (§1), §1.1 item (1),
   §5.1 objection title and reply, and conclusion paragraph 1 all
   rewritten. Where the paper previously said "screening off plus novelty
   is necessary but not sufficient," it now says "indispensable but
   admits two readings ... that existing admittance tests do not
   discriminate," with closure as "the admissibility-indexed selector
   that forces the disambiguation." The §5.1 subsection is retitled
   *"Screening Off Already Suffices"* and its reply now explicitly grants
   that F&R's criterion has the conceptual resources for the modal
   reading, clarifying that the operational selector is what the paper
   adds.

3. **Three-theses signpost.** New paragraph inserted in §1 before the
   structured-space paragraph: T1 (closure as anti-gerrymandering
   constraint) / T2 (closure as the criterion for robust commitment
   under structural-realist and interventionist commitments) / T3
   (programmatic structured-space reading of rainforest realism).
   Readers who grant one thesis but not the next can take the paper's
   contribution in corresponding part. Conclusion paragraph 4 now
   cross-references this signpost and marks the structured-space
   reading as programmatic.

4. **Decoupled structured-space metaphysics.** The "three further
   consequences" paragraph in §3.3.1 (~15 lines) was compressed to a
   single paragraph (~8 lines), cross-referenced to the primary
   structured-space defense in §"Closure, Underdetermination, and
   Commitment Discipline," and flagged as "without making the core
   argument depend on them." This keeps T3 available to readers who
   want it while isolating T1/T2 from its fate.

5. **Rosas take/reject paragraph.** Found already present at the head
   of §3 (lines ~972–979): "What is taken from their treatment is the
   formal diagnostics of informational, causal, and computational
   closure, and the strong-lumpability benchmark. What is added here
   is an intervention-indexed criterion..." No edit required.

### Tier 2 — Argumentative strengthening

6. **Second pressure case added: hidden-feedback parasitism.** New
   §3.3.2 placed after the common-cause proxy subsubsection. A
   structurally distinct parasitism source: fast hidden dynamics (not
   a shared upstream driver) that continually relax the macro-variable
   back toward its apparent attractor, so admissible perturbations on
   timescales shorter than the hidden relaxation expose within-class
   heterogeneity. Full five-step reporting template. A closing
   paragraph draws out the convergence: two structurally different
   parasitism sources with the same failure signature — passive
   conditional independence sustained by off-partition organization.
   This blocks the "common-cause is an edge phenomenon" retreat that
   Review 2 flagged as available to a committed F&R defender. The
   wedge paragraph in §1, §Why Closure is Not Just Screening Off, and
   §5.1 now reference both cases.

7. **Admissibility three-class framing.** New structural paragraph at
   the top of §Regime Individuation and Circularity. Classifies cases
   as (A) regime specification uncontroversial → criterion adjudicates;
   (B) contested but iteration stabilizes → reflective equilibrium
   converges; (C) contested and unstable → indeterminate verdict is
   the discipline working, not the criterion failing. This promotes
   the "relocates rather than resolves" observation from a mid-section
   concession to an explicit structural feature.

8. **Admissibility checklist.** Already present at §2.5 (lines 538–553):
   four-condition numbered template. No edit required.

### Tier 3 — Local defensive fixes

9. **Expanded Kim reply (§5.3).** Added a paragraph engaging the
   supervenience-plus-completeness stage of Kim's argument directly.
   Grants both premises, denies the exclusion conclusion on the ground
   that supervenience is instantaneous-realization-indexed while
   closure-autonomy is admissible-perturbation-indexed, so token
   micro-sufficiency does not entail type-level redundancy.

10. **Traffic example fixed.** Replaced the straw vehicle-ID-cluster
    partition ($P_{id}$) with a time-of-day partition ($P_{tod}$) —
    five-minute clock buckets calibrated to typical commuter patterns.
    That is a reasonable modeler's choice that a researcher might
    actually propose. Under admissible perturbations (ramp metering,
    incident response, weather-driven rerouting), the time-of-day
    partition becomes brittle because perturbations don't respect clock
    boundaries. Updated in the §2.6 regime-individuation anchor and in
    the full §4.9 worked example (five-step disclosure).

11. **Horizon-stability worked example.** New paragraph in §5.9
    ("Horizon-Relativity Makes the Criterion Too Weak"). Concrete
    illustration: if lane-flow closure is claimed at $L = 15$ min,
    check at $L = 20$ and $L = 30$ min; robust standing requires no
    rank-flip against the time-of-day partition and no viability-floor
    crossing. Replaces a promissory-note feel with a specific
    procedure.

12. **Phlogiston reconstructive acknowledgment.** Added one sentence to
    §4.7 granting that the principled-development vs. ad-hoc-rescue
    distinction is itself contested philosophy of science and that the
    illustration is reconstructive rather than a clean empirical test.
    The closure contribution is then stated as supplying *one*
    principled way to draw the distinction (within-class
    micro-bookkeeping vs. new transition-autonomous structure).

### Tier 4 — Conservative trim and conclusion

13. **Trim.** The paper was already disciplined about non-repetition;
    the main redundancy was the "three further consequences" paragraph
    in §3.3.1, which was compressed as part of the structured-space
    decoupling (Tier 1 item 4). No further cuts taken, per the
    instruction to only cut verbatim duplicates or weakening content.

14. **Conclusion sharpened.** Paragraph 1 rewritten to follow the
    reviewer's closing shape: screening off + novelty remains
    necessary; observational success can be parasitic (both pressure
    cases cross-referenced); closure distinguishes autonomous from
    borrowed macro-structure; strong lumpability is the exact
    benchmark; non-ideal cases require graded verdicts and downgrade
    discipline. Paragraph 4 rewritten to explicitly separate
    established (T1, T2) from programmatic (T3) with a cross-reference
    to the three-theses signpost.

## Third reviewer pass — 5 surgical suggestions evaluated

A third AI review proposed five surgical edits. Evaluated and acted on
selectively per user's "safer cut" preference:

**Executed (3):**

- **Non-negligible viability floor patched (§4.2).** Replaced the
  magnitude-comparison-to-macro-gain formulation with a within-system
  comparandum formulation: a gain is non-negligible when its magnitude,
  observed between two candidate partitions, would be recorded as a
  genuine comparative difference under the same diagnostic conventions.
  This pre-empts a "where's the number?" attack and makes the
  ordering-stability emphasis later in §4.2 follow naturally.

- **§3.3 opening now leads with a compact four-point enumeration.**
  The four contributions (intervention-indexed closure, ontological-
  commitment conditions, graded verdict protocol, explicit downgrade
  rules) were already developed in prose across four paragraphs of
  §3.3 but buried. Surfacing them as a compact list at the section head
  gives a reviewer familiar with Rosas et al. a clean answer to "what's
  new" in the first paragraph. Detailed prose development retained below.

- **§2.12 one-sentence addition.** Added a sentence linking the
  phase-space-decoupling claim to the §1.2 structural-realist
  commitment: under structural realism, what makes such decouplings
  ontological is that they are the stable relational structure realist
  commitment was supposed to track in the first place, so a criterion
  that identifies them is picking out what the ontology consists in,
  not describing a consequence of it.

**Rejected (2):**

- **Consolidate §2.8, §2.11, §2.14.** The proposed restructure would
  cut §2.8 entirely, fold §2.11 into §2.7, and move §2.14's
  structured-space defense to §6. Rejected because (a) each subsection
  does distinct work (pattern-reality-vs-commitment, predictive-vs-
  interventional, underdetermination plus T3 defense); (b) moving the
  ~50-line structured-space defense to the conclusion would bloat §6
  and break the "T3 is programmatic upshot" separation; (c) user's
  safer-cut preference explicitly rules out aggressive structural
  consolidation.

- **Replace the legal-tender failed case with cellular pH homeostasis.**
  Rejected because the legal-tender failed case pairs with the monetary
  stable case, connects to the Ross-inflected social ontology strand,
  and tests the induced/stipulated clarification. The biological angle
  the reviewer wanted is already covered by the organism case at
  §4.5 lines 1642–1650, which specifically describes screening-structure
  breakdown under severe systemic disruption. Replacing wholesale
  would churn without gain.

## Fourth reviewer pass — 3 vulnerability-closing additions

A fourth review flagged three specific vulnerabilities not fully
addressed by the combined edits so far. All three were small single-
paragraph additions. All three executed.

- **Consolidated admissibility reply (§5.2).** The admissibility-
  circularity reply was previously distributed across §2.6, §2.7, §2.9,
  and a brief passage in §5.2, so a hostile reviewer reading §5.2 in
  isolation would find only the procedural safeguards. Replaced that
  brief procedural passage with a consolidated one that states the
  complete reply: admissibility selection is not a free parameter but
  is constrained by the system's actual control ecology (physically
  realizable, regime-preserving perturbations the system already faces
  under naturally occurring sources); the analyst identifies that
  ecology rather than constructing it; where the ecology is genuinely
  contested, the criterion correctly returns indeterminate verdicts;
  and the "shifting" worry treats intervention-class choice as free
  when the framework treats it as physically anchored, so the
  replacement is by a problem the world itself has largely solved.

- **Three-strengths self-awareness lead at §6 opening.** The existing
  conclusion follows the reviewer-suggested closing shape ("screening
  off remains necessary, observational success can be parasitic...")
  but led with that declarative summary before the self-aware three-
  strengths framing appeared later in paragraph 4. Prepended a short
  methodological-self-awareness paragraph that restates the three-
  strengths distinction from §1.3 with explicit section references,
  giving skeptical readers an immediate off-ramp to accept narrower
  subsets of the contribution.

- **"What closure is not" clarifications at end of §2.1.** Added three
  compact clarifications to pre-empt predictable misreadings before
  the reader encounters the substantive novelty work in §2.2: closure
  is not a test for every sense of pattern reality (weaker senses remain
  available); not a demand for perfect transition autonomy (graded
  verdict structure handles partial cases); not a replacement for
  special-science kinds (most earn qualified rather than robust
  standing, which is the intended output). Distinct from the three-
  theses signpost in §1.3 because the signpost is about claim strength,
  while these clarifications are about claim scope and character.

Build clean at 47 pages after these edits (+1 page from the three
additions).
