## What changed

- Added a short target-precision paragraph naming Strawson, Goff, and Ramm as careful cases where first-person authority is asked to support further metaphysical work.
- Revised the pure-awareness/reportability discussion to grant the private-acquaintance objection more explicitly, while preserving the public-evidence constraint and the original dilemma.
- Revised the explanatory-burden section to distinguish phenomenal structure from phenomenal existence, granting that panpsychists may aim the hidden phenomenal base chiefly at the latter.
- Used the saved attractor exercise to avoid over-broad "not X, but Y" framing, decorative closers, and excessive sentence stacking.

## Rationale

The review identified three places where a sympathetic referee could press the paper: lack of exact targets, possible conflation of structure and existence, and a compressed reply to private acquaintance in pure-awareness cases. These edits keep the thesis narrow. They do not claim to refute panpsychism. They clarify that once hidden phenomenal ontology is introduced, its warrant must be theoretical rather than directly inherited from first-person acquaintance.
