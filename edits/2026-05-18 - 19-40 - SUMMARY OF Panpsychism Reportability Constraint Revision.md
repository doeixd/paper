# Summary of Panpsychism Reportability Constraint Revision

This revision adds a focused reportability argument to section 4 of the panpsychism paper.

## Changes Made

- Added a new subsection, "The Reportability Constraint," after the existing discussion of depsychologized consciousness.
- Acknowledged the strongest contentless-awareness literature rather than dismissing it, including Metzinger on minimal phenomenal experience, Gamma and Metzinger's questionnaire study, Albahari on witness-consciousness, Thompson on pure awareness and dreamless sleep, and Laukkonen et al. on meditation-induced cessations.
- Added the evidential point that pure-awareness and cessation reports reach the theorist through memory, retrospective report, temporal location, and interpretation.
- Used Lin on totally selfless states, Windt on dreamless sleep temporality, Zahavi on first-person subjectivity, and Henry and Thompson on bodily perspective to show why report-based evidence tends to reintroduce the structure it may appear to exclude.
- Preserved the cautious dialectical stance: the paper does not deny that minimal, nondual, selfless, or contentless states exist. It argues only that reports of such states do not establish bare metaphysical subjectivity as a first-person datum.
- Updated `pan/overdrawing-on-experience.bib` and the root `references.md` with the new citations.

## Rationale

The revision strengthens the section 4 dilemma by adding an evidential prong. The original dilemma argued that if fundamental consciousness has phenomenal character, it is not a simple primitive, and if it lacks phenomenal character, it is not what first-person experience discloses. The new subsection blocks an obvious escape route: appealing to pure-awareness reports as evidence for bare subjectivity. Even granting such reports, their status as evidence depends on reportability conditions that involve memory, temporal order, and some standpoint of later attribution.

This keeps the argument modest and defensible. It does not enter the empirical dispute over whether pure awareness, dreamless sleep experience, or nirodha samapatti is real. It instead asks what those reports can warrant when used in a metaphysical argument for panpsychism.
