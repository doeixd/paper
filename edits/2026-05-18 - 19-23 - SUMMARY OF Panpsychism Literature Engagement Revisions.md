# Summary of Panpsychism Literature Engagement Revisions

## Changes made

- Added a literature-positioning paragraph in the scope section that situates the paper beside recent work on the combination problem and constitutive panpsychism.
- Added explicit engagement with Chalmers, Coleman, Mørch, Roelofs, and the Brüntrup and Jaskolla edited volume.
- Clarified that the paper is not trying to solve the combination problem literature. Its contribution is an upstream warrant constraint on the phenomenal posits that such theories introduce.
- Added a paragraph in the warrant-transfer section explaining how the argument intersects with combination-problem debates about micro-experience, micro-subjects, bonding relations, structural correspondences, and phenomenal powers.
- Added a calibration paragraph in the Goff/Strawson section noting that Mørch and Roelofs offer constructive theories rather than crude warrant transfers, while still forfeiting direct first-person warrant once they rely on theoretical machinery.
- Updated `pan/overdrawing-on-experience.bib` and `references.md` with the new sources.

## Rationale

The main referee risk was that the paper could appear to target a rhetorical tendency rather than a position held by serious panpsychists. These revisions make the dialectical target more precise. The paper now acknowledges the contemporary panpsychism literature and identifies its own narrower contribution: first-person evidence may establish consciousness and determinate phenomenal character, but it does not directly warrant the hidden phenomenal ontology deployed in panpsychist explanations.

This preserves the paper's modest scope. It does not pretend to answer Chalmers, Coleman, Mørch, or Roelofs on every combination-problem detail. Instead, it makes clear that if their posits succeed, their warrant is theoretical and explanatory, not transferred from first-person acquaintance.
