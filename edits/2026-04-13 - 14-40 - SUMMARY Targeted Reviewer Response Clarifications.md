## Targeted Reviewer Response Clarifications

### What changed

This pass addressed a narrow cluster of live reviewer concerns without changing the paper's architecture, examples, or thesis.

In Section 1.2, I added one short paragraph clarifying the relation between EOSR and interventionism. The manuscript now states more sharply that it does not require interventions as metaphysical glue, but uses intervention-invariance as evidence that a candidate partition is tracking stable organization across physically real, regime-preserving perturbations. In the admissibility discussion, I also replaced the phrase `Nature intervenes on itself` with plainer structural language about environmentally imposed perturbations, so the point reads less metaphorically and less like a causal-metaphysical slogan.

In Section 3, immediately after the strong-lumpability benchmark is introduced, I added a compact paragraph explaining that the exact benchmark does not ignore history. The paper now says earlier, rather than only in the objections section, that memory-bearing systems may require history-enriched state descriptions before the closure question is asked. I also shortened the later Markov objection reply accordingly so it now functions more as a callback than as the first place where that issue is answered.

In the common-cause wedge, I added one sentence making explicit that the screening-off success at issue is not a one-step snapshot. The paper now says directly that under passive observation the shared upstream driver can sustain the relevant conditional independence dynamically across time. In Section 5.1, I added one short clarifying sentence stating that the disagreement is not between static and dynamic screening off, but between dynamic observational screening off and transition stability under admissible perturbation.

At the first major lattice-payoff passage, I added a brief plain-language explanation of what the lattice amounts to: an ordered space of admissible partitions, some finer, some coarser, and some standing in clean nesting relations rather than flat competition. This is meant to make the metaphysical payoff more legible to readers who have not read Rosas.

In the regime/horizon discussion, I added a very compact ice-crystal analogy to make objective regime dependence more intuitive. The point is strictly limited: objective structures can be conditional on objective background constraints without becoming epistemically subjective.

Finally, I softened the tone of the reporting section slightly by recasting the assessment sequence as a safeguard against post hoc shifts in target, horizon, and admissibility rather than as a workflow recipe.

### Why these changes were made

The target of this pass was misreading risk, not substantive deficiency. Recent reviews were pressing three points repeatedly:

- that the paper might be smuggling thick interventionist metaphysics into an OSR frame,
- that the Franklin-Robertson wedge might rely on a static reading of screening off,
- that the exact Markov benchmark might appear irrelevant to path-dependent special-science systems if its memory qualifications arrive too late.

These edits were designed to make those readings harder to sustain while preserving the paper's conditional, referee-facing tone.

### Objections this pass is meant to pre-empt

- The paper is really doing Woodwardian causal ontology rather than structural realism.
- The common-cause proxy only works because the paper treats screening off as a static snapshot.
- Strong lumpability is a benchmark for memoryless toy systems rather than an exact benchmark whose relevance depends on the right state description.
- Regime and horizon dependence collapse the paper into subjective pragmatism.
- The lattice payoff is too underdefined for readers outside the Rosas literature.
- The reporting section reads like a methods appendix rather than like anti-goalpost-moving discipline.

### Remaining pressure points

The approximate-closure material and admissibility framework still carry much of the paper's philosophical burden in non-ideal domains. The present pass clarifies that burden, but does not remove it, nor should it.

The common-cause proxy remains the paper's main dialectical fulcrum. The manuscript now states more explicitly that the relevant screening-off success is dynamically sustained under passive observation, but this will continue to be the most scrutinized section, as it should be.
