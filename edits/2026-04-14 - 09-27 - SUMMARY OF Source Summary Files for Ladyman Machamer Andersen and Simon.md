# Summary of Edits

Created four new detailed source-summary files in `sources/detailed-source-summaries/`:

- `41-ladyman2026.md`
- `42-machamer2000.md`
- `43-andersen2025.md`
- `44-simon1962.md`

## What I Added

Each file follows the local house style already used in nearby summaries:

- key heading
- `## Full Citation`
- `## Detailed Summary`
- `## Core Argument Map`
- `## Relation to Real Patterns Need Closure`
- `## Reviewer-Relevant Cautions`
- `## Text Source`

The summaries were written directly from the local text-converted source files rather than inferred from outside abstracts or memory. I kept the RPNC relevance sections deliberately calibrated. They explain why each source matters for the project without overstating that any of these papers already supplies a full closure criterion.

## Source-Version Caveats

Two of the four summaries required explicit source-version notes:

- `41-ladyman2026.md` uses the local 2024 preprint text as a stand-in for the cited 2026 MIT Press chapter.
- `43-andersen2025.md` uses the local preprint text as a stand-in for the cited 2025 *Synthese* article.

Those caveats were stated both in `Reviewer-Relevant Cautions` and in `Text Source`, so future use of the summaries does not silently blur preprint and publication versions.

## Rationale

The main editorial goal was reliability rather than maximal breadth. I emphasized:

- the actual argumentative structure of each paper
- the limits and scope conditions each author states
- the specific way each source could help, or fail to help, RPNC

This should make the files safer to rely on later when drafting literature review sections, framing dialectical opponents, or checking whether a citation really supports a stronger closure-based claim.
