# Source Review: Jiang and Millhouse

## Jiang, 2024, *The metaphysics of mechanisms*

**Thesis summary relevant to the paper.** Jiang argues that object-based metaphysics of mechanisms runs into the bottoming-out problem, and that a structuralist OSR-style ontology is a better fit for mechanisms, special science practice, and the physics constraint. For our manuscript, the useful point is not just “structures instead of objects,” but the more precise claim that mechanism talk needs a lower-level metaphysical discipline that can explain when higher-level entities are only heuristically useful versus when they are ontologically serious.

**Where to use it more sharply.**
- Cite it in the paragraph that introduces mechanisms as a third point of contact, because it gives us a live example of why object-based special-science metaphysics now has to answer to physics.
- Use it in the PPC discussion as a supporting bridge: Jiang gives a concrete case in which the metaphysical question is not utility alone, but whether the lower-level ontology really bottoms out the higher-level story.
- Use it again where the manuscript says closure is an ontological criterion rather than a mere representational convenience, because Jiang’s OSR framing helps keep that claim aligned with recent mechanism literature.

**Short quotes and locations.**
- “potential alignment between OSR and processual ontology” (Abstract, lines 16-20).
- “objects asymmetrically depend on structures” (Page 11, lines 493-500).
- “dynamic causal processes are metaphysically underpinned by structures” (Page 15, lines 747-750).

## Jiang, 2025, *Biological object as real patterns*

**Thesis summary relevant to the paper.** Jiang defends effective realism for biology by treating objects as real patterns of processes. The account is explicitly utility-facing: objects are patterns that help explain, predict, and intervene, and the paper combines that with pluralism about carving objects at different scales. For our purposes, this is a useful interlocutor because it gives a current, biology-facing version of real-pattern realism that still risks stopping at explanatory utility unless closure is added.

**Where to use it more sharply.**
- Cite it in the opening literature paragraph where the manuscript situates current real-pattern work in biology, especially if we want a recent biology-facing example of the utility tradition.
- Use it in the “why closure is more than compression” and PPC passages as a foil: Jiang’s wording makes the permissive real-pattern picture visible, which helps show why our paper asks for transition autonomy rather than usefulness alone.
- Use it in the pluralism discussion only if we are explicitly contrasting plural carving with a stricter admissibility/downgrade discipline.

**Short quotes and locations.**
- “defined by their utility” (Abstract, lines 20-24).
- “stable, emergent features” (Abstract, lines 28-31).
- “multiple valid ways” (Section “Accommodating Pluralism,” lines 396-399).
- “explanatory power and pragmatic utility” (Section “The Criterion for a Pattern’s Reality,” lines 663-664).

## Millhouse, 2021, *Really Real Patterns* (`Really Real Patterns Millhouse 2021.txt`)

**Thesis summary relevant to the paper.** Millhouse is the best local source for the move from compression-only realism to a more demanding criterion based on similarity, structural fit, and counterfactual support. He also keeps two things in view that matter for our paper: first, predictive success can diverge from ontological fit; second, multiple realizability does not disappear when realism gets stricter. This makes him useful whenever we want to say that closure is not merely shorter description or better prediction, but a further test of dynamical fit.

**Where to use it more sharply.**
- Cite him in the compression/similarity transition, especially where the manuscript says compression alone is too permissive and closure adds a stricter test.
- Use him in the Wallace engagement, because his discussion of mapping and abstraction gives us a clean way to distinguish representational adequacy from transition autonomy.
- Use him in the anti-gerrymandering discussion, because he gives the strongest local language for why spurious mappings are not enough even when they are predictively useful.

**Short quotes and locations.**
- “compression and similarity” (Abstract, lines 51-57).
- “gerrymandered” (Page 8, lines 316-323).
- “high-level similarity” (Page 14, lines 639-654).
- “counterfactual-supporting mapping” (Page 15, lines 666-671).

## Distinctions We Must Preserve

- Utility, explanatory power, and pragmatic fruitfulness are not enough by themselves. They can show that a description is useful, but they do not yet show that lower-level variation has become dynamically irrelevant to macro-transition structure.
- Structural similarity is still weaker than closure. Millhouse gives us a strong similarity-based realism, but our paper needs the further claim that a candidate survives admissible intervention without borrowing its stability from upstream structure.
- Explanatory organization and transition autonomy must stay separate. A pattern can organize explanation well, or even compress efficiently, without yet being autonomous in the sense our manuscript needs.
- Recent real-pattern and effective-realist work should be treated as a family resemblance, not as a single settled position. Jiang’s biology paper is especially permissive on utility and stance, so it should be used as a foil when we want to show why closure is stricter.

## Warnings

- `Jiang-2025-Biological-object-as-real-patterns-preprint.txt` explicitly says the DOCX conversion does not preserve stable page numbers. Cite it by section heading and local line numbers, not by page.
- The two Millhouse files are not two different publications. They are the same 2021 article in two local transcriptions, so bibliography work should treat them as one source.
- Keep every direct quotation short. The local files are enough for targeted quotation, but the memo should not accumulate long blocks of verbatim text.
- When revising the paper later, do not let the real-pattern literature collapse into a generic “utility” story. The main philosophical gain in our manuscript is the transition-autonomy requirement, and that distinction needs to stay explicit.
