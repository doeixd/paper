Revised `latex/real-patterns-need-closure-SYNTHESE-mainbody.tex` to integrate a set of high-impact reviewer-style suggestions while preserving the paper's central claims, regime-indexing, and anti-gerrymandering discipline.

Main changes:

1. Terminology pivot away from default `macro-objecthood` framing.
The paper now defaults more often to `robust macro-level standing`, `robust macro-level commitment`, and related structural-realist language. This was done to reduce unnecessary exposure to mereological objections and keep the paper focused on when a macro-variable or macro-pattern warrants realist commitment. I retained limited object-language where it is dialectically useful, especially in the explicit composition discussion, and I left existing internal labels intact where changing them would add noise without philosophical gain.

2. Common-cause proxy argument reformatted into a visible staged argument.
The core pressure case against screening off alone was rewritten as a five-step list: observational success, screening off, admissible perturbation, reappearance of hidden heterogeneity, and verdict. This makes the wedge against structurally parasitic proxies much harder to miss on a skim and clarifies that the argument is not a verbal trick but a modal pressure case.

3. Admissibility section softened from strong ontological determination to procedural objectivity.
I removed the stronger suggestion that the system's dynamics uniquely and straightforwardly dictate the admissible intervention class. The revised formulation now says that domain interests can shape which control channels are worth testing, but objectivity comes from advance declaration, cross-comparison, auditability, and downgrade when verdicts are fragile across nearby defensible admissibility choices. This should make the section more resilient against philosophy-of-science objections about hidden subjectivity while preserving the paper's realist ambitions.

4. Tone and prose tightened in several high-friction passages.
I pruned some recurring defensive formulations and softened a few lines that risked sounding over-claiming, including one line that described closure as a `completion` of the admittance protocol. The replacement language keeps the paper confident while reducing needless rhetorical exposure.

5. Later-section consistency pass.
I propagated the terminology shift through the middle and back half of the manuscript so the paper no longer reverts unpredictably to `objecthood` language when discussing verdicts, formal interpretation, sparse intervention, or objections. This was important for definition drift and argumentative cohesion.

Why these changes improve the paper:

- They make the paper look less like an attempt to solve a general metaphysical composition problem and more like a disciplined structural-realist criterion for realist commitment to macro-patterns.
- They sharpen the Franklin and Robertson pressure case by making the common-cause proxy argument explicit, linear, and easy to track.
- They better anticipate reviewer pushback on admissibility by shifting the emphasis from `nature uniquely dictates the intervention class` to `the protocol prevents researchers from cheating and treats fragility itself as evidence`.
- They reduce repetitive throat-clearing without dropping the qualifications that protect the argument.
