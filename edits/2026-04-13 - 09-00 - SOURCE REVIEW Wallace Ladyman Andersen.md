## Wallace, `Real patterns in physics and beyond`

### Relevant thesis
Wallace treats real patterns as a way to understand higher-level ontology in physics as structurally and dynamically organized, not as mere composition or instrumental convenience. He is useful here because he explicitly says the real-pattern idea can be developed in phase-space or dynamic terms, while also warning against treating language or predicate-precision as the whole story.

### Citation and use suggestions
1. Use Wallace in the introduction’s roadmap, especially where the paper now promises that closure adds a dynamical admission criterion beyond compression and screening off. The best anchor is the introduction around `lines 183-196` and `2217-2220`.
2. Use him in `\S\ref{closure-and-admissibility}` and `\S\ref{closure-and-the-primacy-of-physics-constraint}` to support the claim that higher-level ontology is best understood as structure in distributions and dynamics, not as a mere list of macro-labels.
3. Use him in the section that distinguishes predictive closure from interventional closure, because Wallace gives you a source for saying that phase-space structure matters, but does not itself settle the ontological admission question.
4. Use him when explaining why the paper is not just adding another compression criterion. Wallace’s account supports the move from “useful representation” to “real structure in dynamics,” which is exactly the gap the closure criterion tries to fill.
5. Use him in the objection responses that warn against instrumentalism. The source lets you say the paper is not rejecting representational usefulness, only refusing to identify usefulness with objecthood.
6. Use him in the late discussion of lattice-shaped ontology, but only as a bridge. He can support the thought that the higher-level ontology is dynamically organized, while the lattice claim remains this paper’s stronger metaphysical packaging.

### Short quotes and locations
- `converted text line 300-304`: “structures — in the distributions and dynamics”
- `converted text line 505-512`: “maps or diagrams”
- `converted text line 799-811`: “real patterns all the way down”

### Overclaim risks
- Do not make Wallace say that phase-space language by itself settles the realist question. He explicitly treats ontology as only partly fixed by representation.
- Do not use him as if he endorsed your full closure criterion. He supports a dynamic structural reading, not your stronger admissibility and downgrade discipline.
- Avoid turning his remarks about mathematized representation into a claim that natural language is philosophically secondary in all respects. His point is narrower.

### Targeted improvement suggestions
- In the dynamic/phase-space discussion, say that phase-space language helps reveal transition structure, but closure is doing the extra work of deciding when that structure is enough for objecthood.
- In the commuting-map style worries, use Wallace to motivate the idea that a good higher-level description must preserve structure across representations, but then add your own claim that preservation is still insufficient without admissible intervention stability.
- Keep the manuscript’s language about dynamics and structure, but avoid implying that a mathematically smooth representation automatically earns ontological standing.

## Ladyman, `Patterns all the way up`

### Relevant thesis
Ladyman’s chapter is the closest source for the paper’s rainforest background. He argues that real patterns are scale-relative, projectible, and tied to mathematical structure in the world, while also insisting that the pattern idea should not be restricted by a sparse or uniqueness-driven metaphysics. He is especially useful for defending the shift from compression to projectibility and from a single hierarchy to scale-relative ontology.

### Citation and use suggestions
1. Use him in the introduction where the paper frames rainforest realism as scale-relative structural realism and says closure sharpens its non-redundancy demand. Anchor: `lines 4-14`, `42-45`, and `2190-2207`.
2. Use him in `\S\ref{closure-and-admissibility}` and the PPC subsection to support the claim that projectibility is necessary but not sufficient. He clearly links real patterns to projectible generalisations.
3. Use him in the section on the Primacy of Physics Constraint. The source gives direct support for saying that naturalistic metaphysics should not hard-wire a single privileged level or a single hierarchy of fundamentality.
4. Use him when translating the “what closure formalizes” table into prose. His chapter repeatedly says real patterns are scale-relative, dynamic, and linked to coarse-graining, which matches your effort to make the rainforest into a lattice of admissible grains.
5. Use him in the objections section on pluralism and underdetermination, especially where the paper says multiple closed grains need not collapse into arbitrariness.
6. Use him to justify the distinction between predictive and interventional closure: projectibility is part of the story, but the paper is right to add a stricter transition-stability test.
7. Use him sparingly in the conclusion. He can support the “real patterns all the way up” framing, but the paper should still claim the lattice ontology and the verdict structure as its own contribution.

### Short quotes and locations
- `converted text line 9-14`: “projectible generalisations that predict and explain”
- `converted text line 14-15`: “real patterns are computational”
- `converted text line 54-55`: “the special sciences are possible”

### Overclaim risks
- Do not say Ladyman already gives the closure criterion. He gives projectibility, scale-relativity, and naturalistic pressure, not your full intervention-indexed admissibility test.
- Do not overread the PNC passage as if he had already endorsed your exact PPC reconstruction. The source still uses a more general naturalistic-closure benchmark.
- Avoid implying that he denies ontology altogether. He is explicitly realist, but structurally and scale-relatively so.

### Targeted improvement suggestions
- On dynamic/phase-space framing, distinguish “patterns as dynamic structure” from “patterns as transition-autonomous grains.” The latter is the manuscript’s added step.
- On commuting-map style concerns, say that your closure test captures when a higher-level representation commutes with admissible interventions in the relevant regime, not just when it compresses well or tracks outputs.
- On the PPC, make explicit that closure operationalizes the non-redundancy side of the constraint by asking whether lower-level differences still make a difference at the macro level.

## Andersen, `The density of structure`

### Relevant thesis
Andersen’s core claim is that sparse structure should not be presupposed. The world may be dense in genuine structure, and the philosopher’s job is not to force uniqueness but to let method and inquiry sort among many genuine pattern candidates. This is especially relevant to the manuscript’s verdict taxonomy and its defense of multiple admissible closed grains.

### Citation and use suggestions
1. Use Andersen in the conclusion and pluralism objection sections, especially around `lines 1984-2001`, `1666-1672`, and `2231-2239`. He gives the clearest support for saying that multiple admissible grains are not an embarrassment if structure is genuinely dense.
2. Use him in the section that explains why verdict categories are ontology-reporting rather than mere confidence markers. His paper helps justify a ranked but non-unique closure lattice.
3. Use him in the underdetermination discussion. He gives a direct argument against the assumption that a good realism must be sparse in advance.
4. Use him in the “closure is too conservative” response. His pragmatist line supports the idea that there can be more genuine structure than any inquiry exhausts, which makes qualified verdicts philosophically respectable.
5. Use him in the PPC and admissibility discussion only indirectly. His paper is not about closure, but it is helpful for the broader point that methodological narrowing should not be built into metaphysics too early.
6. Use him to support the claim that the paper’s lattice is not a uniqueness machine. The lattice can contain many admissible grains without becoming permissive in the arbitrary sense.
7. Use him when clarifying why the paper keeps downgrade rules. Density does not imply “anything goes”; it implies that genuine structure may outstrip any single sparse ontology.

### Short quotes and locations
- `converted text line 10-10`: “the world may be dense with structure”
- `converted text line 72-72`: “there is no metaphysical magic”
- `converted text line 96-96`: “the direction of fit is from the world”

### Overclaim risks
- Do not use Andersen as if he were defending your exact closure criterion. He is arguing for density and pragmatist pattern metaphysics, not for intervention-indexed objecthood.
- Do not make him say that all patterns are equally good. He explicitly says that further methodological considerations still matter.
- Avoid sliding from “dense structure” to “every candidate is real.” His own view is richer than that.

### Targeted improvement suggestions
- In the density/pluralism discussion, use Andersen to say that multiplicity of closed grains is evidence for density, not a failure of realism.
- In the commuting-map style discussion, treat closure as a way of ranking candidate grains inside a dense structure space, rather than as a mechanism for selecting a single privileged grain.
- In the dynamic/phase-space discussion, use Andersen only to reinforce the methodological point that there can be more genuine structure than a sparse metaphysics would allow.

## Cross-source synthesis for the manuscript

1. Wallace should do the work of making the dynamic and phase-space framing look philosophically serious.
2. Ladyman should do the work of linking real patterns to projectibility, scale-relativity, and the rainforest background.
3. Andersen should do the work of making density and pluralism look principled rather than permissive.
4. Your manuscript should then add the missing step: closure as an intervention-indexed, regime-sensitive, ontology-reporting criterion that turns those background ideas into a structured admissibility story.
5. The safest phrasing is cumulative: compression identifies candidates, projectibility and dynamic structure discipline them, closure decides whether they are autonomous enough to count as serious macro-ontology.
6. The main overclaim to avoid is making any of these sources sound like they already contain the full closure-lattice picture. They support the path to it, not the exact destination.
