## Final Polish Pass

### What changed

This pass made a final set of small editorial changes aimed at reducing referee friction without altering the paper's architecture or thesis.

In the introduction, I restored one sentence on Millhouse to make the conceptual map clearer. The compressed literature paragraph now again signals that Millhouse is a nearby tightening of Dennett-style realism that still stops short of requiring transition autonomy. This restores a useful orientation point without rebuilding the earlier literature parade.

In the approximate-closure section, I added one compact paragraph clarifying where judgment is constrained. The manuscript now says more directly that approximate closure is not a discretionary replacement for exact closure, but the non-ideal evidential route to the same ontological target, constrained by upstream fixing, comparative robustness, and explicit downgrade or indeterminacy when rankings do not stabilize.

In the reporting and evidential-discipline section, I compressed the surrounding commentary so it reads less like a case-study manual. The schema remains, but the prose around it is shorter and more clearly subordinate to the paper's ontological argument.

I also reduced duplication between late Section 3 and the opening of Section 5.1. The common-cause wedge now more clearly lives in late Section 3, while the objection reply functions as a shorter callback rather than a near re-presentation.

Finally, I replaced the second occurrence of `macro-what-follows` in the conclusion with the more formal phrase `the relevant macro-transition profile under the declared constraints`, since the term had already been defined earlier and did not need a second informal appearance there.

### Why these changes were made

Recent reviews converged on the same remaining issue: the paper's core argument was now visible, but a small amount of duplication and protocol-style prose still risked diluting its force. The paper also benefited from one restored conceptual marker in the introduction, namely the Millhouse comparison, because that helps readers see the gradient from Dennett-style realism to the stronger transition criterion defended here.

The goal of this pass was therefore editorial sharpening: make the paper land faster, make the approximate-closure framework look more disciplined, and keep the center of gravity on the metaphysical criterion rather than on reporting procedure.

### Objections this pass is meant to pre-empt

- The paper still repeats its core point after the argument has already landed.
- The approximate-closure framework looks like a loose model-selection methodology rather than a constrained access route to a real property.
- The reporting section makes the paper sound like a procedural manual.
- The introduction no longer makes clear how the paper differs from nearby stronger real-pattern views such as Millhouse's.
- `macro-what-follows` still sounds too informal when reused later in the paper.

### Remaining pressure points

The approximate-closure material is now more explicitly constrained, but that remains the section where a skeptical reviewer is most likely to press on judgment and methodology. The manuscript now states the relevant constraints more directly, which should help, but it does not eliminate the fact that non-ideal ontology requires disciplined judgment.

The common-cause proxy remains the paper's main argumentative fulcrum. The present pass reduces duplication around it, but readers will continue to concentrate their scrutiny there first, as they should.
