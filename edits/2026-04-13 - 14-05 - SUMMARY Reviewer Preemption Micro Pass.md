## Reviewer Preemption Micro Pass

### What changed

This pass made a small set of targeted clarifications aimed at pre-empting hostile or inattentive readings without reopening the paper's structure.

In the intervention and causation discussion, I added one sentence clarifying that the paper is not simply doing within-partition Woodwardian causal inference. The manuscript now says more explicitly that ordinary intervention tests operate within an accepted variable scheme, whereas the present criterion asks when a candidate coarse-graining has earned the right to figure as a macro-variable at all.

In the admissibility discussion, I added a short concession-and-constraint passage for the special sciences. The paper now acknowledges that regime, target, and control-channel specification can be interest-guided at the front end in an ordinary way, while insisting that closure verdicts are not thereby interest-relative once those specifications are fixed and openly defended. It also now states more plainly that instability across defensible specifications should yield downgrade or indeterminacy rather than forced realism.

In the formal benchmark and leakiness sections, I added two short clarifications to keep the formalism visibly subordinate to the philosophy. The first says that the formal illustrations do not independently prove the metaphysical claim, but make the relevant failure mode transparent enough for philosophical assessment. The second explains in plain language what a non-zero conditional mutual information means in the present context: once the current macrostate is fixed, residual microdetail still helps predict the next macrostate, which is the signature of a leaky partition.

In the non-ideal and sparse-intervention sections, I added one sentence clarifying that what is graded is not confidence alone, but the stability of closure across regime, horizon, admissibility, and robustness variation. I also added a sentence making explicit that admissible perturbation evidence in observational sciences can come from naturally occurring disturbances, comparative cases, exogenous shocks, and similar physically relevant perturbations, not only from humanly executable interventions.

### Why these changes were made

Recent reviews and our own discussion identified a recurring class of objection: the paper can be misread as if it were merely restating ordinary interventionism, as if admissibility in the special sciences were simply analyst preference, as if the formalism were decorative, or as if observational sciences were penalized merely for lacking human control channels.

The goal of this pass was not to add new argument, but to make those readings harder to sustain. Each edit therefore clarifies a distinction the paper already relies on, but does so in a calmer and more explicit way.

### Objections this pass is meant to pre-empt

- The paper is just doing Woodwardian causal inference in new vocabulary.
- Admissibility in socially structured domains is just a disguised analyst choice.
- The formal apparatus is doing rhetorical rather than philosophical work.
- Graded verdicts are only epistemic confidence labels.
- Observational sciences are being downgraded merely because humans cannot intervene directly.

### Remaining pressure points

These edits improve the paper's defensive posture, but they do not remove the fact that admissibility and approximate closure still carry substantial philosophical weight. A skeptical reviewer can still press there, though the manuscript now states more plainly how those judgments are constrained and what happens when they do not stabilize.

The common-cause wedge remains the paper's main argumentative fulcrum. That is appropriate, but it also means readers will continue to focus their scrutiny there first.
