# Reviewer-Reply Sharpening Pass

Date: 2026-04-22

Target file: `latex/real-patterns-need-closure-SYNTHESE-mainbody.tex`

These edits absorb the strongest lines of defense from a steelmanned
author-side reply to anticipated reviewer pressure. Each edit sharpens
an argument that was already implicit in the paper but was not stated
crisply enough to land on first reading.

## 1. Admissibility burden reframed as universal

§2.5 ("Admissibility and Hierarchical Evaluation") now states early
that any serious macro-ontology must answer the same admissibility
question; closure does not invent the burden but makes the
corresponding choices public, comparable, and downgrade-sensitive.
This shifts the dialectic from "closure has a problem" to "closure
makes a universal problem auditable."

## 2. Relocating disagreement is the right outcome

§2.6 ("Regime Individuation and Circularity") now closes the iterative
paragraph with explicit acknowledgment that the framework sometimes
relocates disagreement rather than resolving it, and that this is the
intended outcome in hard cases. The aim is to distinguish robust from
qualified or indeterminate verdicts, not to guarantee a unique answer
in every domain.

## 3. Indeterminate standing is not a declaration of unreality

§2.7 ("Pattern Reality Versus Robust Macro-Commitment") now explicitly
states that indeterminate standing is a refusal to overstate
commitment when evidence does not stabilize, and that the criterion is
restrictive about robust commitment rather than about every possible
sense of reality. This forecloses a common misreading that takes
indeterminate verdicts to be eliminativist.

## 4. Which kind of modeling success is ontology-relevant

§2.13 ("Why Closure Carries Ontological Weight") now contains the
sharpened thesis that the paper is not smuggling ontology out of
modeling success; it is defending a specific thesis about which kind
of modeling success is ontology-relevant, namely transition autonomy
under admissible perturbation. This pre-empts the "you are inferring
metaphysics from prediction" misreading.

## 5. Closure as the missing selector (in §3.4.1)

The closing of the dialectical paragraph in §3.4.1 ("The Common-Cause
Proxy as the Decisive Test") now lands the punchline crisply: closure
is not a rival principle bolted on from outside but the missing
selector that disambiguates which reading of screening off matters
for robust admission. This sharpens the relationship to F&R from
"clarification" to "operational selector."

## 6. Same missing-selector line mirrored in §5.1

The same one-line punchline now appears at the end of the §5.1
"Beyond Screening Off" reply, so a reviewer reading only the
objections-and-replies section also encounters the sharpened framing.

## Scope

All six edits are 1–2 sentences each. No content was removed. No new
named concepts, no boldface, no em-dashes. Net length impact:
approximately +9 sentences. The PDF grew by one page (42 → 43).
