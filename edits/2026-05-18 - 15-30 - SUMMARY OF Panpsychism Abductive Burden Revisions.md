# Summary of Panpsychism Abductive Burden Revisions

## Changes made

- Revised the Springer mainbody to make the paper's target more explicit: the argument is a constraint on panpsychist warrant, not a refutation of panpsychism.
- Added direct engagement with the stronger panpsychist reply that first-person evidence functions as one premise in an inference to the best explanation.
- Softened claims about what first-person experience discloses. The draft now says that experiences with which we are acquainted have determinate phenomenal character, rather than relying on stronger claims about embodiment, attention, memory, or self-relation across all conscious states.
- Revised the explanatory-burden section so it does not require panpsychism to explain every cognitive feature by the hidden phenomenal base alone. The sharper burden is now whether the hidden base explains enough, once organization accounts for ordinary conscious structure, to justify its theoretical cost.
- Updated the Springer wrapper abstract to match the revised argument.

## Rationale

These edits make the argument harder to dismiss by conceding the strongest panpsychist formulation. Panpsychism can be framed as an abductive metaphysical theory: consciousness is known directly, physics gives structure, radical emergence seems suspect, and a phenomenal or proto-phenomenal base is proposed as the best explanation. The revised draft accepts that dialectical framing, then asks what explanatory work the hidden phenomenal ontology performs beyond blocking brute emergence.

The revisions also reduce vulnerability to objections from minimal experience, meditative states, moods, objectless anxiety, dreamless sleep reports, and pure-consciousness claims. The paper no longer needs to deny those cases. It needs only the more modest claim that first-person acquaintance does not present consciousness as bare metaphysical subjectivity without any determinate phenomenal character.
