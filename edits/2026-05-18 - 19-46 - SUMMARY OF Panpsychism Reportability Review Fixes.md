# Summary of Panpsychism Reportability Review Fixes

This revision addresses three review concerns about the new reportability subsection in section 4.

## Changes Made

- Narrowed the reportability claim so it applies specifically to public philosophical evidence from pure-awareness and cessation reports.
- Added a reply to the private-acquaintance objection: if pure awareness is claimed to be privately given, the original dilemma returns, since it must either have some phenomenal character or fail to be disclosed as experience in the relevant first-person sense.
- Distinguished minimal phenomenal experience and witness-consciousness from meditation-induced cessations, which are often treated as interruptions, absences, or border cases rather than as straightforward cases of minimal consciousness.
- Clarified that Lin's work on totally selfless states functions as an analogue for the reportability problem rather than as a direct argument about contentless awareness.

## Rationale

The aim was to make the reportability subsection harder to attack. The previous wording risked suggesting that all first-person warrant must be report-mediated. The revised version keeps the intended evidential point while leaving room for private-acquaintance claims, then explains why those claims still face the paper's central dilemma.
