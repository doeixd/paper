Summary

This pass made a small set of style-only edits to better align the Synthese draft with `STYLE_GUIDE.md` and the project writing instructions, without changing the paper's thesis, structure, examples, or citations.

Changes made

1. Tightened self-announcing prose.
   The opening introduction sentence was revised from "This paper argues..." to a more direct thesis statement. The conclusion's "staged protocol" phrasing was also replaced with less procedural language.

2. Softened the reporting section's manual-like tone.
   In the reporting/disclosure subsection, wording such as "reporting schema" and "report five items explicitly" was revised to sound less like a workflow checklist and more like a philosophical disclosure discipline. The Rosas sentence was also tightened so the section stays subordinate to the metaphysical argument rather than sounding like a methodological program.

3. Reduced residual licensing language.
   The verdict paragraph was lightly revised so it still states the practical significance of robust, qualified, and indeterminate verdicts, but with less "manual" or compliance-style rhetoric.

4. Removed the remaining quasi-technical shorthand.
   The single surviving use of "macro-what-follows" was replaced with the more formal "relevant macro-transition profile" to improve register consistency.

5. Synced the blinded submission source.
   The same local wording edits were copied into the inlined blinded submission file so it remains aligned with the main body source.

Why these edits were made

The current draft was already substantively strong. The remaining style risk was not philosophical weakness but slight residual friction against the project's resilience-first style: a few repeated self-announcing formulations, a slightly procedural reporting section, and one informal shorthand term. These edits aim to make the paper read more like a tightly argued philosophy paper and less like a hybrid of argument plus protocol.

What was deliberately not changed

- No substantive claims were strengthened or weakened.
- No literature positioning was altered.
- No examples were moved or expanded.
- No new terminology, notation, or citations were introduced.
- Existing qualifications, regime-indexing, and reviewer-facing defenses were preserved.
