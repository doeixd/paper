## What changed

I created a standalone planning document for a higher-level follow-up paper on acquaintance, revelation, warrant transfer, and theoretical posits in philosophy of mind.

The document includes:

- A working title and alternative title options.
- A core thesis, short thesis statement, and one-paragraph abstract draft.
- A proposed contribution framing that treats panpsychism as the central case study rather than the whole target.
- Key distinctions between datum and posit, acquaintance warrant and theoretical warrant, and revealing phenomenal character versus revealing metaphysical role.
- A full section-by-section outline.
- Objection handling.
- Guidance on how the follow-up relates to the current panpsychism paper.
- Warnings about what to avoid, including anti-introspection framing, physicalist overreach, excessive formalism, and unnecessary proliferation of named principles.

## Rationale

The current panpsychism paper contains a broader methodological argument. This outline extracts that argument without turning the current manuscript into a less focused paper. The follow-up would argue that first-person acquaintance gives philosophy of mind a datum, not a transferable license for hidden ontology. Panpsychism remains the cleanest case, but the proposed paper also applies the constraint to idealism, Russellian monism, phenomenal concept strategies, illusionism, introspective skepticism, and pure-awareness arguments.
