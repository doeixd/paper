## Purpose

This note consolidates the current status of the rainforest-reframing revision. It is based on:

- `latex/springer-sn-template/sn-article-template/claude-notes/overall.md.txt`
- the April 2026 edit summaries
- the current manuscript state after the reframing, cleanup, and continuity passes

The goal is to separate:

- what has already been implemented
- what has only been partly implemented

This is a tracking document, not a new argumentative proposal. It should let the next revision pass proceed without reconstructing the whole reframing history from chat logs.

## Core Repositioning Already Completed

The main reframing has been carried out.

Old framing:

- Dennett's compression criterion is too permissive.
- Closure is introduced primarily as the fix.

Current framing:

- Rainforest realism needs a disciplined admittance criterion.
- Franklin and Robertson are treated as the strongest recent account of admission via screening off plus novelty.
- Closure is presented as a further criterion that tests whether candidate macro-variables carry autonomous transition structure.
- The paper now presents itself as completing a live debate about admission and downgrade conditions, not merely correcting Dennett.

This larger shift is already visible in the manuscript's title, abstract, introduction, objections section, and conclusion.

## What Has Already Been Done

### 1. Front Matter and Positioning

- The Springer wrapper title has been changed to:
  `Beyond Screening Off: Transition Closure as the Dynamical Criterion for Rainforest Admission`
- The abstract has been rewritten around:
  - rainforest admission
  - Franklin and Robertson as the primary recent interlocutors
  - screening off as necessary but insufficient
  - closure as a transition criterion
  - the staged protocol of compression, screening off, and closure
- The blinded inlined submission source has been synchronized with the revised main body and front matter.

### 2. Introduction

- The introduction now opens with the admittance problem for scale-relative ontology rather than with an extended complaint about Dennett's permissiveness.
- Franklin and Robertson are introduced early as the most developed recent proposal.
- Dennett has been repositioned as historical origin rather than the primary present-day target.
- The basic staged protocol is now visible early:
  - compression identifies candidates
  - screening off plus novelty filters gerrymanders
  - closure tests whether the survivors carry autonomous transition structure
- The novelty framing has already been revised to track the new orientation.

### 3. Causation Tension

- A dedicated subsection on the causation worry has already been added.
- The current line is:
  intervention is being used as a diagnostic probe for structural autonomy, not as a metaphysical primitive.
- This resolves the immediate tension between a Woodward-style testing framework and Ladyman-style structural realism well enough for the paper's current purposes.

### 4. Franklin and Robertson Engagement

- Franklin and Robertson are no longer only background citations. They are now direct interlocutors in the main text.
- The paper now argues that screening off is not the end of the story.
- The common-cause or proxy style case has been inserted as the main wedge case.
- A dedicated objection subsection, `Beyond Screening Off`, has been added.
- The tone has been moderated so the disagreement remains constructive rather than adversarial.

### 5. Rosas and Formal Machinery Positioning

- The manuscript now distinguishes between:
  - what formal closure machinery can diagnose
  - what philosophical interpretation is still needed if closure is to carry ontological weight
- Rosas et al. are now positioned as providing formal architecture rather than the full metaphysical interpretation.

### 6. Effective OSR Integration

- Ladyman and Lorenzetti are now cited in the body where effective, regime-relative ontology is being discussed.
- The current formulation is intentionally moderate:
  closure supplies operational discipline for effective realism
- This replaces a stronger earlier wording that risked overclaiming.

### 7. Instrumentalism Objection

- The instrumentalism objection has already been strengthened.
- The paper now includes the anti-instrumentalist structural-realist point that leakiness is not merely predictive inconvenience.
- The current line is roughly:
  where the paper's metaphysical commitments are granted, deficits in autonomous predictive structure are themselves part of what is ontologically at issue.

### 8. Conclusion

- The conclusion has been rewritten around the staged admittance result.
- It now closes by emphasizing the relation between:
  - candidate identification
  - screening-off filtering
  - closure-based admission or downgrade

### 9. Literature and Bibliography Work Already Done

- Recent literature has been added to the BibTeX database, including:
  - Franklin and Robertson
  - Ladyman and Lorenzetti
  - Andersen
  - Millhouse
  - Jiang 2024 and 2025
  - Wallace
  - the MIT Press real-patterns volume
  - Ladyman's chapter
- The Rosas entry now includes the verified arXiv DOI.
- The Ladyman chapter metadata has been corrected to the final book-year form rather than left as a planning placeholder.
- The source retrieval and transcription work was already completed earlier and committed separately.

### 10. Prose and Continuity Cleanup

- A cleanup pass removed several claims that were stronger than the manuscript had yet earned.
- A continuity pass then softened some of the newer text so it reads more like the inherited paper.
- The key result is that the manuscript is now more defensible and stylistically more seamless than the first reframing draft.

## What Is Only Partly Done

These items are present in some form, but they do not yet match the full ambition of the plan in `overall.md.txt`.

### 1. Franklin and Robertson Wedge

Partly done:

- The manuscript now clearly says that closure tests something screening off alone does not settle.
- The common-cause proxy case is already doing this work.

Still incomplete:

- The paper does not yet fully reconstruct Franklin and Robertson's own criterion on its own terms and then show, step by step, why the proxy would satisfy the observational and explanatory conditions while still failing closure.
- The current wording is safer:
  under passive observation, the case can look as though the screening-off requirement has been met.
- That wording is reviewer-safe, but it is weaker than the stronger dialectical result envisioned in the plan.

### 2. Ladyman and Lorenzetti Engagement

Partly done:

- The paper now cites effective OSR in the right places.
- The causation subsection and the regime-relative discussion already point in the right direction.

Still incomplete:

- The engagement is not yet deep enough to count as a full dialogue with their paper.
- The manuscript currently borrows support from their framework, but it does not yet fully spell out how closure relates to regime-dependence, effective ontology, and downgrade discipline in a detailed way.

### 3. Recent-Literature Engagement

Partly done:

- The sources are now mostly in the bibliography and at least lightly cited.
- The introduction has a cleaner recent-literature paragraph.

Still incomplete:

- Several sources are still functioning more as orientation markers than as genuine argumentative interlocutors.
- This is especially true for:
  - Ladyman's chapter
  - Wallace

### 4. Seamlessness Review

Still incomplete:

  - whether the introduction now promises more than later sections explicitly deliver

## What Is Still Missing or Underdeveloped

These are the main structural gaps relative to the plan.

### 1. Primacy of Physics Constraint

This is the clearest missing subsection-level item.

The plan called for an explicit treatment of the Primacy of Physics Constraint and for the paper to say, in substance:

- closure operationalizes the non-redundancy side of the PPC
- micro-irrelevance must be understood dynamically, in terms of transition structure, not merely descriptive convenience

Current state:

- pieces of this thought are present
- the common-cause case points toward it
- the projectibility discussion also points toward it

Missing:

- an explicit subsection or concentrated paragraph that names the PPC issue directly and shows how closure sharpens it

### 2. The Lattice as the Central Metaphysical Payoff

This remains the largest unfinished element.

The plan called for the paper to argue that:

- the structure of rainforest realism is the lattice of computationally closed coarse-grainings

Current state:

- Rosas is cited
- closure machinery is discussed
- there are scattered gestures toward formal architecture

Missing:

- a compact but explicit statement of the lattice claim
- a clear explanation of why that claim matters philosophically
- the three implications from the plan:
  - levels are discovered features of dynamics, not mere metaphors
  - levels are nested, not eliminatively ranked
  - causal autonomy does not automatically privilege the micro-level

### 3. Cashing Out "What Closure Formalizes in Ladyman and Ross"

The table in `overall.md.txt` has not yet been fully translated into prose argument.

Still needed:

- show explicitly how closure gives content to claims such as:
  - real patterns make a difference
  - the rainforest is structured
  - patterns are projectible
  - not all patterns are equal
  - fundamentality is not privileged

Right now these links are present only in dispersed and partly implicit form.

### 4. Verdict Structure and Lattice Ontology

Still needed:

- tie `robust`, `qualified`, and `indeterminate` verdicts directly to the lattice picture
- explain that these are not ad hoc epistemic labels
- show that they are the natural reporting vocabulary once ontology is understood as a structured space of more and less stable closure relations

### 5. Ross / ETMG Connection in the Monetary Example

The plan called for the monetary system example to connect more explicitly to Ross's economic ontology.

Current state:

- the example remains in place
- the broader Ross connection is not yet explicit enough

### 6. Specific Literature Engagement Still Missing

#### Ladyman chapter

Still needed:

- ask directly whether the phase-space or dynamic formulation already captures what closure adds
- state clearly where closure goes beyond that framework, if it does

#### Wallace

Still needed:

- connect the commuting-map idea to closure more directly
- show how closure distinguishes a genuine higher-level structure from a merely in-sample or representational fit

#### Jiang

Still needed:

- sharpen the contrast between utility for explanation or generalization and the stronger transition-autonomy demand being defended here

#### Andersen

Still needed:

- use structural density more explicitly in the pluralism discussion
- make the point that multiple admissible closed partitions are not an embarrassment if structure itself can be dense

### 7. Platonism Breadcrumb

This was intentionally left implicit.

That is still a live strategic choice, not an oversight.

Possible remaining move:

- add one quiet sentence in the conclusion suggesting that closure may help clarify the substrate-independent status of some mathematical or formal structures

Risk:

- if handled poorly, this will distract reviewers into a different debate

Recommendation:

- keep this optional and low priority

## What Still Needs Review Even If No New Writing Is Added

The following checks should still be run against the current manuscript.

### 1. Argumentative Fit Check

Review whether the new framing is actually supported by later sections.

Questions:

- Does the introduction promise a stronger result against Franklin and Robertson than the paper later establishes?
- Does the conclusion accurately reflect the strength of the argument as presently written?
- Do the unchanged technical sections still feel like they are answering the newly framed question?

### 2. Seamlessness Check

Review whether any of the new prose still sounds imported.

Questions:

- Are there sudden changes in cadence between inherited sections and newly added sections?
- Are there repeated formulations of the staged protocol that can be consolidated?
- Are there paragraphs that now sound too programmatic or strategic rather than reviewer-facing and philosophical?

### 3. Citation Fit Check

Review whether each new citation is doing clear argumentative work.

Questions:

- Is each cited source genuinely supporting or contrasting a claim?
- Are there citations that remain only decorative?
- Are any recent citations now clustered in a way that feels like name-checking rather than engagement?

### 4. Overclaim Check

Run another pass for phrasing that silently outruns the support on the page.

Known pressure points:

- Franklin and Robertson
- effective OSR
- claims about what Rosas already establishes
- any suggestion that closure simply settles rainforest admission without remainder

### 5. Style and House-Rule Check

Review the new text against project style.

Questions:

- Is the tone measured and philosophically precise?
- Have any necessary qualifications been dropped?
- Are there any new terms that create unnecessary conceptual overhead?
- Has any new phrasing slipped into manifesto or promotional language?
