**Ontic Structural Realism (OSR)** emerged in the late 1990s/2000s, primarily through James Ladyman's 1998 paper "What is Structural Realism?" and his later work with Steven French, especially their book *Every Thing Must Go: Metaphysics Naturalized* (2007).

**The problem it's responding to:**

Traditional scientific realism says: our best theories are (approximately) true, and the entities they posit (electrons, fields, etc.) really exist. But there's an embarrassing historical pattern — theories get replaced, and their ontologies get abandoned. Phlogiston, caloric, the ether, absolute space. Why think *our* current entities will fare better?

**The structural realist move:**

What *survives* theory change isn't the entities — it's the *mathematical/relational structure*. Fresnel's equations for light survived into Maxwell's theory, even though the ontology (elastic ether → electromagnetic field) was completely swapped out. The structure was preserved; the "stuff" wasn't.

**Epistemic vs. Ontic:**

- **Epistemic Structural Realism** (Worrall): We can only *know* the structure. There might be underlying objects, but we have no epistemic access to them.

- **Ontic Structural Realism** (Ladyman, French): There *are no* underlying objects. Structure is all there is. Relations without relata. The world doesn't have structure — it *is* structure.

**Why go ontic?**

Ladyman and French argue that modern physics pushes us here:

1. **Quantum mechanics** — particles lose identity. Two electrons aren't "this one" and "that one." They're permutation-invariant. The "individual object" concept breaks down.

2. **Quantum field theory** — particles are excitations of fields. The field (a structure) is primary; "particles" are derivative.

3. **General relativity** — spacetime points have no identity independent of the metric structure. No background stage on which things sit.

If physics keeps undermining the notion of individual objects with intrinsic properties, maybe we should stop believing in them.

**The radical claim:**

Relations don't need relata. Structure is ontologically primitive. This sounds paradoxical — how can there be relations without things being related? Ladyman and French bite the bullet: our intuition that relations require prior objects is just folk metaphysics that modern physics has obsoleted.
