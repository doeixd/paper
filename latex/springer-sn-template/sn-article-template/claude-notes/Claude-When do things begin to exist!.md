That's one of the deepest questions in metaphysics. There are a few major perspectives:

**Gradual emergence** — Many things don't have a sharp moment of beginning. A heap of sand forms grain by grain; an organism develops continuously from fertilization onward. The "beginning" is somewhat arbitrary, a line we draw for practical reasons.

**Coming into arrangement** — Some philosophers argue things begin to exist when their parts are arranged in the right way. A chair exists once wood is assembled, not before. On this view, existence is tied to structure and function.

**Absolute creation** — In theology and some cosmological thinking, things begin to exist when they're brought into being from nothing (*ex nihilo*). This is central to arguments like the Kalām cosmological argument: "Everything that begins to exist has a cause."

**They don't, really** — Some views (certain readings of Buddhism, or mereological nihilism) hold that composite objects never truly "begin to exist." Only fundamental particles (or no objects at all) really exist; everything else is a useful fiction.

The question gets especially tricky with abstract objects — did the number 7 begin to exist? What about a law of physics? These edge cases reveal how much the answer depends on what you think "existing" and "things" mean in the first place.
