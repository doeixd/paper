\title[Real Patterns Need Closure]{Real Patterns Need Closure: Transition Autonomy as a Dynamical Criterion for Macro-Objecthood}

\author*[1]{\fnm{Patrick} \sur{Glenn}}\email{pwmglenn@outlook.com}

\affil*[1]{\orgname{Independent Researcher}, \orgaddress{\city{East Providence}, \country{USA}}}

\abstract{Dennett's real-pattern realism links ontology to compression and prediction, but compression alone is too permissive: contrived codings and dynamically idle aggregates can satisfy it. This paper argues that pattern realism needs an explicit closure condition. A candidate macro-object qualifies when, for a fixed regime, horizon, and admissible intervention class, its macrostate is sufficient for its macro-transitions, so that within-class micro-differences make no difference to what happens next at the macro level. Closure is a fact about the system; the regime, horizon, and intervention class specify which modal claim is being made and are identified from the system's physical features. In exact Markov settings, strong lumpability provides a benchmark realization of the condition. In non-ideal settings, closure is graded through leakiness and convergent diagnostics under fixed constraints. The resulting realism excludes high-leak composites, supports graded verdicts in borderline cases, and remains compatible with microphysical completeness. Tying commitment strength to robustness under anti-gerrymandering tests gives selective realism a principled basis. The criterion addresses whether an autonomous macro-level unit exists, and it leaves questions of kind, function, and individuation to complementary accounts. The paper contributes a philosophical criterion and an audit protocol for applying it, and leaves domain-specific estimator engineering to downstream methodological work.}

\keywords{real patterns, closure, macro-objecthood, strong lumpability, coarse-graining, pattern realism}
