\title[Panpsychism and First-Person Warrant]{Overdrawing on Experience: Panpsychism and the Limits of First-Person Warrant}

\abstract{Panpsychism often draws its force from an epistemic asymmetry. Physical science seems to describe matter in structural, relational, dispositional, or dynamic terms, while consciousness is known directly from the first-person point of view. That appeal is genuine. Its reach is limited. First-person experience establishes consciousness as a datum and shows that the experiences with which we are acquainted have phenomenal character. It does not disclose bare metaphysical subjectivity, micro-experience, proto-experience, cosmic subjectivity, hidden phenomenal fields, or relations of phenomenal inheritance. Those further posits require theoretical support. Calling them phenomenal or proto-phenomenal does not give them the authority of acquaintance. The argument enters debates over introspection and the revelation thesis at a precise point: first-person acquaintance supports the datum of consciousness, not a hidden phenomenal ontology. It also reframes the emergence pressure. Once organization explains much of the structure of ordinary consciousness, a hidden phenomenal base must still show what explanatory work it performs. Panpsychism remains available as metaphysics. Its hidden phenomenal posits, however, must earn their support by argument instead of borrowing the immediacy of the first-person datum.}
