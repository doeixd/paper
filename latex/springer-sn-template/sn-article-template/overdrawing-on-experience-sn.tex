\title[Panpsychism and First-Person Warrant]{Overdrawing on Experience: Panpsychism and the Limits of First-Person Warrant}

\abstract{Panpsychism often draws its force from an epistemic asymmetry. Physical science seems to describe matter in structural, relational, dispositional, or dynamic terms, while consciousness is known directly from the first-person point of view. This paper argues that the appeal is genuine but limited. The issue is not whether first-person experience alone proves panpsychism, but whether first-person warrant can transfer to the hidden posits panpsychism introduces. First-person experience establishes consciousness as a datum and shows that the experiences with which we are acquainted have phenomenal character. It does not, by itself, disclose bare metaphysical subjectivity, micro-experience, proto-experience, cosmic subjectivity, hidden phenomenal fields, or relations of phenomenal inheritance. Those posits may still be defended, but as theoretical posits, on the basis of the explanatory work they do. This also places the argument in debates over introspection and the revelation thesis: first-person acquaintance supports the datum of consciousness, not a hidden phenomenal ontology. Rather than derive consciousness from organization, the paper asks what explanatory work remains for a hidden phenomenal base once organization explains the structure of ordinary consciousness. The conclusion is not that panpsychism is false, but that it forfeits the special authority of first-person evidence when it moves beyond ordinary consciousness into hidden phenomenal ontology.}

\keywords{panpsychism; first-person warrant; introspection; revelation thesis; Russellian monism; knowledge of consciousness; phenomenal consciousness; panprotopsychism; cosmopsychism; explanatory gap}
