\title[Panpsychism and First-Person Warrant]{Overdrawing on Experience: Panpsychism and the Limits of First-Person Warrant}

\abstract{Panpsychism often draws its force from an epistemic asymmetry. Physical science seems to describe matter in structural, relational, dispositional, or dynamic terms, while consciousness is known directly from the first-person point of view. This paper argues that the appeal is genuine but limited. The issue is not whether first-person experience alone proves panpsychism, but whether the warrant supplied by first-person experience can transfer to the hidden posits panpsychism introduces. First-person experience establishes consciousness as a datum and shows that the experiences with which we are acquainted have phenomenal character. It does not, by itself, disclose bare metaphysical subjectivity, micro-experience, proto-experience, cosmic subjectivity, hidden phenomenal fields, or relations of phenomenal inheritance. When panpsychism introduces those further posits, their support has to come from theory rather than from acquaintance itself. This places the argument in debates over introspection and the revelation thesis: first-person acquaintance supports the datum of consciousness, not a hidden phenomenal ontology. It also reframes the emergence pressure. The paper asks what explanatory work remains for a hidden phenomenal base once organization explains the structure of ordinary consciousness. Panpsychism remains available as metaphysics, but it forfeits the special authority of first-person evidence when it moves beyond ordinary consciousness into hidden phenomenal ontology.}

\keywords{panpsychism; first-person warrant; introspection; revelation thesis; Russellian monism; knowledge of consciousness; phenomenal consciousness; panprotopsychism; cosmopsychism; explanatory gap}
