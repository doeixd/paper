## Edit 1: Soften the "only data" impression in the Introduction (around line 5)

**Current text:**
> First-person experience answers the first robustly. It bears on the second more cautiously, since our grasp of phenomenal character may be partial and fallible. It does not by itself answer the third, which asks whether consciousness is fundamental, intrinsic, derivative, organizational, microphysical, or cosmic.

**Rationale:** The user's reviewer worried about lines that sound like "first-person experience is the only data we have." The current text does not say this explicitly, but the triadic structure could be read as implying that first-person evidence is the only game in town. We should add a brief qualification that first-person experience is indispensable but not an all-purpose detector.

**Proposed change:** After the sentence "It does not by itself answer the third...", insert:

> First-person experience is the indispensable datum for consciousness, but it is not an all-purpose metaphysical detector.

This pre-empts the easy objection from neuroscience, behavior, meditation reports, and theoretical inference.

**Rationale:** The user's external reviewer suggested formalizing this into a named principle, but the user has instructed us to avoid introducing new named concepts. This keeps the same label (lowercase, already present in the paper) but makes the content crisper and harder to miss.

## Edit 4: Make the abductive concession the argumentative center (Section 2, around lines 23--26)

**Current text:**
> The better argument is abductive: consciousness is known directly, physics gives only structure, radical emergence looks suspect, and panpsychism is therefore offered as the best metaphysical hypothesis. I grant that this is the stronger version of the view. The warrant problem remains. Once the argument is abductive, the warrant has changed kind. The abduction may begin from the first-person datum, but it cannot simply retain the datum's special authority for the hidden ontology it introduces... At that point panpsychism may remain a legitimate metaphysical theory while forfeiting the special authority of first-person evidence. Its micro-, proto-, and cosmic-phenomenal posits then have to be judged by what they explain and what they cost.

**Rationale:** This is already the right idea, but the user wants it to be the *argumentative center* and bite harder. The language should be sharpened to say explicitly: panpsychism is now a theoretical posit like its rivals, and must be judged by explanatory payoff, cost, coherence, and comparative advantage.

**Proposed change:** Expand the existing paragraph to make the concession bite harder. After "...the warrant has changed kind.", insert:

> At that point panpsychism is no longer trading on direct first-person authority. It is a theoretical posit, like its rivals. Its hidden phenomenal ontology must now be judged by explanatory payoff, cost, coherence, and comparative advantage.

Then keep the rest of the existing paragraph (about the fittingness assumption, etc.). This makes the point unmistakable and hard for a reviewer to miss.
