# Attractor Exercise for Targeted Reviewer Edits

Date: 2026-05-20

Purpose: draft the next manuscript edits before applying them, using the AI Attractor Editing Method and `pan/AI smells.txt`. No manuscript prose was changed during this exercise.

## Smells to avoid in this pass

- Correction rhythm: "not X, but Y," "the point is not," "the issue is not."
- Sentence stacking: many short declarative sentences in a row, each restating the frame.
- Announcement before claim: "this section will show," "the question is," "the result is."
- Motto closers: tidy final lines that sound too polished.
- Repeated caveat shape: "Panpsychism may still be true, but..."
- Over-signposting: explaining how the reader should understand the paragraph rather than just making the distinction.

## Target 1: Target Precision

### Existing need

The manuscript needs to say who is under discussion without accusing careful panpsychists of a crude inference from "I am conscious" to "panpsychism is true."

### Candidate cluster

1. The paper's target is not a crude inference from ordinary experience to panpsychism. It is the subtler use of first-person authority to make hidden phenomenal ontology look less theoretical than it is.

Smell: "not X, it is Y" correction rhythm. Accurate, but very model-like.

2. Strawson, Goff, and Ramm bring the pressure into view in different ways. Strawson treats experience as a basic given for realistic metaphysics; Goff's Revelation thesis gives introspection a role in the anti-physicalist case; Ramm uses phenomenological reflection to motivate an idealist-panpsychist picture. None of these moves is a simple inference from pain to particle minds. The concern is narrower: in each case, first-person authority helps support a metaphysical role that acquaintance itself does not disclose.

Smell: "The concern is narrower." Useful but formulaic. Also a little stacked.

3. The pressure is easiest to see in three places. Strawson's "fundamental given natural fact" makes experience an obligatory starting point for realistic theory. Goff's Revelation thesis lets introspection carry weight in the move from phenomenal character to anti-physicalist metaphysics. Ramm's first-person route uses phenomenological reflection to motivate an idealist-panpsychist view. These are not careless arguments. The question is how much of their force comes from acquaintance itself, and how much comes from further metaphysical theory.

Smell: "The question is." Also stacked, but closer.

4. Strawson, Goff, and Ramm are useful because they do not give the paper an easy target. Strawson makes experience a given that realistic metaphysics must accommodate. Goff gives Revelation a role in his case against physicalism and in the wider Russellian setting. Ramm uses first-person phenomenological reflection to motivate an idealist-panpsychist view. These moves differ, but each places pressure on the same boundary: acquaintance with experience can start the argument, while claims about fundamentality, categorical nature, or idealist-panpsychist ontology require further theoretical support.

Smell: mild "same boundary," but less slogan-like. Good candidate.

5. A referee might ask where the transfer happens. The answer is not a single careless passage. It appears in a family of careful moves: Strawson's use of experience as a fundamental given, Goff's appeal to Revelation in the anti-physicalist case, and Ramm's first-person route to idealist panpsychism. Each move begins from something genuinely first-personal. The added metaphysical role is where the warrant has to change kind.

Smell: "The answer is." Last sentence is good but a little motto-like.

### Selected draft

Strawson, Goff, and Ramm are useful because they do not give the paper an easy target. Strawson makes experience a given that realistic metaphysics must accommodate. Goff gives Revelation a role in his case against physicalism and in the wider Russellian setting. Ramm uses first-person phenomenological reflection to motivate an idealist-panpsychist view. These moves differ, but each places pressure on the same boundary: acquaintance with experience can start the argument, while claims about fundamentality, categorical nature, or idealist-panpsychist ontology require further theoretical support.

### Why this version

It names the relevant writers and the relevant moves without saying "this is the illicit move" in a prosecutorial voice. It also avoids the worst "not X, but Y" pattern. The colon is acceptable here because it introduces a real distinction, not a fake punchline.

## Target 2: Phenomenal Structure and Phenomenal Existence

### Existing need

The paper must grant that panpsychists may use the hidden phenomenal base to explain why there is experience at all. The pressure is about whether the base explains the determinate form of ordinary consciousness once organization is doing much of the structural work.

### Candidate cluster

1. There are two explanatory tasks here: explaining why there is experience at all, and explaining why ordinary experience has the structure it has.

Smell: acceptable, but abstract and a little generic.

2. Organization can explain unity, temporal grain, reportability, and access without explaining why any of it feels like anything. A panpsychist may therefore assign the hidden phenomenal base to phenomenal existence rather than phenomenal structure. That concession should be granted. It leaves a further question: how does the hidden base shape the determinate consciousness we actually undergo?

Smell: "further question." The rhythm is close to AI default but serviceable.

3. The concession about organization has a limited reach. It may explain why ordinary consciousness is unified, temporally ordered, reportable, and affected by sleep or anesthesia. It does not, by itself, explain why there is experience at all. Panpsychists can reserve that role for the hidden phenomenal base. The burden then becomes more specific: the hidden base must explain either phenomenal existence alone, or its contribution to the determinate structure of ordinary consciousness.

Smell: several short stacked sentences. Good content, rhythm too clipped.

4. The organizational concession should be kept in its proper place. Attention, memory, temporal integration, and reportability may help explain why ordinary consciousness has the structure it has. They do not explain, simply by being organizational, why there is anything it is like. Panpsychists can therefore say that the hidden phenomenal base is aimed at phenomenal existence. That reply sharpens rather than removes the burden, since the theory must then say what relation that base has to the organized, bounded, valenced consciousness that first-person experience actually gives.

Smell: "sharpens rather than removes" is a mild correction rhythm, but it is specific and not too tidy.

5. Once organization explains much of the profile of ordinary consciousness, the hidden base has a more exact job description. It may be invoked to avoid brute emergence of experience from the wholly non-phenomenal. It may also be asked to help explain why experience has this unity, temporal grain, valence, and perspectival closure. Those are different tasks, and the argument should not slide between them.

Smell: "job description" may be too informal for the paper. Last sentence is good but slightly signpost-like.

### Selected draft

The organizational concession should be kept in its proper place. Attention, memory, temporal integration, and reportability may help explain why ordinary consciousness has the structure it has. They do not explain, simply by being organizational, why there is anything it is like. Panpsychists can therefore say that the hidden phenomenal base is aimed at phenomenal existence. That reply sharpens rather than removes the burden, since the theory must then say what relation that base has to the organized, bounded, valenced consciousness that first-person experience actually gives.

### Why this version

It grants the panpsychist reply before pressing the burden. It avoids implying that organization solves the hard problem. It also keeps the paragraph connected to the paper's main datum: the consciousness we undergo.

Possible final tweak before manuscript insertion: replace "sharpens rather than removes" if it feels too polished. Alternative:

> That reply leaves a more exact burden: the theory must say what relation that base has to the organized, bounded, valenced consciousness that first-person experience actually gives.

## Target 3: Pure Awareness and Private Acquaintance

### Existing need

The manuscript already says that public use of pure-awareness reports depends on report, memory, and classification. It needs to grant that private acquaintance may be evidence for the subject, then explain why this does not help the public panpsychist argument.

### Candidate cluster

1. A defender may reply that the report is not the evidence; the private acquaintance is. That is fair as far as it goes. Private acquaintance may have first-person force for the subject. Once it is offered as a reason for panpsychism, however, it has to enter public argument through memory, description, interpretation, and classification.

Smell: "fair as far as it goes" is human-ish, but "once X, however" is standard academic rhythm.

2. The private-acquaintance reply should be granted. If a subject undergoes pure awareness, the subject may have whatever first-person warrant that episode provides. The problem begins when the episode is used as public support for panpsychism. At that point the argument depends on memory, description, interpretation, and classification.

Smell: sentence stacking. "The problem begins" may sound too dramatic.

3. A panpsychist or phenomenologist can insist that the report is only a later trace. The evidence itself is the private episode. That reply has force. It also leaves the dilemma in place. If the episode is given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or any other way of seeming, it has phenomenal character. If nothing is given at all, there is no first-person disclosure of experience for the panpsychist to extend.

Smell: strong, but very stacked. Last sentence close to slogan.

4. The private-acquaintance reply is the strongest version of the objection. A report may be only the later public trace of an episode whose evidential force, for the subject, lay in undergoing it. Grant that much. The difficulty is that the panpsychist wants more than private reassurance. The episode is being asked to support a metaphysical claim. For that public use it must be remembered, described, interpreted, and classified. Even before that public step, the old dilemma remains: if the episode was given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or some other way of seeming, it was not characterless subjectivity; if nothing was given, it was not disclosed as experience.

Smell: some stacking, but has a more human rhythm. "private reassurance" may be too dismissive.

5. A sympathetic reader may put the objection differently. The report is not supposed to be the evidence. It is a later trace of an episode whose force lay in being undergone. The paper should grant that distinction, since otherwise it would sound as if private experience mattered only after public report. The remaining problem is internal to the appeal itself. If the episode was given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or some other way of seeming, it had phenomenal character. If nothing was given, there is no first-person disclosure of experience for panpsychism to extend.

Smell: "The remaining problem" is a bit signposted, but this is the cleanest and least dismissive.

### Selected draft

A sympathetic reader may put the objection differently. The report is not supposed to be the evidence. It is a later trace of an episode whose force lay in being undergone. The paper should grant that distinction, since otherwise it would sound as if private experience mattered only after public report. The remaining problem is internal to the appeal itself. If the episode was given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or some other way of seeming, it had phenomenal character. If nothing was given, there is no first-person disclosure of experience for panpsychism to extend.

### Why this version

It avoids dismissing private acquaintance. It also avoids treating reportability as a verificationist test for whether the episode occurred. The last two sentences are somewhat stacked, so before manuscript insertion they should probably be folded into the existing paragraph rather than inserted as a freestanding block.

Possible final tweak before manuscript insertion:

> The appeal still divides the same way. If the episode was given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or some other way of seeming, it had phenomenal character. If nothing was given, there is no first-person disclosure of experience for panpsychism to extend.

This keeps the dilemma crisp without a decorative closer.

## Final smell check
