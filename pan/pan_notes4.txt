Here is a critical, academic review of the paper “Overdrawing on Experience: Panpsychism and the Limits of First-Person Warrant.”
Overview
This paper offers a surgical, epistemic critique of contemporary panpsychism. In recent years, panpsychism has gained immense traction in the philosophy of mind, largely propelled by a rhetorical and epistemic appeal: unlike physicalism, which struggles to explain consciousness from the "outside," panpsychism takes the first-person "inside" view of consciousness seriously.
The author of this paper does not attempt to prove that panpsychism is metaphysically false. Instead, they target the justification for the theory. By introducing the Introspective Warrant Constraint (IWC), the author argues that panpsychists illegitimately take the absolute certainty we have about our own ordinary, macro-level experiences and transfer that certainty to highly theoretical, hidden entities—such as "micro-experience," "cosmic subjectivity," or "proto-phenomenal properties." Ultimately, the paper concludes that once panpsychism posits these hidden entities, it loses its special "first-person privilege" and must compete as a standard abductive theory (inference to the best explanation) alongside physicalism and dualism.
Strengths of the Paper
1. Dialectical Modesty and Precision:
The paper is remarkably disciplined. The author explicitly brackets off arguments about the "explanatory gap" or the "impossibility of radical emergence." By refusing to defend physicalism or illusionism, the author avoids taking on unnecessary theoretical baggage. This makes the core critique—that panpsychists are conflating the datum of consciousness with the ontology of consciousness—incredibly difficult for a panpsychist to deflect.
2. The Dilemma of "Bare Subjectivity":
One of the most potent arguments in the paper is the dismantling of "minimal" or "proto" experience. Panpsychists often strip away human traits (like memory or complex thought) to make particle-consciousness plausible. The author traps the panpsychist in a dilemma: If this "proto-experience" retains phenomenal character, it is not what we introspect. If it lacks all phenomenal character, it is no longer the "consciousness" that serves as the foundation of their argument.
3. Masterful Handling of the "Meditation" Counter-argument:
The author anticipates the strongest objection to their view: reports of "pure awareness" from meditative states (e.g., nirodha samapatti). The author’s rebuttal is brilliant in its simplicity. To be used as philosophical evidence, a meditative state must be remembered and reported. If it is remembered, it possesses temporal grain and a minimal "for-me-ness" (thus, it is structured, not "bare"). If it truly lacks all structure, it cannot be reliably reported. Therefore, "pure awareness" cannot serve as an introspective bridge to cosmic or micro-level panpsychism.
4. Re-framing the Combination Problem:
The author makes a devastating point regarding the "Combination Problem" (how micro-minds combine into macro-minds). If a panpsychist argues that biological organization is what combines micro-experiences into human consciousness, the author rightly asks: What unique work is the micro-experience doing? If organization does the heavy lifting of creating structured human thought, panpsychism owes an explanation as to why that same organization couldn't just produce consciousness out of non-conscious matter (as standard physicalism claims).
Weaknesses and Areas for Pushback
While the paper is highly effective, a defender of panpsychism could push back on a few fronts:
1. Does it attack a straw man? (The Abductive Pivot):
The author acknowledges that careful panpsychists (like Philip Goff) do not claim introspection directly proves conscious electrons; they frame their argument abductively. However, the author insists that panpsychists still implicitly "borrow the authority" of first-person experience to give their theories rhetorical weight. A panpsychist might argue this is an unfair psychological critique of how panpsychism is marketed, rather than a logical critique of how the abductive argument functions. In an abductive framework, taking the first-person datum as a "starting point" for ontological extrapolation is standard metaphysical practice, not an illegitimate "transfer of warrant."
2. The Nature of the "Vacancy" in Physics:
In Section 6, the author addresses Russellian Monism (the view that physics only gives us structure, leaving a "vacancy" for intrinsic nature). The author writes, "At most, consciousness supplies a candidate model for intrinsic nature." A panpsychist could reply that consciousness is not just a candidate model; it is the only concept of intrinsic nature available to human epistemology. Therefore, extending it to the micro-level is not an arbitrary theoretical leap, but a theoretical necessity if we are to avoid positing "unknowable" intrinsic natures (Kant's noumena).
3. Underestimating the "Category Error" of Emergence:
The author challenges panpsychists to explain why organization can't just yield standard physicalist emergence. A panpsychist would reply that standard emergence involves a logical category error (getting experience from non-experience is like getting extension from non-extension). Panpsychism claims that getting macro-experience from micro-experience avoids this specific category error. The author glosses over this defense, treating the combination problem and the physicalist hard problem as equally burdensome structural issues, which a panpsychist would strongly deny.

The danger is not that the point is too obvious. The danger is that a referee might say:

“Careful panpsychists already know first-person experience alone does not prove panpsychism.”

So the paper needs to keep emphasizing that this is not the objection. The objection is subtler:

Even if panpsychism is officially abductive, its dialectical appeal often depends on letting the first-person datum carry more metaphysical weight than it can bear. “there is an underexamined shift in warrant from acquaintance to theory.”

You are about warrant transfer.

Lin argues that revelation cannot favor panpsychism over physicalism. My argument is not primarily comparative in that sense. I argue that the first-person datum cannot transfer its special warrant to hidden phenomenal ontology at all, whether or not physicalism faces parallel difficulties.
