## Target 1: Goff Dialectical Force

Original:

> In Goff's case, the first-person datum does dialectical work. Goff says that properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it opens us to panpsychist views that otherwise look unmotivated by third-person science \citep[9--10]{goff2017cfr}.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. In Goff's case, the first-person datum is part of the engine of the argument. He says that properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it opens us to panpsychist views that third-person science alone would leave unmotivated.
2. Goff is explicit that the first-person datum changes the dialectical situation. Properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is, he says, ``epistemically transformative,'' because it makes panpsychist views look available despite their lack of ordinary third-person support.
3. Goff does not leave the first-person datum in the background. Properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is, on his view, ``epistemically transformative,'' because it opens space for panpsychist views that third-person observation and experiment do not themselves support.
4. For Goff, consciousness as a datum is supposed to shift what counts as a serious option. Properly internalizing it is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it makes room for panpsychist views that third-person science does not motivate on its own.
5. Goff gives the first-person datum real weight. Properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is ``epistemically transformative,'' since it makes panpsychist views look live even though third-person science gives no direct reason to posit conscious electrons.
6. Goff's use of the datum is stronger than a background reminder that consciousness exists. Properly internalizing it is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it changes which theories of fundamental reality become serious candidates.
7. In Goff's argument, first-person consciousness is not just something a theory must accommodate. Properly internalizing it is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it changes which theories seem worth taking seriously.
8. Goff says the datum does more than set a constraint. Properly internalizing it is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it opens the door to panpsychist accounts that third-person observation would not otherwise recommend.
9. Goff's language is useful because it shows where the dialectical force lies. Consciousness as a datum becomes ``epistemically transformative,'' making panpsychist views serious candidates despite their lack of third-person motivation.
10. The first-person datum is doing work in Goff's setup. It is ``epistemically transformative'' because it makes peculiar theories of fundamental reality, including panpsychism, available as responses to consciousness.

Attractor:

- Repeated "does work" language.
- Repeated "opens/makes room/makes available" structure.
- Correction rhythm: "not merely background, but..." or "not just something...".
- The sentence keeps announcing its argumentative role instead of just saying what Goff does.
- "Dialectical work" is precise but slightly seminar-room generic.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the quote because it is the textual anchor requested.
- Avoid "does work" and "not just."
- Let Goff's own phrase carry the force, then explain in a concrete clause what it opens: peculiar theories, including panpsychism, despite lack of third-person support.

Preferred revision:

> Goff's own language is stronger: properly internalizing consciousness as a datum is ``epistemically transformative,'' because it makes room for panpsychist views that third-person observation and experiment do not themselves support \citep[9--10]{goff2017cfr}.

## Target 2: Pure-Awareness Private/Public Reply

Original:

> Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made; the public question is what happens when that force is offered to others.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made; the issue for the paper is how that force becomes a reason for others.
2. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made. The difficulty begins when that private force is asked to function as public evidence.
3. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made. Once it is offered to others, it has to pass through memory, description, and interpretation.
4. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may settle something for the subject before any report is made. It becomes public evidence only through memory, description, and interpretation.
5. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have its own force for the subject. The question here is narrower: what can it support once it enters public argument?
6. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may be compelling for the subject before any report is made. A paper cannot use it in that form. It reaches the discussion through memory, description, and interpretation.
7. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person authority for the subject. Offered to others, it no longer arrives as acquaintance; it arrives as report.
8. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made. For everyone else, the episode is available only through what is remembered and said.
9. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject. In public argument, though, the evidence has already become report, memory, and interpretation.
10. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may carry first-person force before it is described. Philosophical use of the episode starts later, with memory, description, and classification.

Attractor:

- "The question/issue/difficulty is..." signposting.
- Repeated "if it occurs, may have first-person force" phrase is accurate but stiff.
- "Public question" sounds like a policy phrase rather than philosophy prose.
- Several variants overuse the same public/private contrast in a neat binary.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the concession to private force.
- Let the next sentence do the report/memory/interpretation work, avoiding an extra announcement sentence.
- Use "for everyone else" or "offered to others" because it is plainer and less institutional than "public question."

Preferred revision:

> Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may be compelling for the subject before any report is made. For everyone else, the episode is available only through what is remembered, described, and classified.

Need to check the following sentence for duplication if this revision is used, because it already says the episode enters argument through memory, description, interpretation, and classification.
