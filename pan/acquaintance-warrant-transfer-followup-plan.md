# Follow-Up Paper Plan: Acquaintance and Warrant Transfer

## Working Title

**Acquaintance and Warrant Transfer: First-Person Evidence and Theoretical Posits in Philosophy of Mind**

## Other Title Options

1. **The Limits of Acquaintance**
2. **First-Person Warrant and Hidden Ontology**
3. **From Acquaintance to Theory**
4. **Revelation, Acquaintance, and Theoretical Warrant**
5. **What First-Person Evidence Cannot Carry**

## Core Thesis

First-person acquaintance has real epistemic force, but its force is bounded. It can warrant the existence of conscious experience and disclose something about its phenomenal character. It does not automatically warrant the hidden structures, grounds, extensions, or metaphysical locations that philosophers introduce to explain conscious experience. Once a theory moves from what acquaintance gives to theoretical posits about what grounds, constitutes, extends, or underlies it, the warrant changes kind. The support must then come from explanatory, unificatory, or abductive work rather than from the authority of acquaintance itself.

## Short Thesis Statement

First-person acquaintance gives philosophy of mind a datum, not a transferable license for hidden ontology.

## One-Paragraph Abstract Draft

Philosophical theories of consciousness often appeal to the special epistemic status of first-person experience. Pain, color experience, affective tone, and temporal flow seem known from within rather than posited as theoretical entities. This paper argues that such acquaintance has real but limited warrant. It can establish consciousness as a datum and disclose something about the character of ordinary conscious life. It does not, by itself, warrant theoretical posits introduced to explain, ground, extend, or metaphysically locate consciousness. The relevant mistake is warrant transfer: treating the authority of acquaintance with experience as if it also attached to hidden posits about micro-experience, pure subjectivity, phenomenal grounds, categorical bases, cosmic consciousness, or neural/functional structures. Revelation-style arguments make this transfer especially tempting, but even a strong revelation thesis would reveal only what is given in acquaintance, not the theoretical machinery introduced to explain it. The result is a methodological constraint for philosophy of mind: once a view moves beyond first-person acquaintance, its warrant becomes theoretical and must be assessed by ordinary abductive standards.

## Central Contribution

The paper would not primarily be a critique of panpsychism, idealism, illusionism, or physicalism. It would be a methodological paper about a recurring move in philosophy of mind:

1. Begin with first-person acquaintance as unusually secure.
2. Identify a theoretical or metaphysical problem that acquaintance seems to illuminate.
3. Introduce hidden posits to explain, ground, extend, or locate the datum.
4. Allow those posits to inherit some of the authority of the original acquaintance.

The paper argues that step 4 is where the mistake occurs. The posit may be legitimate. The inference may even be strong. But its warrant is no longer first-person warrant. It is ordinary theoretical warrant.

## Key Distinctions

1. **Datum vs. posit**

   The datum is what acquaintance presents: ordinary conscious experience, however complex, vague, or hard to articulate. A posit is what a theory introduces to explain, ground, extend, or metaphysically locate that datum.

2. **Acquaintance warrant vs. theoretical warrant**

   Acquaintance warrant attaches to what is directly given in experience. Theoretical warrant attaches to entities, structures, or relations introduced because they explain, unify, predict, or otherwise make sense of what is given.

3. **Revealing character vs. revealing metaphysical role**

   Even if acquaintance reveals something about the nature of an experience, it does not follow that it reveals whether that experience is fundamental, derivative, physical, non-physical, cosmic, functional, or categorical.

4. **Starting from experience vs. spending experience**

   A theory may rightly start from first-person experience. The error is to spend the authority of that datum on entities or structures that acquaintance itself does not disclose.

## Proposed Structure

### 1. Introduction: The Authority of the First Person

Open with the intuitive contrast between pain and theoretical entities. Pain is undergone. Electrons, genes, neural mechanisms, and categorical bases are posited. This contrast explains why first-person acquaintance continues to do major work in philosophy of mind.

Main aim:

- Grant the importance of first-person evidence.
- Reject crude skepticism about consciousness.
- Introduce the problem of warrant transfer.

Core claim:

> First-person acquaintance is not being denied. Its reach is being limited.

### 2. The Warrant-Transfer Constraint

State the central methodological constraint:

> A theory may claim first-person warrant only for what first-person acquaintance actually discloses. Anything introduced beyond that must be warranted as theory.

Clarify what the constraint does not say:

- It does not say that only directly experienced entities exist.
- It does not ban theoretical posits.
- It does not make introspection infallible.
- It does not require physicalism.
- It does not solve the hard problem.

Clarify what it does say:

- A posit does not inherit first-person authority merely by being described as phenomenal, experiential, subjective, or consciousness-involving.

### 3. Acquaintance and Revelation

Engage the revelation thesis more directly than the panpsychism paper does. The central question is not only whether revelation is true, but what revelation would reveal if it were true.

Argument:

1. Suppose acquaintance reveals something about the character of conscious experience.
2. That still leaves open the metaphysical role of that experience.
3. Knowing what an experience is like does not by itself show whether it is fundamental, derivative, physical, non-physical, functional, cosmic, or categorical.
4. Revelation, at most, reveals the datum. It does not reveal the hidden theory built around it.

Possible literature anchors:

- Goff on revelation and consciousness.
- Roelofs on medium-strength revelation.
- Lin on revelation's failure to favor panpsychism over physicalism.
- Loar, Papineau, Balog, and Carruthers on phenomenal concepts as an alternative diagnosis of the epistemic asymmetry.

### 4. The Shape of the Mistake

This section would identify the recurring pattern abstractly.

Candidate formula:

1. $E$ is given in acquaintance.
2. $T$ is introduced to explain, ground, extend, or locate $E$.
3. Because $T$ is described in terms related to $E$, $T$ is treated as if it inherits the warrant of $E$.
4. But acquaintance with $E$ is not acquaintance with $T$.

The paper can avoid heavy formalism by treating this as a schematic diagnosis, not a technical model.

Examples of $T$:

- micro-experiences;
- proto-phenomenal properties;
- pure subjectivity;
- cosmic consciousness;
- categorical physical bases;
- neural identity claims;
- functional roles;
- eliminativist error theories.

The final two examples matter because the paper should not seem aimed only at anti-physicalist views. Physicalist and deflationary views can also overdraw on a datum or on introspective fallibility.

### 5. Panpsychism as the Central Case

Use the existing panpsychism paper as the cleanest case study.

Panpsychism often begins from:

> Consciousness is known directly.

It then moves toward:

> Consciousness, proto-consciousness, phenomenal character, or subjectivity belongs to the fundamental nature of reality.

The methodological point:

> That move may be defensible, but not as a direct inheritance from first-person acquaintance.

This section can summarize the current paper's argument without repeating all of it:

- first-person experience discloses ordinary consciousness;
- it does not disclose micro-experience;
- it does not disclose proto-experience;
- it does not disclose cosmic subjectivity;
- it does not disclose phenomenal bonding or inheritance relations;
- it does not show that categorical bases are phenomenal.

Payoff:

> Panpsychism is not refuted. It is relocated. Once its hidden ontology is admitted to be theoretical, it must compete as theory.

### 6. Other Case Studies

This section makes the follow-up paper broader than the panpsychism critique.

Possible subsections:

1. **Idealism**

   Acquaintance with experience may establish that experience is real. It does not by itself establish that reality as a whole is mental or mind-dependent.

2. **Russellian monism**

   Structural physics may leave a question about intrinsic or categorical nature. Acquaintance with consciousness does not by itself show that the categorical basis is phenomenal.

3. **Phenomenal concept strategies**

   Phenomenal concepts may explain the felt asymmetry between physical and phenomenal thought. But the existence of such concepts does not automatically settle ontology either. The strategy must explain the epistemic phenomenon without silently converting it into a metaphysical conclusion.

4. **Illusionism and introspective skepticism**

   Introspective fallibility may undermine some claims about experience. It does not automatically warrant a global deflationary ontology. Moving from local fallibility to broad metaphysical denial is also a warrant-transfer risk.

5. **Pure-awareness arguments**

   Reports of pure or contentless awareness may support claims about unusual forms of experience. They do not by themselves establish bare metaphysical subjectivity as fundamental.

The point of these examples is not to settle each debate. It is to show that the warrant-transfer constraint is methodologically general.

### 7. Theoretical Posits Are Legitimate

This section prevents the paper from sounding verificationist or anti-theoretical.

Core claims:

- Theoretical posits are normal and often indispensable.
- Philosophy of mind cannot rely only on what is directly given.
- Neuroscience, psychology, physics, and metaphysics all introduce hidden structures.
- The issue is not hiddenness. The issue is inherited warrant.

Useful formulation:

> Theoretical posits are not disqualified by being hidden. They are disqualified only from borrowing a kind of warrant they do not possess.

This section should explain what ordinary theoretical warrant involves:

- explanatory payoff;
- unification;
- integration with neighboring theory;
- avoidance of ad hoc patches;
- ability to make sense of the datum without merely renaming the problem.

### 8. Objections

#### Objection 1: This makes first-person evidence too weak.

Reply:

The argument does not weaken first-person evidence. It protects its proper force. Consciousness remains a datum. The limit concerns what can be inferred from that datum.

#### Objection 2: No one thinks acquaintance alone proves hidden ontology.

Reply:

The paper grants this. Its target is subtler. Even when the explicit argument is abductive, philosophers may still let the rhetoric of acquaintance carry more weight than the abduction earns.

#### Objection 3: Theoretical warrant can include first-person evidence.

Reply:

Yes. First-person evidence can be part of an abductive case. The point is that the warrant of the resulting posit is then abductive, not direct acquaintance warrant.

#### Objection 4: Revelation changes the situation.

Reply:

Only if revelation reaches the relevant posit. Revelation about the character of an experience is not revelation about its metaphysical role, grounds, distribution, or cosmic structure.

#### Objection 5: The distinction between datum and posit is unstable.

Reply:

The distinction is not absolute in every case, but it is stable enough for the cases that matter. Pain is given in a way that micro-experiential categorical bases are not. The constraint applies where that difference is doing argumentative work.

### 9. Conclusion: A Discipline on First-Person Philosophy

Close by emphasizing that the paper is not hostile to first-person philosophy. It is a discipline on how first-person evidence is used.

Possible closing line:

> Acquaintance gives philosophy of mind one of its most important starting points. It does not give every theory built around that starting point the same authority.

This follow-up paper would be more abstract and more ambitious. It would take the methodological structure discovered in the panpsychism case and generalize it.

The follow-up can cite the current paper once published, or can be developed independently if the panpsychism paper is still under review.

## What To Avoid

1. Do not make the paper a general attack on introspection.

   The point is not that introspection is useless. It is that its authority is bounded.

2. Do not make the paper a defense of physicalism.

   The constraint applies to physicalists, illusionists, idealists, and panpsychists alike.

3. Do not over-formalize the argument.

   A light schematic structure may help, but the main contribution is methodological clarity, not a formal epistemology.

4. Do not multiply named principles.

   Use one central label: **the warrant-transfer constraint** or **the acquaintance-warrant constraint**. The first is probably better for the follow-up paper because it is broader than introspection.

5. Do not claim novelty too strongly.

   The paper should say it isolates and generalizes a recurring methodological pressure, not that no one has noticed any part of it.

## Best Candidate Central Label

**The warrant-transfer constraint**

Definition:

> A theory may use acquaintance as evidence for what acquaintance discloses. It may not treat theoretical posits introduced beyond acquaintance as if they inherit acquaintance's direct warrant.

This label is better than "introspective warrant constraint" for the higher-level paper because it covers more than introspection narrowly construed. It also applies to revelation, acquaintance, phenomenological reflection, introspective skepticism, and theoretical positing.
