Best current title:

**From Acquaintance to Ontology: Why First-Person Warrant Does Not Automatically Transfer**

This is more immediately intelligible and searchable than the shorter "Acquaintance and Warrant Transfer." The shorter phrase can still be used as the article's central label.

## Core Thesis

First-person acquaintance has real epistemic force, but its force is bounded. It can warrant the existence of conscious experience and disclose something about its phenomenal character. It does not automatically warrant the hidden structures, grounds, extensions, or metaphysical locations that philosophers introduce to explain conscious experience. Once a theory moves from what acquaintance gives to theoretical posits about what grounds, constitutes, extends, or underlies it, the warrant changes kind. The support must then come from explanatory, unificatory, or abductive work rather than from the authority of acquaintance itself.

## Short Thesis Statement

First-person acquaintance gives philosophy of mind a datum, not a transferable license for hidden ontology.

## Publishable Principle

**No Automatic Acquaintance-Transfer:**

> Acquaintance with a conscious experience gives noninferential warrant for the occurrence and phenomenal character of that experience. But absent further argument, it does not give noninferential warrant for theoretical posits concerning the grounds, constituents, essence, location, causal role, metaphysical status, or distribution of that experience.

This formulation is stronger than the vague claim that acquaintance has "limits," but weaker than the risky claim that warrant never transfers. The point is that transfer is not automatic and not noninferential. Acquaintance may become part of a broader argument, but once it does, the warrant has changed kind.

## One-Paragraph Abstract Draft

Philosophical theories of consciousness often appeal to the special epistemic status of first-person experience. Pain, color experience, affective tone, and temporal flow seem known from within rather than posited as theoretical entities. This paper argues that such acquaintance has real but limited warrant. It can establish consciousness as a datum and disclose something about the character of ordinary conscious life. It does not, by itself, warrant theoretical posits introduced to explain, ground, extend, or metaphysically locate consciousness. The relevant mistake is warrant transfer: treating the authority of acquaintance with experience as if it also attached to hidden posits about micro-experience, pure subjectivity, phenomenal grounds, categorical bases, cosmic consciousness, or neural/functional structures. Revelation-style arguments make this transfer especially tempting, but even a strong revelation thesis would reveal only what is given in acquaintance, not the theoretical machinery introduced to explain it. The result is a methodological constraint for philosophy of mind: once a view moves beyond first-person acquaintance, its warrant becomes theoretical and must be assessed by ordinary abductive standards.

## Refined Abstract Direction

Possible tighter version:

It seems to give direct warrant for the reality and character of conscious experience, unlike the warrant we have for electrons, genes, neural mechanisms, or categorical bases. This paper argues that the authority of acquaintance is real but bounded. It warrants what acquaintance discloses; it does not automatically transfer to the theoretical posits introduced to explain, ground, extend, or metaphysically locate what acquaintance discloses. Revelation-style arguments make this transfer especially tempting, since they treat experience as revealing its own nature. But even if acquaintance reveals something about the nature of an experience, it does not by itself reveal that experience's metaphysical role, grounds, distribution, or place in fundamental reality. The result is a methodological constraint: once a theory moves beyond acquaintance into hidden ontology, its warrant becomes theoretical and must be assessed by ordinary abductive standards.

## Central Contribution

The paper would not primarily be a critique of panpsychism, idealism, illusionism, or physicalism. It would be a methodological paper about a recurring move in philosophy of mind:

1. Begin with first-person acquaintance as unusually secure.
2. Identify a theoretical or metaphysical problem that acquaintance seems to illuminate.
3. Introduce hidden posits to explain, ground, extend, or locate the datum.
4. Allow those posits to inherit some of the authority of the original acquaintance.

The paper argues that step 4 is where the mistake occurs. The posit may be legitimate. The inference may even be strong. But its warrant is no longer first-person warrant. It is ordinary theoretical warrant.

## Strongest Version of the Contribution

The paper should not present itself as discovering that introspection is limited. That is too broad and too familiar. The contribution is more specific:

> The paper separates two questions that are often allowed to run together: what first-person acquaintance warrants directly, and what theoretical posits may be warranted by an explanatory argument that begins from acquaintance.

This lets the paper avoid three bad readings:

- It is not skeptical about consciousness.
- It is not hostile to theoretical posits.
- It is not a disguised defense of physicalism.

The novelty claim should be modest:

> The paper isolates a recurring warrant-transfer pattern in philosophy of mind and gives it a general methodological formulation.

That is stronger and safer than saying the paper introduces a wholly new problem.

Another way to put the originality claim:

> The individual ingredients are familiar: acquaintance, revelation, phenomenal concepts, introspective authority, and theoretical posits all have substantial literatures. The paper's contribution is to diagnose a recurring epistemic move across those literatures: treating acquaintance with experience as if it confers warrant on hidden theoretical machinery introduced to explain experience.

This is the safest originality frame. It avoids overstating novelty while still giving the article a clear reason to exist.

## Key Distinctions

1. **Datum vs. posit**

   The datum is what acquaintance presents: ordinary conscious experience, however complex, vague, or hard to articulate. A posit is what a theory introduces to explain, ground, extend, or metaphysically locate that datum.

2. **Acquaintance warrant vs. theoretical warrant**

   Acquaintance warrant attaches to what is directly given in experience. Theoretical warrant attaches to entities, structures, or relations introduced because they explain, unify, predict, or otherwise make sense of what is given.

3. **Revealing character vs. revealing metaphysical role**

   Even if acquaintance reveals something about the nature of an experience, it does not follow that it reveals whether that experience is fundamental, derivative, physical, non-physical, cosmic, functional, or categorical.

4. **Starting from experience vs. spending experience**

   A theory may rightly start from first-person experience. The error is to spend the authority of that datum on entities or structures that acquaintance itself does not disclose.

5. **Epistemic asymmetry vs. ontological asymmetry**

   First-person access may be epistemically distinctive without showing that consciousness is ontologically distinctive in the way a theory claims. This distinction is especially important for panpsychism, idealism, and anti-physicalist revelation arguments.

6. **What-it-is-like knowledge vs. ground knowledge**

   Knowing what an experience is like is not the same as knowing what grounds it. A theory may need to explain why the two are connected, but acquaintance alone does not supply the connection.

7. **Acquaintance with an instance vs. knowledge of a kind**

   Acquaintance may put a subject in contact with a particular pain or color experience. It does not automatically give full knowledge of the kind pain, the kind phenomenal property, or the metaphysical category experience belongs to.

8. **Constitution vs. disclosure**

   A hidden structure may constitute what is disclosed without itself being disclosed. This distinction matters for Roelofs-style replies in the panpsychism case and for functionalist or neural identity claims in physicalist cases.

## Current Conversation Lessons To Preserve

These points emerged from the current panpsychism revision process and should guide the follow-up:

1. The strongest target is not "panpsychism is bad" or "introspection is unreliable."

   The target is warrant transfer: first-person warrant cannot move to hidden theoretical posits without becoming ordinary abductive warrant.

2. The paper should grant the careful opponent early.

   Say plainly that no serious philosopher need think acquaintance alone proves a full metaphysics. The question is what happens to the warrant once the argument becomes abductive.

3. The phrase "changed kind" is useful.

   Once a view moves from acquaintance to theory, the warrant has changed kind. This is simple, accurate, and avoids sounding like a slogan.

4. The paper should treat the constraint as a success condition, not a refutation machine.

   If a theory accepts the constraint and defends its posits by explanatory work, that is the desired result.

5. The paper should use panpsychism as the cleanest case, not as the only target.

   Otherwise it risks looking like the same paper at a higher level of abstraction.

6. Avoid over-structured prose in the finished article.

   The plan can be structured. The paper should move by simple distinctions, concessions, and payoffs.

## Proposed Structure

The recommended structure is now more general than the panpsychism paper. Panpsychism should be one application, not the organizing identity of the article.

### 1. Introduction: The Authority of the First Person

Open with the intuitive contrast between pain and theoretical entities. Pain is undergone. Electrons, genes, neural mechanisms, and categorical bases are posited. This contrast explains why first-person acquaintance continues to do major work in philosophy of mind.

Main aim:

- Grant the importance of first-person evidence.
- Reject crude skepticism about consciousness.
- Introduce the problem of warrant transfer.

Core claim:

> First-person acquaintance is not being denied. Its reach is being limited.

### 2. The Warrant-Transfer Constraint

State the central methodological constraint:

> A theory may claim noninferential first-person warrant only for what first-person acquaintance actually discloses. Anything introduced beyond that may still be warranted, but only through further argument.

Clarify what the constraint does not say:

- It does not say that only directly experienced entities exist.
- It does not ban theoretical posits.
- It does not make introspection infallible.
- It does not require physicalism.
- It does not solve the hard problem.

Clarify what it does say:

- A posit does not inherit first-person authority merely by being described as phenomenal, experiential, subjective, or consciousness-involving.
- Acquaintance can be evidence in a broader case, but then the warrant is inferential, theoretical, or abductive.

### 3. Acquaintance and Revelation

Engage the revelation thesis more directly than the panpsychism paper does. The central question is not only whether revelation is true, but what revelation would reveal if it were true.

Argument:

1. Suppose acquaintance reveals something about the character of conscious experience.
2. That still leaves open the metaphysical role of that experience.
3. Knowing what an experience is like does not by itself show whether it is fundamental, derivative, physical, non-physical, functional, cosmic, or categorical.
4. Revelation, at most, reveals the datum. It does not reveal the hidden theory built around it.

This section should treat revelation as the strongest attempted transfer principle. If any view could make acquaintance carry metaphysical conclusions, revelation or phenomenal transparency would be the candidate. That makes it the natural pressure test for the paper.

Possible literature anchors:

- Goff on revelation and consciousness.
- Roelofs on medium-strength revelation.
- Lin on revelation's failure to favor panpsychism over physicalism.
- Loar, Papineau, Balog, and Carruthers on phenomenal concepts as an alternative diagnosis of the epistemic asymmetry.

### 4. The Shape of the Mistake

This section would identify the recurring pattern abstractly.

Candidate formula:

1. $E$ is given in acquaintance.
2. $T$ is introduced to explain, ground, extend, or locate $E$.
3. Because $T$ is described in terms related to $E$, $T$ is treated as if it inherits the warrant of $E$.
4. But acquaintance with $E$ is not acquaintance with $T$.

The paper can avoid heavy formalism by treating this as a schematic diagnosis, not a technical model.

Examples of $T$:

- micro-experiences;
- proto-phenomenal properties;
- pure subjectivity;
- cosmic consciousness;
- categorical physical bases;
- neural identity claims;
- functional roles;
- eliminativist error theories.

The final two examples matter because the paper should not seem aimed only at anti-physicalist views. Physicalist and deflationary views can also overdraw on a datum or on introspective fallibility.

### 5. Panpsychism as the Central Case

Use the existing panpsychism paper as the cleanest case study.

Panpsychism often begins from:

> Consciousness is known directly.

It then moves toward:

> Consciousness, proto-consciousness, phenomenal character, or subjectivity belongs to the fundamental nature of reality.

The methodological point:

> That move may be defensible, but not as a direct inheritance from first-person acquaintance.

This section can summarize the current paper's argument without repeating all of it:

- first-person experience discloses ordinary consciousness;
- it does not disclose micro-experience;
- it does not disclose proto-experience;
- it does not disclose cosmic subjectivity;
- it does not disclose phenomenal bonding or inheritance relations;
- it does not show that categorical bases are phenomenal.

Payoff:

> Panpsychism is not refuted. It is relocated. Once its hidden ontology is admitted to be theoretical, it must compete as theory.

Make sure this section says explicitly:

> This is not an objection to panpsychism's being abductive. It is an objection to treating an abductive posit as if it retained noninferential first-person warrant.

### 6. Other Case Studies

This section makes the follow-up paper broader than the panpsychism critique.

Possible subsections:

1. **Idealism**

   Acquaintance with experience may establish that experience is real. It does not by itself establish that reality as a whole is mental or mind-dependent.

2. **Russellian monism**

   Structural physics may leave a question about intrinsic or categorical nature. Acquaintance with consciousness does not by itself show that the categorical basis is phenomenal.

3. **Dualism**

   Acquaintance with experience may establish that experience is real and difficult to capture in physical vocabulary. It does not by itself establish a nonphysical substance or property ontology. That conclusion needs its own argument.

4. **Physicalism**

   Acquaintance with experience does not disclose neural realization, functional role, or physical identity either. Those claims are theoretical. This case helps prevent the paper from sounding anti-panpsychist or anti-dualist only.

5. **Phenomenal concept strategies**

   Phenomenal concepts may explain the felt asymmetry between physical and phenomenal thought. But the existence of such concepts does not automatically settle ontology either. The strategy must explain the epistemic phenomenon without silently converting it into a metaphysical conclusion.

6. **Illusionism and introspective skepticism**

   Introspective fallibility may undermine some claims about experience. It does not automatically warrant a global deflationary ontology. Moving from local fallibility to broad metaphysical denial is also a warrant-transfer risk.

7. **Pure-awareness arguments**

   Reports of pure or contentless awareness may support claims about unusual forms of experience. They do not by themselves establish bare metaphysical subjectivity as fundamental.

The point of these examples is not to settle each debate. It is to show that the warrant-transfer constraint is methodologically general.

### 7. Theoretical Posits Are Legitimate

This section prevents the paper from sounding verificationist or anti-theoretical.

Core claims:

- Theoretical posits are normal and often indispensable.
- Philosophy of mind cannot rely only on what is directly given.
- Neuroscience, psychology, physics, and metaphysics all introduce hidden structures.
- The issue is not hiddenness. The issue is inherited warrant.

Useful formulation:

> Theoretical posits are not disqualified by being hidden. They are disqualified only from borrowing a kind of warrant they do not possess.

This section should explain what ordinary theoretical warrant involves:

- explanatory payoff;
- unification;
- integration with neighboring theory;
- avoidance of ad hoc patches;
- ability to make sense of the datum without merely renaming the problem.

### 8. Objections

#### Objection 1: This makes first-person evidence too weak.

Reply:

The argument does not weaken first-person evidence. It protects its proper force. Consciousness remains a datum. The limit concerns what can be inferred from that datum.

#### Objection 2: No one thinks acquaintance alone proves hidden ontology.

Reply:

The paper grants this. Its target is subtler. Even when the explicit argument is abductive, philosophers may still let the rhetoric of acquaintance carry more weight than the abduction earns.

#### Objection 3: Theoretical warrant can include first-person evidence.

Reply:

Yes. First-person evidence can be part of an abductive case. The point is that the warrant of the resulting posit is then abductive, not direct acquaintance warrant.

#### Objection 4: Revelation changes the situation.

Reply:

Only if revelation reaches the relevant posit. Revelation about the character of an experience is not revelation about its metaphysical role, grounds, distribution, or cosmic structure.

#### Objection 5: The distinction between datum and posit is unstable.

Reply:

The distinction is not absolute in every case, but it is stable enough for the cases that matter. Pain is given in a way that micro-experiential categorical bases are not. The constraint applies where that difference is doing argumentative work.

### 9. Conclusion: A Discipline on First-Person Philosophy

Close by emphasizing that the paper is not hostile to first-person philosophy. It is a discipline on how first-person evidence is used.

Possible closing line:

> Acquaintance gives philosophy of mind one of its most important starting points. It does not give every theory built around that starting point the same authority.

Stronger possible final line:

> Acquaintance may be a source of first-person authority, but it is not a metaphysical transmission belt.

Use this only if the surrounding prose is measured enough. It is memorable, but it should not sound like a slogan dropped into an otherwise academic conclusion.

## What To Avoid

1. Do not make the paper a general attack on introspection.

   The point is not that introspection is useless. It is that its authority is bounded.

2. Do not make the paper a defense of physicalism.

   The constraint applies to physicalists, illusionists, idealists, and panpsychists alike.

3. Do not over-formalize the argument.

   A light schematic structure may help, but the main contribution is methodological clarity, not a formal epistemology.

4. Do not multiply named principles.

   Use one central label: **the warrant-transfer constraint** or **the acquaintance-warrant constraint**. The first is probably better for the follow-up paper because it is broader than introspection.

5. Do not claim novelty too strongly.

   The paper should say it isolates and generalizes a recurring methodological pressure, not that no one has noticed any part of it.

6. Do not make "hidden ontology" sound automatically suspicious.

   Hidden posits are normal in science and philosophy. The paper's complaint is not hiddenness. It is misdescribed warrant.

7. Do not conflate acquaintance with introspective judgment.

   Acquaintance may be a relation to experience; introspective judgment is a belief or report formed on that basis. Keeping these apart helps answer Gertler-style acquaintance views and Schwitzgebel-style fallibility worries at the same time.

8. Do not let "theoretical posit" become a term of dismissal.

   The whole point is that theoretical warrant can be strong. Calling a posit theoretical relocates the burden; it does not defeat the view.

## Best Candidate Central Label

**The warrant-transfer constraint**

Definition:

> A theory may use acquaintance as evidence for what acquaintance discloses. It may not treat theoretical posits introduced beyond acquaintance as if they automatically inherit acquaintance's noninferential warrant.

This label is better than "introspective warrant constraint" for the higher-level paper because it covers more than introspection narrowly construed. It also applies to revelation, acquaintance, phenomenological reflection, introspective skepticism, and theoretical positing.

## Referee-Risk Matrix

1. **"This is just Sellars against the given."**

   Response: The paper need not deny the given or adopt Sellars's full critique. It grants first-person acquaintance for the sake of argument and asks what follows from it. That makes the argument narrower and less revisionary than Sellars.

2. **"This is just the phenomenal concept strategy."**

   Response: The paper is not explaining away the epistemic gap. It is distinguishing direct first-person warrant from theoretical warrant. Phenomenal concept strategies are one possible account of why the asymmetry arises, but the warrant-transfer constraint applies whether or not those strategies succeed.

3. **"Acquaintance theorists already know acquaintance is limited."**

   Response: Good. The paper should grant that. Its point is that debates in consciousness often move from that limited authority to broader metaphysical uses without keeping the warrant change explicit.

4. **"Panpsychists argue abductively, so the target is a straw man."**

   Response: The paper should say early that abductive panpsychism is the serious target. The point is that abductive warrant is not first-person warrant. If panpsychism is defended abductively, it should be evaluated by explanatory payoff and theoretical cost.

5. **"The datum/posit distinction is unstable."**

   Response: It is not meant as an absolute metaphysical division. It is a dialectical distinction. It applies when a theorist relies on the special status of a datum to support something that is not itself given.

6. **"The paper is verificationist."**

   Response: Theoretical posits are allowed throughout. The paper should explicitly cite theoretical entities and inference to the best explanation to avoid this reading.

7. **"The paper is biased toward physicalism."**

   Response: Include at least one physicalist or deflationary case. Neural identity claims, functional roles, phenomenal concept strategies, and illusionist inferences can also be assessed under the same constraint.

8. **"The paper collapses into skepticism about introspection."**

   Response: The paper can remain neutral on strong introspective skepticism. Schwitzgebel is a reinforcing caution, not the foundation.

## Argumentative Tests For Each Case Study

For every case study, ask the same five questions:

1. What is the first-person datum?
2. What theoretical posit is introduced?
3. What relation is claimed between the datum and the posit?
4. Is the posit being treated as if it inherits first-person warrant?
5. If not inherited, what explanatory or abductive work is supposed to warrant it?

This test keeps the case studies parallel and prevents the paper from drifting into separate mini-critiques.

> The problem is not that acquaintance can never support theoretical claims. The problem is that acquaintance does not make those claims first-personally warranted merely by being recruited into an explanation of what is acquainted with.

Use examples:

* From “I am acquainted with pain” it does not follow that pain is nonphysical.
* From “I am acquainted with red experience” it does not follow that red experience is physically irreducible.
* From “I am acquainted with consciousness” it does not follow that consciousness is fundamental.
* From “I am acquainted with unified experience” it does not follow that my subject is metaphysically simple.

## 10. Objections and replies

### Objection 1: “No serious philosopher thinks acquaintance alone proves hidden ontology.”

Reply:

Good. Then the paper has succeeded in clarifying the burden. The hidden ontology is supported, if at all, by ordinary theoretical warrant rather than by acquaintance itself.

### Objection 2: “Revelation reveals essence, not merely occurrence.”

Reply:

Even if acquaintance reveals something essential about phenomenal character, that does not entail that it reveals all grounding, realization, causal, constitutive, or distributional facts. Essence-talk must be carefully separated from explanatory-grounding-talk.

### Objection 3: “But acquaintance can support metaphysical inference.”

Reply:

Yes. The target is not inferential support. The target is **automatic noninferential transfer**. The view allows acquaintance to be a premise in an argument; it denies that the conclusion inherits acquaintance’s special authority merely by explaining the acquainted datum.

### Objection 4: “This collapses into the phenomenal concept strategy.”

Reply:

No. The phenomenal concept strategy is usually deployed in defense of physicalism. Your principle is neutral. It applies equally to physicalist, dualist, panpsychist, Russellian, idealist, and illusionist uses of first-person evidence.

### Objection 5: “This is just introspective skepticism.”

Reply:

No. Schwitzgebel-style skepticism strengthens the argument, but the argument does not depend on it. Even perfectly accurate acquaintance with an experience would not automatically reveal hidden ontology. ([faculty.ucr.edu][4])

## 11. Conclusion

The conclusion should be crisp:

> First-person acquaintance is epistemically important, but not metaphysically self-extending. It can warrant the reality and character of experience. It cannot, without further argument, warrant the hidden ontology introduced to explain experience. Once a theory moves from acquaintance to ground, essence, constitution, realization, distribution, or fundamentality, it leaves the domain of first-person warrant and enters ordinary theory choice.

Strong final sentence:

> Acquaintance gives us consciousness; it does not give us a metaphysics of consciousness for free.
