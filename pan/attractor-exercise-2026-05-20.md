# Attractor exercise for the panpsychism paper

Date: 2026-05-20

Method used: for each high-convergence passage, generate nearby rephrasings, look for repeated structures in the variants, name the attractor, then decide whether a conservative manuscript edit is justified. These are diagnostic notes, not manuscript prose.

## Target 1: Section 2 literature-positioning paragraph

Original:

> This discussion belongs near the recent literature on the combination problem and constitutive panpsychism, the terrain mapped in \citet{bruntrup2017}. That literature asks whether panpsychism can meet its internal explanatory obligations. Chalmers distinguishes panpsychism from panprotopsychism and treats the combination problem as the central obstacle for views that derive macro-consciousness from micro-level phenomenal or proto-phenomenal facts \citep{chalmers2015,chalmers2017combination}. Coleman presses the subject-summing problem against micropsychist combination \citep{coleman2014}. M{\o}rch argues that dispositionalism about properties supports a phenomenal-powers panpsychism \citep{morch2018,morchForthcoming}, and Roelofs defends the coherence of composite subjectivity against several anti-combination arguments \citep{roelofs2019}. Nagel's classic discussion raises a related question, whether panpsychism follows from claims about mental properties, reduction, and material composition \citep{nagel1979panpsychism}. My question is prior to all of these in the order of warrant: when such views introduce micro-experience, proto-phenomenal powers, phenomenal bonding, composite subjects, or cosmic subjectivity, what part of their support comes from first-person evidence, and what part must be earned as theory?

Nearby variants:

1. The closest literature is the debate over combination and constitutive panpsychism, as mapped by \citet{bruntrup2017}. Chalmers, Coleman, M{\o}rch, Roelofs, and Nagel ask how panpsychism can meet its internal explanatory burdens. My question comes before that: when the view introduces micro-experience, proto-phenomenal powers, bonding relations, composite subjects, or cosmic subjectivity, how much of the support is first-personal and how much is theoretical?
2. The combination literature supplies the immediate background. Chalmers treats combination as a central problem for deriving macro-consciousness from micro-level phenomenal or proto-phenomenal facts; Coleman presses subject-summing; M{\o}rch and Roelofs develop constructive replies; Nagel raises an older route from mental properties and composition. I ask a prior question about warrant.
3. The argument is adjacent to constitutive panpsychism and the combination problem. That debate asks whether the posits needed by panpsychism can explain ordinary consciousness. The present issue is whether those posits inherit any first-person authority before they do that explanatory work.
4. Work on the combination problem provides the main setting. Chalmers, Coleman, M{\o}rch, Roelofs, and Nagel all address the relation between micro-level mentality, composition, and macro-consciousness. The present paper does not try to solve that relation. It asks what kind of warrant attaches to the hidden items the relation requires.
5. Recent combination debates are the natural comparison class. They ask whether panpsychism can make good on its internal structure. My question is narrower and prior: when that structure includes micro-experience, bonding, proto-phenomenal powers, or cosmic subjectivity, does first-person evidence support those items directly?
6. The combination problem literature is the relevant neighborhood. It asks whether panpsychism can explain how ordinary consciousness arises from micro-level phenomenal or proto-phenomenal bases. I ask how the relevant bases are warranted in the first place.
7. The paper is not a new solution to the combination problem. It works one step earlier. Chalmers, Coleman, M{\o}rch, Roelofs, and Nagel examine whether panpsychism's internal posits can do the work assigned to them. I ask whether those posits receive first-person warrant at all.
8. Constitutive panpsychism already has a large literature on combination, subject-summing, phenomenal powers, and composite subjectivity. That literature matters here because it names the machinery panpsychism needs. The present question is whether that machinery can borrow its authority from acquaintance with ordinary experience.
9. The combination literature names the pressure points: micro-experience, proto-phenomenal bases, subject-summing, phenomenal powers, and composite subjectivity. Those are the very items whose warrant is at issue here.
10. Chalmers, Coleman, M{\o}rch, Roelofs, and Nagel are not treated here as a single target. They mark a set of problems about composition and consciousness. My question is about the epistemic status of the posits that enter those problems.

Attractor observed:

- "The closest literature / relevant neighborhood / adjacent debate" opener.
- Repeated "my question is prior/narrower" signposting.
- List-compression that can become a citation dump.
- The model keeps producing a map paragraph followed by a "but my question is..." move.

Conservative direction:

Use one direct positioning sentence, then name the literature through the problem it raises rather than through a survey rhythm. Keep all citation keys. Avoid "This discussion belongs near" and avoid repeating "my question is prior" if the final sentence can carry the contrast.

Recommended manuscript edit:

Replace the opener with "The nearest literature is the debate over the combination problem and constitutive panpsychism..." and let the final sentence carry the warrant contrast.

## Target 2: Section 2 contribution paragraph

Original:

> Several of these pressures are familiar, and the paper does not claim them as new. The reportability point is close to Lin on selfless states and Windt on dreamless sleep; the depsychologization worry overlaps with Frankish; the combination problem as a theoretical burden runs through Chalmers and Coleman; the structure/dynamics rejoinder follows a familiar physicalist response. The paper's contribution is the synthesis: it shows what panpsychism forfeits when the first-person route is treated as abduction, and makes explicit the further assumption that experience is the right kind of item to fill the categorical role structural physics is said to leave open.

Nearby variants:

1. I do not claim each pressure as new. Lin and Windt already bear on reportability, Frankish on depsychologization, Chalmers and Coleman on combination, and familiar physicalist replies on structure and dynamics. The paper brings these pressures together around a single question: what happens to panpsychism's first-person motivation once the route becomes abductive?
2. Much of the material is familiar. Reportability, depsychologization, combination, and structure/dynamics worries each have nearby sources. The contribution is to show how they converge on the transfer of first-person warrant.
3. The novelty is limited. The paper does not invent the reportability point, the depsychologization worry, the combination problem, or the structure/dynamics rejoinder. It uses them to isolate a further assumption: that experience is the right candidate to fill the categorical role left open by structural physics.
4. These are not free-standing discoveries. They are familiar pressures placed in one line of argument. That line shows what panpsychism loses when first-person evidence is treated as the starting point for an abduction rather than as direct warrant for hidden ontology.
5. I rely on familiar materials. Lin, Windt, Frankish, Chalmers, Coleman, and standard physicalist replies already supply much of the background. What is added is the warrant diagnosis: once the first-person route becomes abductive, the hidden phenomenal base must earn its place as theory.
6. The paper's claim to novelty is modest. It gathers familiar worries under one restriction on first-person warrant and applies that restriction to the hidden ontology panpsychism introduces.
7. The point is not that earlier authors missed every part of the argument. They did not. The point is that reportability, depsychologization, combination, and structure/dynamics all put pressure on the same transfer of warrant.
8. The familiar objections are left in place, but re-ordered. Their shared role is to separate the datum of experience from the metaphysical use panpsychism wants to make of it.
9. What is new, if anything, is the order of explanation. The paper begins with first-person warrant and asks how far it can travel before panpsychism must rely on ordinary abductive virtues.
10. The paper does not need to claim novelty for each component. Its contribution is to make the transfer of warrant explicit and to show why the categorical-role assumption cannot be supplied by acquaintance alone.

Attractor observed:

- "The novelty is modest / contribution is synthesis" academic contribution boilerplate.
- "The point is not..." correction rhythm.
- "What is new, if anything..." over-careful self-positioning.
- "Familiar pressures converge" abstract noun clustering.

Conservative direction:

Keep the humility, but avoid the stock "contribution is synthesis" line. The strongest version names what the paper does in active terms: it separates the datum from the metaphysical use made of it, and identifies the categorical-role assumption.

Recommended manuscript edit:

Replace "The paper's contribution is the synthesis..." with a more specific sentence: "The paper uses these familiar pressures to separate the datum of experience from the metaphysical role panpsychism asks it to play..."

## Target 3: Conclusion final paragraph

Original:

> Where panpsychism treats the epistemic privilege of ordinary consciousness as support for a hidden phenomenal ontology, it overdraws on experience. Panpsychism in its first-person-motivated forms is the central case considered here, but the same constraint applies more generally. The first-person datum remains indispensable, and the metaphysics built on top of it requires argument of its own.

Nearby variants:

1. Panpsychism overdraws on experience when it treats the privilege of ordinary consciousness as support for hidden phenomenal ontology. The argument has focused on first-person-motivated panpsychism, but the restriction is broader: the datum is indispensable, and the metaphysics added to it must be argued for.
2. The overdraw occurs when first-person authority is spent on what first-person experience never gives. Panpsychism is the central case here. The datum remains indispensable; the metaphysics built on it does not inherit that status.
3. The lesson is not that first-person experience should be demoted. It is that its authority should not be transferred to hidden ontology. Panpsychism is the case at hand, but the restriction is more general.
4. Ordinary consciousness is indispensable evidence. It becomes overdrawn only when its authority is extended to micro-experience, proto-phenomenal bases, cosmic subjectivity, or other hidden posits. Those posits require argument of their own.
5. The first-person datum does real work. It establishes consciousness and constrains any theory that would deny or deflate it. It does not settle the metaphysics a theory builds on top of it.
6. Panpsychism overdraws on experience when it moves from the authority of ordinary consciousness to hidden phenomenal ontology without an independent argument. That is the central restriction defended here.
7. First-person experience remains the starting point. It is not a blank check. The metaphysics that follows must be earned by argument.
8. The paper's target has been panpsychism's first-person route. The broader restriction is simple: do not spend the authority of acquaintance on structures acquaintance never reveals.
9. If panpsychism uses first-person evidence to establish consciousness, it is on firm ground. If it uses the same evidence to underwrite hidden phenomenal ontology, it has shifted to metaphysics and needs a further argument.
10. The point is not to weaken the first-person datum. It is to keep it from being made to do another job. Consciousness is given; fundamental phenomenal ontology is not.

Attractor observed:

- "The lesson is not..." correction rhythm.
- "The broader restriction is simple..." motto-making risk.
- "not a blank check" too cute and too slogan-like.
- "does real work" repeats safe philosophical idiom.
- The variants keep trying to end with a neat aphorism.

Conservative direction:

Do not make the ending clever. Keep "overdraws on experience" because it is the title phrase and not merely decorative. Prefer concrete named posits over abstract "hidden ontology" where possible.

Recommended manuscript edit:

Slightly revise the final paragraph to name the hidden posits and remove one abstract repetition: "Where panpsychism treats the epistemic privilege of ordinary consciousness as support for micro-experience, proto-phenomenal bases, cosmic subjectivity, or other hidden phenomenal posits, it overdraws on experience..."

## Overall attractor from the exercise

Across all three targets, the model keeps drifting toward:

- "my question is prior/narrower"
- "the contribution is modest/synthetic"
- "the lesson is not X but Y"
- "the broader restriction is simple"
- tidy final sentences that sound like a moral

The safest edits are not more dramatic rewrites. They are small substitutions that remove stock academic signposting and replace abstract labels with the paper's own concrete terms: first-person warrant, acquaintance, micro-experience, proto-phenomenal bases, cosmic subjectivity, phenomenal bonding, and categorical role.
