# Source Integration Plan for `pan/`

Working target: `pan/overdrawing-on-experience-mainbody.tex`

This plan keeps the paper's existing architecture intact. The aim is not to add a literature-review section or make the paper responsible for every major debate in consciousness and physicalism. The aim is to strengthen four pressure points that reviewers are likely to notice: Russellian monism, Russellian monism as a physicalist option, the phenomenal concept strategy, and ignorance-based responses to anti-physicalist inference.

## Verification Status

The bibliographic and interpretive claims below were checked against primary publisher pages where available, and against stable scholarly records where publisher pages were not as informative.

- Alter's book is accurately cited as a 2023 Oxford University Press book. Its subtitle and Oxford abstract show that its central route is from the knowledge argument to Russellian monism, not simply a standalone structure/dynamics argument. The plan therefore treats Alter as a recent major Russellian monist target, while avoiding the narrower claim that he is only or chiefly the target of the structure/dynamics section.
- Pereboom's book is accurately cited as a 2011 Oxford University Press book. Oxford's abstract confirms that it combines a response based on possible introspective inaccuracy with a Russellian appeal to ignorance of things in themselves. It is therefore best used to show that Russellian monism can be explored within physicalist dialectics, not as a straightforward panpsychist view.
- Stoljar's book is accurately cited as a 2006 Oxford University Press book. Oxford's abstract confirms that the book explains the consciousness problem partly through ignorance of relevant physical facts. It should be used only to discipline inference from epistemic limitation to metaphysical conclusion.
- Balog's article is listed by the Rutgers record as January 2012 in *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 84(1): 1-23, with DOI `10.1111/j.1933-1592.2011.00541.x`. Some PhilPapers records list 2011, apparently tracking online-first or pre-publication metadata. For this manuscript, use 2012 to match the journal issue record, unless the journal's own formatted citation is later found to require 2011.

## Integration Principle

Use the new sources as defensive anchors, not as new argumentative engines. The present paper already has a strong and defensible thesis. These sources should show that the author knows the relevant terrain and has placed the introspective warrant constraint in the right dialectical neighborhood.

The guiding rule is:

> If a source does not directly clarify the limits of first-person warrant, the structure/dynamics inference, or the epistemic force of phenomenal concepts, it should not be added.

## 1. Torin Alter

Primary source to add:

Alter, Torin. 2023. *The Matter of Consciousness: From the Knowledge Argument to Russellian Monism*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.

Best location:

- Section 2, `Scope and Bracketing`, where the paper currently says it brackets the full case for Russellian monism but addresses one step within it.
- Section 6, `Structure and Dynamics Do Not Rescue the Inference`, where the structure/dynamics argument is presented and constrained.

Why this helps:

Alter is a recent major defender of the movement from the knowledge argument toward Russellian monism. Since the paper already engages Russellian monism throughout Section 6, omitting him may look like an avoidable gap. Adding Alter allows the paper to say, more precisely, that it is not ignoring a strong contemporary route to Russellian monism.

How to integrate without bloat:

Add one sentence in Section 2:

> Recent defenses of Russellian monism, especially Alter's movement from the knowledge argument to Russellian monism, give this route a serious contemporary form; the present paper does not attempt to refute that route as a whole.

Add one sentence in Section 6:

> Alter's defense of Russellian monism makes the vacancy more than a rhetorical device: the claim is that standard physical description omits intrinsic properties that may both underlie physical structure and bear on consciousness.

Then immediately preserve the paper's central distinction:

> The present point is narrower: even if such a vacancy is granted, first-person acquaintance does not by itself show that the categorical filler is phenomenal or proto-phenomenal.

Risk to avoid:

Do not make Alter the main target. If Alter is made central, Section 6 will need a fuller reconstruction of the knowledge argument, Russellian monism, and the relation between intrinsic properties and physical structure. That would blow up the paper. Use Alter to show awareness of a strong current target, then keep the argument focused.

## 2. Derk Pereboom

Primary source to add:

Pereboom, Derk. 2011. *Consciousness and the Prospects of Physicalism*. Oxford: Oxford University Press.

Best location:

- Section 2, when bracketing the full Russellian monist case.
- Section 6, when explaining that Russellian monism may be a physicalist option rather than an anti-physicalist panpsychist add-on.

Why this helps:

Pereboom is useful because he treats Russellian monism as part of the prospects for physicalism. That matters for this paper because it prevents a reviewer from reading the critique as if Russellian monism were simply a panpsychist escape hatch. Pereboom also helps keep the paper's framing modest: the target is not Russellian monism as such, but a transfer of first-person warrant to hidden phenomenal ontology.

How to integrate without bloat:

Add a compact qualification in Section 2:

> Russellian monism need not be introduced as a straightforward rejection of physicalism; Pereboom treats it as one way of exploring physicalism's prospects once introspective representation and ignorance of things in themselves are both handled cautiously.

In Section 6, cite Pereboom in the fair presentation of the Russellian point:

> The thought that ignorance of things in themselves leaves room for a Russellian account is a serious metaphysical option, including in physicalist contexts.

Then preserve the constraint:

> What follows from that option is not first-person warrant for phenomenal microstructure, but a theoretical burden to explain why phenomenal or proto-phenomenal intrinsic properties are the best occupants of the role.

Risk to avoid:

Do not frame Pereboom as a panpsychist in the same sense as Goff or Strawson. The point of citing him is precisely to avoid that flattening.

## 3. The Phenomenal Concept Strategy

Recommended minimal additions:

- Keep Papineau as the existing anchor.
- Add Balog as the clearest general defense of the strategy.
- Add Loar only if the prose needs the historical origin of the strategy.
- Add Carruthers only if the revision needs a fuller list of physicalist phenomenal-concept views, or if it discusses recognitional concepts. Do not make Carruthers a worked target unless the manuscript later discusses his higher-order or global-workspace commitments.

Primary source to add:

Balog, Katalin. 2012. "In Defense of the Phenomenal Concept Strategy." *Philosophy and Phenomenological Research* 84(1): 1-23.

Optional historical source:

Loar, Brian. 1990. "Phenomenal States." *Philosophical Perspectives* 4: 81-108.

Best location:

- Section 3, `The Introspective Warrant Constraint`, after explaining that first-person warrant is not transferable.
- Section 8, `Explanatory Burden`, where Papineau is currently cited.

Why this helps:

The phenomenal concept strategy is close to the paper's diagnostic project. It argues that the epistemic asymmetry between physical and phenomenal thought may arise from the way we think about conscious states, rather than from a non-physical or fundamental phenomenal ontology. The present paper should not collapse into that strategy, but it should distinguish itself from it. That distinction will make the paper more defensible.

How to integrate without bloat:

Add a paragraph in Section 8, expanding the existing Papineau paragraph:

> The phenomenal concept strategy gives a nearby but distinct diagnosis. On that strategy, the felt asymmetry between phenomenal and physical thought may be explained by the special character of phenomenal concepts rather than by a further phenomenal ingredient in the world. The present argument need not accept that full physicalist diagnosis. Its narrower point is that even if phenomenal concepts or acquaintance give us a distinctive way of thinking about our own experiences, that fact does not extend first-person warrant to micro-experiences, proto-phenomenal categorical bases, or cosmic subjectivity.

This adds one distinction:

- Phenomenal concept strategy: explains why the epistemic gap need not imply an ontological gap.
- Present paper: explains why first-person warrant does not transfer to hidden panpsychist posits.

Risk to avoid:

Do not let this become a broad physicalist reply to the hard problem. The paper should remain neutral on whether the phenomenal concept strategy succeeds. Its only needed claim is that the strategy shows a serious alternative explanation of epistemic asymmetry.

## 4. Daniel Stoljar

Primary source to add:

Stoljar, Daniel. 2006. *Ignorance and Imagination: The Epistemic Origin of the Problem of Consciousness*. New York: Oxford University Press.

Best location:

- Section 2, after the scope restriction, as part of the broader warrant-question framing.
- Section 8, near Papineau and the phenomenal concept strategy, where the paper asks whether hidden phenomenal posits explain enough to justify their cost.

Why this helps:

Stoljar is relevant because the paper is about warrant under ignorance. His ignorance hypothesis gives another way to resist quick metaphysical inference from epistemic limitation. This supports the paper's core discipline: a gap in what we know does not by itself identify what fills the gap.

How to integrate without bloat:

Add one sentence in Section 2:

> Stoljar's ignorance-based treatment of the consciousness problem is also relevant here, since it warns against moving too quickly from epistemic limitation to metaphysical conclusion.

Add one sentence in Section 8:

> If part of the pressure toward panpsychism arises from ignorance of the relevant physical or categorical facts, then the proper response is not to deny the datum of consciousness, but to separate the datum from speculative claims about what must underlie it.

Risk to avoid:

Do not use Stoljar to imply that future science will solve the hard problem. That would make the paper sound more scientistic than it is. Use him only to discipline the inference from ignorance to ontology.

## Sources Not Recommended for Immediate Addition

Carruthers should not be added as a worked interlocutor in the first pass unless the manuscript adds a specific discussion of recognitional concepts, higher-order theories, or global workspace theory. He is relevant to the phenomenal concept strategy, but adding him without a precise role would expand the citation surface without strengthening the main argument. If the author wants the paper to show explicit awareness of the broader phenomenal-concept literature named by reviewers, Carruthers can be included in a compact parenthetical alongside Loar and Balog, without further discussion.

Loar is optional. He is historically important, but Balog plus Papineau may be enough for a compact, reviewer-facing acknowledgment of the phenomenal concept strategy. If Loar is added, cite the 1990 *Philosophical Perspectives* article as historical background, not as a target discussed at length.
