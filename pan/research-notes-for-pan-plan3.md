## 1. Goff 2017, `Consciousness and Fundamental Reality`, chapter 5

Existing key: `goff2017cfr`

Exact bibliographic citation:

Goff, Philip. 2017. "Revelation and the Transparency Argument." In *Consciousness and Fundamental Reality*, 106-132. Oxford: Oxford University Press. DOI: `10.1093/oso/9780190677015.003.0005`.

Repository citation to use in manuscript:

- General Revelation discussion: `\citep[106--132]{goff2017cfr}`
- If making the specific "essential nature" claim without a verified internal page, cite the whole chapter range, not a single page.

Verified source link:

- Oxford Academic chapter page: https://academic.oup.com/book/3834/chapter/145321304

Verified source summary:

The chapter defends Revelation as a special epistemic relation to one's conscious states. The chapter abstract says the thesis has two parts: first, the nature of conscious states is introspectively revealed; second, one can know with near-certainty that such states are instantiated. The abstract also states that Revelation is used to secure a premise in the transparency conceivability argument and that it conflicts with physicalism because physicalism would imply introspective access to the alleged physical nature of conscious states.

Short verified quote candidate:

> "their nature is introspectively revealed to us"

Use with caution. This wording is from the OUP chapter abstract, not from the chapter body as checked here. If used in the manuscript, cite the chapter range or verify the same wording in the book text first.

Revision use:

The target is not Goff's weaker claim that introspection reveals something about experience. The target is the stronger step from introspective acquaintance to knowledge of a phenomenal property's nature or essence. The paper can grant, for argument's sake, that introspection reveals the character of the experience one is undergoing. The blocked inference is from that character to the metaphysical role of the property: whether it is fundamental, categorical, microphysical, cosmic, or available to fill a Russellian vacancy.

Suggested close-engagement move:

Goff's Revelation thesis is strong enough to make acquaintance do anti-physicalist work, because it treats introspection as revealing more than the mere occurrence of experience. The paper should say exactly where it accepts and where it stops: acquaintance may disclose the phenomenal character of a current experience, but it does not disclose that the property instantiating that character is a fundamental categorical property of matter. That second claim is a metaphysical role assignment, not part of the first-person datum.

Possible manuscript sentence:

Goff's Revelation thesis is most useful to panpsychism when it treats introspection as revealing the nature of a phenomenal state, not merely its occurrence. Even if that much is granted, the revealed item is still the experience as undergone. Nothing in the first-person presentation tells us that the property so presented is fundamental, categorical, microphysical, or the occupant of a Russellian vacancy.

## 2. Roelofs 2020, "Consciousness, Revelation, and Confusion"

Existing key: `roelofs2020revelation`

Exact bibliographic citation:

Roelofs, Luke. 2020. "Consciousness, Revelation, and Confusion: Are Constitutive Panpsychists Hoist by Their Own Petard?" *Dialectica* 74 (1): 63-96. DOI: `10.48106/dial.v74.i1.04`.

Repository citation to use in manuscript:

- General argument: `\citep{roelofs2020revelation}`
- Article page range where needed: `\citep[63--96]{roelofs2020revelation}`
- Avoid more precise page numbers unless the final PDF page breaks are checked.

Verified source links:

- Publisher HTML: https://philosophie.ch/roelofs-2020
- PhilPapers metadata: https://philpapers.org/rec/ROECRA
- DOAJ metadata: https://doaj.org/article/428e43242d924e1387d5ebad3c3d7e27

Verified source summary:

Roelofs frames constitutive panpsychism as facing a "revelation problem." Panpsychists often rely on the idea that being conscious gives special insight into the nature of consciousness, but constitutive panpsychism also says ordinary consciousness is constituted by many microexperiences that do not appear as such in introspection. Roelofs's response is not to abandon revelation, but to qualify it. He distinguishes several revelation theses and argues that a medium-strength version can preserve enough first-person authority to challenge physicalism while allowing constitutive panpsychism to say that microexperiences are present only confusedly or en masse, not as individually introspected components.

Short verified quote candidates:

> "a plausible, well-motivated 'medium-strength' sort of revelation"

> "strong enough to bring down physicalism but weak enough to leave constitutive panpsychism standing"

Use with caution. These are from the publisher HTML and search-result-accessible text. They are safe as descriptions of Roelofs's own framing, but page-specific journal pagination should be verified before manuscript use.

Most important exact distinctions from Roelofs:

- He distinguishes revelation claims by topic: knowledge of a token experience, understanding of a phenomenal property, and knowledge of a property's whole nature.
- He distinguishes appearance-to-reality and reality-to-appearance directions.
- He explicitly describes RT5 as a self-intimation thesis: reflection on a pure phenomenal concept can reveal the whole nature of the corresponding phenomenal property.
- He treats the problem for constitutive panpsychism as arising because macroexperience does not reveal a vast array of microexperiences.
- His positive reply invokes limits on attention, concepts, and interpretation. The microexperiences may be immediately present only collectively, not individually.

Revision use:

Roelofs is not an opponent to flatten. He is useful because his moderation supports the paper's warrant limit. If revelation must be qualified to leave constitutive panpsychism standing, then first-person acquaintance does not transparently disclose all constitutive facts about consciousness. That is exactly the space the paper needs: the hidden constitution can be defended, but not as something directly given in first-person acquaintance.

Suggested close-engagement move:

Present Roelofs as granting the key epistemic moderation. The paper should not say that Roelofs proves the introspective warrant constraint. Rather, it should say that Roelofs's own solution to the revelation problem depends on a distinction between what is present to consciousness and what is discriminated, conceptually grasped, or available as a constitutive fact. Once that distinction is admitted, first-person warrant cannot simply be transferred to the micro-level constitution.

Possible manuscript sentence:

Roelofs's medium-strength revelation thesis is important because it shows how little the present argument needs. Roelofs can protect constitutive panpsychism only by allowing that introspection does not disclose every constitutive fact about consciousness. That concession is not a defeat for panpsychism, but it does mark the limit of first-person warrant: whatever the hidden micro-constitution is, its support is no longer the direct authority of acquaintance.

## 3. Goff 2024, "How Exactly Does Panpsychism Help Explain Consciousness?"

Existing key: `goff2024`

Exact bibliographic citation:

Goff, Philip. 2024. "How Exactly Does Panpsychism Help Explain Consciousness?" *Journal of Consciousness Studies* 31 (3): 56-82. DOI: `10.53765/20512201.31.3.056`.

Repository citation to use in manuscript:

- General claim: `\citep{goff2024}`
- Article range if needed: `\citep[56--82]{goff2024}`
- Do not add precise journal page numbers from the accessible preprint unless checked against the final journal version.

Verified source links:

- Durham repository record: https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/output/2486736/how-exactly-does-panpsychism-help-explain-consciousness
- Accessible PDF used for text verification: https://achrafkassioui.com/library/Philip%20Goff%20-%20How%20Exactly%20does%20Panpsychism%20Help%20Explain%20Consciousness.pdf

Verified source summary:

Goff distinguishes strong and weak emergentist panpsychism. He argues that standard weak emergentist panpsychism faces an explanatory-gap analogue, while strong emergentist panpsychism faces causal-closure or dualism-like worries. His proposed solution is hybrid cosmopsychism: subjects of experience strongly emerge, but their phenomenal properties are weakly emergent because the emergent subjects inherit or receive phenomenal property instances from the universe subject. The proposal is designed to preserve a panpsychist advantage while avoiding standard combination and causal-closure pressures.

Short verified quote candidates:

> "subjects of experience are strongly emergent"

> "their phenomenal properties are weakly emergent"

The accessible PDF states both in the abstract. These are short and directly useful, but confirm final pagination before using them with a page cite.

Exact argumentative pressure for this paper:

Goff's hybrid view is especially valuable for this paper because it concedes strong emergence at the level of subjects. That is the point where panpsychism was supposed to relieve the pressure most directly. If ordinary subjects still require strong emergence, basic laws, localization, thinning, or transference relations, then the view has not simply dissolved the emergence difficulty by placing phenomenality at the base. It has relocated the difficulty into a theoretical account of how local subjects arise and how phenomenal properties are assigned to them.

Details from the accessible PDF:

- Goff says weak emergentist panpsychism risks a physicalism-like explanatory gap, while strong emergentist panpsychism risks dualism-like causal worries.
- Hybrid cosmopsychism combines strong emergence about subjects with weak emergence about phenomenal properties.
- On the hybrid view, emergent subjects inherit phenomenal property instances that previously belonged to the universe.
- Goff introduces localization and thinning requirements to explain how local subjects receive only the right aspects of the universe's experience.
- The paper's critique should not claim that Goff has abandoned panpsychism. The point is narrower: the machinery needed to make the view work is theoretical machinery, not something inherited from first-person acquaintance.

Suggested close-engagement move:

Use Goff 2024 to show that the emergence difficulty reappears inside a sophisticated panpsychist framework. The target is not the mere word "emergence." The target is the dialectical shift: once subjects strongly emerge and their experiential profile depends on laws of localization, thinning, or property transfer, first-person acquaintance with ordinary consciousness does not itself underwrite the hidden cosmopsychist structure.

Possible manuscript sentence:

Goff's hybrid cosmopsychism makes the point from within panpsychism itself. If subjects strongly emerge while phenomenal properties are weakly emergent, then panpsychism has not removed emergence from the explanation of ordinary subjecthood. It has moved the burden to the laws or relations by which a local subject arises and receives the relevant phenomenal properties.
