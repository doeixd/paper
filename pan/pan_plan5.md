## 6. Targeted content fixes (Notes 4-7)

### Note 4 — pure awareness: dilemma primary, reportability secondary
Section 4.1 currently leads with reportability and is titled after it. Re-order
so the dilemma frames the subsection: if pure awareness has some phenomenal
character it is not bare characterless subjectivity; if it has none it is not
disclosed as experience. Reportability becomes the secondary, supporting move,
explicitly marked as such, because a defender will rightly say the report is
fallible but the episode itself was directly known, and the reply to that is the
dilemma, not reportability. Retitle the subsection "Pure Awareness." Keep the
private-acquaintance / public-evidence distinction, framed so it never reads as
a claim that reportability limits private evidence itself.

### Notes 5-6 — the quality/role hinge, made central
These are one point. Acquaintance reveals what a phenomenal quality is like; it
does not reveal that the property bearing it plays the categorical-grounding
role. That role assignment is theoretical. The point already exists (old
Section 5 on Revelation, old Section 7 on Goff's simplicity argument) but is
buried mid-paragraph. Elevate it: state it crisply in Section 3.2 as the
load-bearing reply, in plain language, without coining a capitalized term, and
reuse it in Section 5 against the Russellian claim that we know phenomenal
character "as intrinsic nature itself." Distinguish the two Russellian claims
explicitly: that physics leaves categorical nature unspecified, and that
consciousness is our only acquaintance with categorical nature. The second
carries the weight and is where the hinge bites. State the hinge once, well; do
not turn it into a recurring slogan.

### Note 7 — tighten the organization reply
In Section 6, tighten the combination passage. If organization explains unity,
temporality, contents, attention, reportability, and valence, the panpsychist
must name the residual explanatory contribution of fundamental phenomenality.
If that contribution is only "preventing emergence from the wholly
non-phenomenal," the view has supplied metaphysical comfort, not yet an
explanation of the consciousness we undergo. Use that sharper phrasing while
keeping the existing contrastive question and the category-error reply intact.

## 9. Transitions and the visible spine

The reviewer wants the argumentative spine visible. The spine is made visible by
the section structure and by each section opening on its actual claim. It is not
made visible by signpost sentences. These pull in opposite directions: more
signposting makes the spine less visible, not more, because it buries the
argument under narration about the argument.

So the restructure is also a chance to delete transitions the old structure
needed. Concretely:
- A section opens on its first real claim. No "This section argues that ...," no "In what follows ...," no "Having established X, we now turn to Y."
- A section ends on a substantive point, not a bridge sentence and not a capping motto.
- Cross-references are used only when a later step genuinely depends on an earlier one, and then they are bare ("the case Section 2 brackets"), not narrated ("As we saw in our earlier discussion ...").
- The dissolved Section 2 material lands inside its new host sections, integrated into existing paragraphs. It does not arrive as a new bridge paragraph.

On hand-holding: the CLAUDE.md instruction to hold the reader's hand down the
logical path means the logic is gapless, every premise stated, no teleporting
between claims. It does not mean meta-commentary about what the paper is doing.
Make the logic complete; do not narrate it.

## 10. Anti-AI-smell guidance

A restructure reopens every seam. The first and last sentence of every moved
paragraph is a seam and is the highest-risk prose in this pass. Audit every
seam against the list below. Source: `pan/AI smells.txt`.

### Restructure-specific smells (the new risk this pass introduces)
- Bridge sentences whose only job is to reach the next section or paragraph.
- Section or paragraph openers that announce ("This section examines ...," "We now consider ...").
- Recap sentences at section starts ("Recall that ...," "As established above ...") used where no load-bearing dependency requires them.
- Decorative capping lines at section ends, written for the closer position rather than to make a point.
- Restatement: re-saying a claim already made, in fresh words, because the draft does not trust the reader to have kept it. The user flags restatement and announcement as the big one.

### Sentence and rhythm smells
- The negation/correction rhythm: "not just X, but Y," "isn't merely X," "no longer X but Y," "less about X than Y," and the subtle forms "on paper X, in reality Y," "the promise is X, the result is Y," "what looks like X is really Y." This is a major smell. The prose keeps denying one idea to reveal the "real" one. Hunt it specifically.
- Motto-making: turning a claim into a slogan or aphorism. The quality/role hinge is the prime temptation here; state it as plain argument.
- Metronome rhythm: medium sentence, medium sentence, medium sentence; paragraphs of suspiciously similar length. Vary sentence and paragraph length to the thought. Section 2 is short on purpose; do not pad it.
- Banned openers and fillers: "the question is," "the answer is," "the result is," "this matters because," "it is worth noting," "importantly," "crucially," "moreover," "furthermore," and "however" used as a polished pivot rather than a real contrast.
- Em dashes: none. Colons: sparingly.

### Voice smells
- Hedging a claim until it has no teeth; vague attribution ("some critics argue"); consensus-middle language that drains the position. Qualifications in this paper are precise scope statements, not hedges; keep the precision, keep the position.
- Sanding off the author's voice. The style is simple, clear, detailed, measured, humble but confident. Preserve its rhythm and its existing phrasings; do not neutralize them into corporate prose.
- Staying abstract too long. The paper's concrete anchors (the given pain versus the posited electron, the mass analogy, dreamless sleep, temporal grain) earn their place; keep them.

### Procedure
After each new section is drafted, re-read only its first and last sentences and
its subsection seams, and check them against this list. Then read the section
once for rhythm, out of the editor, listening for the metronome. The plan4 pass
already cleaned the prose; the risk now is re-introducing smells at the joins.

## 14. Guidance on what not to lose

This pass is mostly structural, so the danger is not making a false new claim.
The danger is dropping a qualification that kept an existing claim defensible,
or replacing a sharp local phrase with a smoother but weaker one. Use this
section while revising, not only at the end.

### Load-bearing argumentative details
- Keep the paper's target narrow. The claim is about first-person warrant and
  its limits, not a refutation of panpsychism. Every major section should still
  allow that panpsychism may be true, may be abductively defended, or may be
  motivated by arguments outside the target of the paper.
- Keep the three-way distinction in the datum: experience establishes that
  consciousness exists; it gives cautious warrant for some phenomenal features;
  it does not by itself establish metaphysical status, distribution,
  fundamentality, cosmic subjecthood, microphysical placement, or bonding
  relations.
- Keep the grain-of-acquaintance formulation. Do not revert to "ordinary
  consciousness" as the datum unless the contrast really is with unusual cases.
  The paper grants pure awareness, dreamless-sleep reports, minimal experience,
  objectless moods, and attenuated phenomenal character. The datum is
  experience as given, with some phenomenal character however thin or unstable.
- Keep the distinction between explaining phenomenal structure and explaining
  phenomenal existence. Organization may explain unity, temporality, contents,
  attention, reportability, and valence without explaining why there is anything
  it is like. The pressure on panpsychism is then to say what fundamental
  phenomenality contributes beyond blocking emergence from the wholly
  non-phenomenal.
- Keep the primitive-determinacy concession. The central objection is not that
  all determinate phenomenal character must be reductively explained. It is that
  determinate character in our case does not license claims about where such
  character is distributed or whether it is fundamental.
- Keep the quality/role hinge. Acquaintance may reveal what a phenomenal
  quality is like. It does not reveal that the property so encountered plays the
  categorical-grounding role. That role assignment is theoretical.
- Keep the private-acquaintance concession in the pure-awareness subsection.
  Do not make reportability carry the argument. Even if the subject has private
  acquaintance with the episode, the dilemma remains: either the episode has
  some phenomenal character, or it does not.
- Keep the Goff 2024 qualification. The point is not that the view collapses.
  The point is that the anti-emergentist motivation has narrowed if subjecthood
  must still emerge, and so it cannot by itself secure the whole first-person
  datum.

### Defenses that should remain visible in the body
- Warrant-not-existence: the paper denies inherited warrant, not the existence
  of the posits.
- Constraint-not-refutation: the introspective warrant constraint limits one
  motivation for panpsychism; it leaves other motivations standing.
- Abductive allowance: theoretical posits are legitimate when they earn their
  place by explanatory work.
- Saad and harmony cases: positions that do not lean on first-person warrant are
  largely outside the paper's target, except as useful contrasts.
- Morch partial case: the paper should not overstate the objection where a view
  uses first-person acquaintance as one premise in a wider theoretical argument.
- Category-error reply: allow the panpsychist to say micro-experience is not
  meant to explain macro-experience in the same way organization explains
  reportable structure. Then press the residual explanatory burden.
- No-illusionism disclaimer: the paper is not denying consciousness, reducing it
  away, or treating first-person evidence as worthless.

### Phrasings worth preserving or varying carefully
- Preserve verbatim: "real but finite" once, "earned, not inherited" in the
  conclusion, the quote-box constraint, and the five-step enumerate.
- Use "hidden phenomenal ontology" sparingly. It is a good phrase for the
  central thesis, but repeated too often it sounds prosecutorial. Vary with
  "hidden phenomenal posits" for lists of micro-experience, proto-experience,
  cosmic subjectivity, or bonding; "theoretical phenomenal base" when discussing
  explanatory burden; and "unacquainted phenomenal structure" only where the
  point is specifically that the structure is not given in experience.
- Keep "overdraws on experience" limited to high-payoff locations: title,
  one mid-paper diagnosis if needed, and conclusion. Do not turn it into a
  refrain.
- Keep concrete anchors. The pain/electron contrast, the mass analogy,
  dreamless-sleep reports, pure awareness, and temporal grain are not decorative.
  They prevent the paper from floating at the level of abstract epistemology.
- Do not replace vivid but precise source-note language with neutral academic
  filler. If a phrase has heat and does real work, keep it. If it sounds like a
