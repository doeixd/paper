### D. Strawson close reading (Section 7) -- anchored

Section 7 already quotes Strawson "the fundamental given natural fact" (p. 4) and
glosses the licensing step. Sharpen it into an actual reading of the inference.
Strawson makes experience "the obligatory starting point for any remotely
realistic" theory of what there is (p. 4); that is the move from acquaintance to
a constraint on fundamental theory. Show it in one continuous sentence rather
than setup-and-payoff. The existing reply (acquaintance with ordinary experience
does not warrant fundamental-level experientiality) stays. Optionally add the
p. 8 quote ("the existence of every real concrete thing involves experiential
being") if it earns its place; one well-read quote beats two stacked ones.

### E. Roelofs close reading (Section 7) -- anchored

This is the most important new engagement. The reviewer singles Roelofs out as
the panpsychist who has thought hardest about exactly this constraint.
Verification also clarified the dialectic, which the plan must get right:

Roelofs warns against "inferring absence of structure from the failure of
structure to be manifest" (p. 87). That warning targets an inference to
non-existence: do not conclude the micro-structure is absent just because
introspection does not reveal it. The paper's constraint does not make that
inference. Section 3 already says explicitly that the first step "concerns
warrant, not existence" and "leaves every panpsychist posit metaphysically
open." So Roelofs's warning, far from threatening the paper, is consistent with
it: the paper draws only the warrant conclusion, never the existence conclusion.

Better still, Roelofs concedes the paper's premise. His "confusion" account
holds that micro-experiences are present but not distinctly introspectible; he
concludes we are "unable to tell introspectively just how composite they might
really be" (p. 89). That is the manifest-level limit the constraint relies on.
The engagement: the most careful constitutive panpsychist defends the view by
conceding that introspection does not disclose the micro-level, which is exactly
why warrant for the micro-level must be theoretical. Roelofs's medium-strength
revelation is "strong enough to bring down physicalism but weak enough to leave
constitutive panpsychism standing" (pp. 61--62); the paper's point is that the
second half of that formula concedes the warrant transfer.

Place this in Section 7's brushstroke/grain paragraph. Merge in the Roelofs
sentence currently at the start of Section 7's "other sophisticated responses"
paragraph, which now partly duplicates it (see Item M, cuts). One tightened
paragraph with the quotes, not "a full page" (the reviewer asked for a page;
house style and the existing Roelofs material in Sections 5 and 7 do not
warrant it).

### I. Answer the category-error reply (Section 8)

The reviewer's strongest unmet objection: a panpsychist holds that
non-experience-to-experience is a category error while
micro-experience-to-macro-experience is not, so the two emergence gaps are not
equally burdensome.

Add a short response near Section 8's two-gaps passage. Be careful not to
overclaim (the v1 draft said granting the reply "costs nothing"; that is false).
Honest version, two points:
1. The category-error claim is one of the anti-emergentist arguments the paper
   brackets in Section 2. If it succeeds, the two gaps are indeed not symmetric
   in kind. But that asymmetry would be a theoretical result, established by an
   anti-emergentist argument, not something delivered by first-person
   acquaintance. So it does not restore first-person warrant for the hidden
   base; it relocates the defense to abductive ground, which is the paper's
   thesis.
2. Even granted, the panpsychist gap is not free. Subject-summing, unity, and
   structure (the combination problem) remain. Panpsychism has not dissolved
   emergence in advance; it has exchanged one explanatory debt for another.

This sits with, not against, the existing two-gaps point.

### G. Clarify Mørch's status (Section 5)

The reviewer asks whether Mørch is a target or exempt. Section 5's
phenomenal-powers paragraph already grants her argument is largely theoretical.
Add the precise clarification: the panpsychist argument Mørch develops runs from
the premise that "the only categorical properties we know are phenomenal
properties." That premise is a knowledge claim; it trades on the epistemic
standing of the first-person case even when the surrounding argument is
abductive. So Mørch is a partial case, not fully exempt: the explicit argument
is theoretical, but the premise that motivates it is exactly the kind of warrant
claim the constraint examines. Fair to Mørch, and it sharpens the constraint's
reach. One quote, one or two sentences.

### J. Sharpen the Section 6 "only model we have" response

The reviewer: a panpsychist replies that consciousness is not merely a candidate
model of intrinsic nature but the *only* concept of intrinsic nature available,
so extending it is a theoretical necessity, not an arbitrary leap.

Section 6 already concedes "the move is not arbitrary" and that consciousness
"appears to be the only available model." Add the counter in the paper's own
words (no Chalmers quote; it could not be verified): being our only positive
model of intrinsic nature is a fact about the limits of our concepts, not
warrant for the model's truth. "We have no other model" does not entail "this
model is correct"; it is equally compatible with intrinsic nature being
genuinely unknown. The honest alternative to phenomenal filling is acknowledged
ignorance, not default acceptance. This connects to Stoljar's ignorance
hypothesis, already cited in Section 8. Two or three sentences, no new citation.

## Anti-AI-smell discipline for this pass

Adding quotes and four argumentative passages is exactly the kind of edit that
reintroduces machine-writing tells. Rules for the new prose:
- No quote lead-ins. Do not write "as Goff writes," "as Strawson puts it,"
  "consider what Roelofs says." Place the quote where the sentence's own grammar
  needs it.
- No quote followed by "this matters because," "what this shows is," "the point
  is." If the surrounding sentence does not already make the point, rewrite it
  so it does.
- No setup-and-payoff pairs. Avoid "Goff says X. But Y." Carry the contrast in
  one continuous sentence where possible.
- Do not introduce "not X but Y" or correction-rhythm framing when contrasting
  what a quote says against what it warrants.
- Each quote earns its place. If the sentence works without the quote, cut the
  quote or cut the sentence; do not keep both.
- Read each new passage aloud for metronome rhythm; vary sentence length.
- Hold the modest posture. The reviewer's word is "takedown"; the same reviewer
  praised the paper's dialectical modesty as its first strength. Quotes pin the
  target fairly. No prosecutorial tone.
