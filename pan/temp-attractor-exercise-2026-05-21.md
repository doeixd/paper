## Target 1: Introduction Thesis Paragraph

Original:

> Although panpsychism remains a live metaphysical option, one of its most powerful motivations cannot bear all the weight sometimes placed on it. The disputed move is the slide from acquaintance with phenomenal character to a metaphysical claim about the role that phenomenal character plays in the constitution of matter. Acquaintance may reveal what a phenomenal quality is like; the assignment of that quality to a categorical-grounding, microphysical, cosmic, or fundamental role requires further argument. Once the relevant phenomenal posits lie beyond what experience discloses, the emergence problem remains open rather than dissolved in advance. The gap reappears between those theoretical posits and the consciousness we actually undergo. Goff's recent treatment of subjects as strongly emergent makes this visible from within his own framework.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. Panpsychism remains a live metaphysical option. The problem is that one important motivation moves too quickly from acquaintance with phenomenal character to a claim about phenomenal character's metaphysical role.
2. The paper does not deny panpsychism; it denies that acquaintance with experience already fixes the role of experience in the constitution of matter.
3. First-person acquaintance may tell us what pain or color experience is like. It does not tell us that the property so known is categorical, fundamental, microphysical, or cosmic.
4. The central slide is from a datum to a role assignment. Experience gives the datum; metaphysics supplies the role.
5. If panpsychist posits go beyond what experience discloses, the emergence problem has not disappeared. It has been redescribed in phenomenal terms.
6. The issue is not whether panpsychism is possible, but whether first-person experience can carry the move from experienced consciousness to hidden phenomenal bases.
7. Acquaintance reaches the character of experience. It does not reach the claim that this character is the intrinsic nature of matter.
8. The strongest first-person motivation for panpsychism weakens at the point where phenomenal character is assigned a categorical, cosmic, or microphysical job.
9. Goff's recent treatment of subjects as strongly emergent shows how quickly the original first-person datum gives way to theoretical machinery.
10. Panpsychism can still be defended, but the defense must separate what experience gives from what the theory assigns.

Attractor:

- Repeated "panpsychism may still be true, but..." posture.
- Repeated "not X, but Y" and "does not tell us..." syntax.
- Tidy contrast between "datum" and "role" can become motto-like if repeated.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the quality/role distinction early.
- Avoid ending with a slogan.
- Prefer one direct thesis sentence over several miniature restatements.

## Target 2: Constraint and Schematic Argument

Original:

> The constraint leaves room for theoretical posits, since physics, biology, and psychology all legitimately posit entities and structures outside experience. Those posits are warranted by explanatory, predictive, or unificatory work rather than by inherited first-person authority. If panpsychism posits micro-experience, proto-experience, cosmic subjectivity, hidden phenomenal fields, or relations of phenomenal inheritance, those posits may be defended as theoretical entities. Their support must be earned by the work they do, not drawn from the mere fact that consciousness is first-personally given.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. The constraint does not ban theoretical posits; it changes the kind of support they require.
2. Physics, biology, and psychology all posit what nobody directly experiences. Their warrant comes from explanatory and predictive work.
3. Micro-experience, proto-experience, cosmic subjectivity, and phenomenal inheritance can be introduced in the same way, as theoretical commitments.
4. Such posits become vulnerable only when they are presented as if they inherited the authority of the first-person datum.
5. Panpsychist posits are allowed; they just have to justify themselves as theory.
6. First-person evidence gives consciousness. It does not automatically underwrite every consciousness-like posit introduced downstream.
7. The constraint is permissive about metaphysics and strict about warrant.
8. Nothing prevents the panpsychist from positing hidden phenomenal fields. The question is how those posits are supported.
9. If the support is explanatory, then the warrant is explanatory.
10. The mere fact that experience is given cannot do the work that the hidden posit itself must do.

Attractor:

- "Does not ban X; it changes Y" correction rhythm.
- "Allowed, but..." defensive phrasing.
- Repetition of "work they do" and "earned" language risks sounding juridical or slogan-like.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the permissive point, because it prevents misunderstanding.
- Reduce juridical diction where possible.
- Make the transition from direct warrant to theoretical warrant more concrete.

## Target 3: Applications to Authors

Original:

> The constraint applies unevenly across panpsychist views. It bears most directly on views that treat first-person experience as support for undisclosed phenomenal structure. It also bears on views that argue abductively in their official statements while still drawing dialectical force from first-person privilege. Views that rest entirely on theoretical virtues, without claiming first-person warrant for their posits, are largely untouched. Saad's argument from psychophysical harmony is a useful example of the third kind.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. Different panpsychist arguments are exposed to the constraint in different ways.
2. The pressure is strongest where first-person experience is asked to support undisclosed phenomenal structure.
3. It also matters where an officially abductive argument still borrows force from the privileged status of consciousness.
4. Purely theoretical forms of panpsychism are not the target.
5. Saad's psychophysical-harmony argument belongs mostly to that theoretical category.
6. The constraint cuts across the literature by source of warrant, not by school label.
7. A view is vulnerable here only when its hidden posits lean on first-person privilege.
8. Panpsychism defended by theoretical virtues alone can be assessed on those virtues.
9. The division is not between good and bad panpsychism, but between different uses of first-person evidence.
10. What matters for this paper is how the view gets its warrant.

Attractor:

- Taxonomic signposting.
- "Not the target" disclaimers.
- "What matters..." and "the division is..." style of academic sorting.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the source-of-warrant distinction.
- Avoid sounding like a survey map.
- Retain Saad as protection against overgeneralization.

## Target 4: Pure Awareness Reply

Original:

> These cases can be granted for the sake of argument. The argument need not deny that there are minimal, nondual, selfless, or apparently contentless states, nor decide whether nirodha samapatti is best described as consciousness without content, absence of consciousness, or a remembered transition around an absence. The evidential issue arises when such states are used in public philosophical argument. They reach the theorist through report, memory, interpretation, and later classification, and a later report does not settle what structure the episode had. A sympathetic reader may object that the report is only a later trace, while the evidence itself is the private episode. That distinction matters. Private acquaintance, if it occurs, may have first-person force for the subject before any report is made. Once the episode is offered as a public reason for panpsychism, however, it enters the argument through memory, description, interpretation, and classification. Even apart from that public step, the appeal divides the same way: if the episode was given as silence, openness, presence, bare temporality, or some other way of seeming, it had phenomenal character; if nothing was given, there is no first-person disclosure of experience for panpsychism to extend.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. The paper can grant the reports without deciding the phenomenology of every case.
2. Minimal, nondual, selfless, and contentless states may occur; the warrant question begins when they are used as reasons for panpsychism.
3. A report reaches public argument through memory, description, and classification.
4. A later report cannot settle by itself whether the episode lacked all structure.
5. A sympathetic reader may insist that the report is not the evidence. The private episode is.
6. That reply is important. Private acquaintance may have force for the person who undergoes it.
7. Once the episode enters public philosophy, however, the route runs through report and interpretation.
8. The dilemma also applies before publication: either something was given, or nothing was given.
9. If silence, openness, presence, or bare temporality was given, the state had phenomenal character.
10. If nothing was given, panpsychism has no first-person disclosure to extend.

Attractor:

- The paragraph naturally falls into a very AI-looking "grant, qualify, object, reply, dilemma" sequence.
- Repetition of "report, memory, interpretation, classification" is defensible but heavy.
- "That distinction matters" is an announcing sentence.

Conservative direction:

- Keep the sympathetic-reader objection; it is load-bearing.
- Cut one layer of announcement.
- Let the private/public distinction do the work without repeating the procedural chain too often.

## Target 5: Burden Shift and Conclusion

Original:

> A panpsychist may reply that this burden is shared. Physicalism also owes an account of why organization gives rise to experience, unless it denies or deflates the explanandum, and panpsychism is not alone in facing structural burdens. Independent abductive arguments may also be possible. Panpsychist arguments that make no claim on first-person warrant, of which Saad's harmony argument is the clearest case, are outside the target. The introspective warrant constraint is a discipline on how first-person evidence is used; it has nothing to say about views that do not use it. First-person evidence cannot by itself pay the cost of undisclosed phenomenal structure. Where panpsychism is defended by harmony, phenomenal powers, cosmopsychic individuation, or composite subjectivity, its warrant is theoretical and should be evaluated as such.

Nearby rephrasings:

1. The burden is shared. Physicalism also owes an account of why organization gives rise to experience.
2. Panpsychism is not being singled out as uniquely burdened.
3. The point is limited to panpsychist arguments that trade on first-person warrant.
4. Saad's harmony argument remains outside that target because it is not built on first-person inheritance.
5. The constraint governs the use of first-person evidence, not every route to panpsychism.
6. If a view is defended by harmony, powers, cosmopsychic individuation, or composite subjectivity, it should be assessed as theory.
7. First-person evidence cannot cover the cost of structures it never discloses.
8. The hidden posit may still be worthwhile; its case has to be made elsewhere.
9. Physicalism and panpsychism both face explanatory debts, but the debts are different.
10. The paper removes one source of borrowed support, not the whole panpsychist research program.

Attractor:

- "The paper is not saying..." defensive over-explanation.
- "The point is limited..." and "not every route..." pattern.
- "pay the cost" and "borrowed support" are vivid but may sound too neat if repeated near "earned, not inherited."

Conservative direction:

- Keep the shared-burden reply.
- Reduce slogan-like cost/burden language if repeated.
- Let the final sentence close plainly, not with a maxim.

## Reviewer-Style Vulnerability Notes

1. Target precision is much better than earlier drafts, especially with Strawson, Goff, Roelofs, Mørch, Ramm, and Saad separated by type of warrant. Remaining risk: a referee may still want two or three direct quotations where the warrant-transfer happens. The paper has some quotes from Strawson, Roelofs, and Goff, but Goff's positive simplicity argument is mostly summarized. This is probably acceptable if the claim remains about a pattern of warrant rather than a charge of explicit fallacy.
2. The quality/role distinction is clear and appears early enough. Risk: repetition can make it sound like a slogan. Best fix is not to repeat the phrase too often.
3. The pure-awareness section is defensible and charitable. The largest misunderstanding risk is that the paper might look like it dismisses meditation reports as mere reports. The paragraph already protects against this by granting private acquaintance, but the prose can be tightened so the private/public distinction feels less procedural.
4. The burden-shift section has the right distinction between phenomenal existence and phenomenal structure. Risk: repeated "gap" language and "burden" language can sound like the paper is announcing its dialectical position rather than making it.
5. The conclusion's final line, "earned, not inherited," is neat. It is also high-convergence and risks sounding like a polished AI closer. A plainer ending may be better for publication.
